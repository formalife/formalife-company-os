# 1. ACT 0 — opening + Mirror Test framing

## Opening meaning boundary

Retain the current human opening and `RICONOSCERE. DECIDERE. AGIRE.` objective.

Add one concise operating clarification:

`Anti-Panico` is not a promise that participants will not feel fear or will remain calm. It means the course is designed to give them an orientation/response structure so they do not have to invent everything from zero.

Do not turn this into a disclaimer lecture.

## ACT 6 debrief wording

Replace any debrief framing that presumes improvement with the question:

**`Che cosa è cambiato rispetto all'inizio?`**

Operating order:

1. volunteer describes what felt/appeared different;
2. facilitator names only observable changes;
3. if an error remains, correct/rerun the relevant passage;
4. no competence/clinical-outcome conclusion.

The Mirror Test remains immediate simulation/transfer evidence only.

# 4. ACT 3 — choking

## Recognition hierarchy

Retain partial vs severe/total obstruction as the underlying conceptual model.

Live teaching hierarchy becomes:

**observable effective cough/airflow vs ineffective/absent cough/airflow -> conceptual obstruction label -> decision -> action.**

Reason:

The parent acts on observable state, not on an abstract anatomical diagnosis.

## S25 edge cases

The two existing founder-selected edge cases remain in the first production rehearsal.

Operating cap:

- approximately 2 minutes total;
- no additional edge cases;
- exact adaptation only after Mafalda sign-off.

Status:

`OPTIONAL_REHEARSAL_CUT`.

## Practical-lab progression control

Add a private instructor progression check before moving a learner from corrected practice into scenario/no-prompt mode.

Subject to clinical validation, check:

- state recognition;
- position/support;
- critical mechanics;
- reassessment/state change;
- escalation/next state;
- absence of material unsafe/prohibited action.

If a critical problem remains, repeat/correct rather than advancing for schedule cosmetics.

This is not a participant score or certification.

# 5. ACT 4 — pediatric CPR / QCPR

## Adult evidence removal

The adult survival/time graph no longer belongs to the main teaching flow.

Keep it in appendix/reference.

The live transition becomes:

**choking/respiratory problem -> hypoxia/unresponsiveness/arrest -> time matters -> pediatric mechanism/ventilation -> simple pediatric sequence.**

Do not spend reclaimed time replacing the adult graph with another evidence lecture.

## Pediatric translation

Teach directly that ventilations matter in pediatric resuscitation at the approved consumer level, with AED retained where indicated/available.

No verbal dependency on an adult curve.

## QCPR boundary retrieval

After the existing QCPR debrief ask:

**`Questo grafico ci ha aiutato. Che cosa NON ci sta misurando?`**

Recover the broader response jobs before moving to full-sequence retrieval:

- ventilation;
- recognition/algorithm entry;
- 112;
- AED;
- complete sequence.

Target:

~20–30 seconds.

The point is to stop measurable compression performance from replacing the complete pediatric-resuscitation mental model.

# 6. ACT 5 — other emergencies

Retain eight topics and the common grammar, but state explicitly at the start:

**this is first-action orientation, not eight mini-courses.**

## Four micro-retrievals maximum

Add only these short retrieval jobs:

1. burn — first useful action;
2. anaphylaxis — what must not be awaited;
3. drowning — what changes if not breathing normally after removal;

- approximately 5–10 seconds each;
- question -> commitment -> reveal/correct;
- no discussion block;
- no new slide;
- no retrieval prompt on all eight topics.

Exact answer wording remains subject to clinical sign-off.

## Buffer protection

The 1-minute unallocated ACT 5 buffer remains protected.

Micro-retrievals should be absorbed primarily by time recovered elsewhere in the final audit, not by deleting useful safety content.
