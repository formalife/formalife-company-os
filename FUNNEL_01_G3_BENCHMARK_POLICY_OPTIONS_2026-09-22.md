## 2. Live duration — benchmark and recommendation

### Current external benchmark checked 2026-09-22

Representative parent/caregiver offers:

- **Tiny Hearts — Public Baby & Child First Aid**: 4 hours live; broad emergency scope, hands-on CPR/choking, handbook and post-course resources.
- **British Red Cross — First aid for baby and child**: 4-hour public/family course.
- **CPR Kids — Baby + Child CPR & First Aid**: approximately 3h15; broad parent-first curriculum with some lower-priority topics delivered online.
- **Safe Beginnings — Infant/Child CPR, Choking and Injury Prevention**: approximately 2h–2h15 for essentials; broader certification-level infant/toddler first aid is 5 hours.
- **Mini First Aid — Baby & Child First Aid**: generally 2 hours.
- **Daisy First Aid — Baby & Child First Aid**: 2 hours.
- **Camillo Rescue Baby 2.0**: 3 hours in person; narrower than Formalife target Full and heavily centered on choking/resuscitation/selected first aid.
- **Italian CRI examples**: commonly 3 hours for pediatric lifesaving/choking/CPR; some 4-hour formats exist.

### Interpretation

The market pattern is coherent:

- ~2 hours works for essentials / narrower parent classes;
- ~3 hours works for choking + CPR + selected first aid;
- ~4 hours is a validated consumer duration for a broader non-certification parent-first flagship;
- ~5 hours starts to resemble broader/certification-style training and increases fatigue/commitment.

Formalife's planned Full is broader than the common 2–3 hour choking/CPR class but should not become a professional-certification syllabus.

### RECOMMENDATION

**Keep the commercial promise at approximately 4 hours of live education/practice.**

Operationally reserve a **4h10–4h20 calendar slot** so the experience can include a short break/transition without stealing practical time.

Working design target:

- approximately 3h50–4h00 meaningful teaching/practice;
- approximately 10–20 minutes total break/transition;
- total on-site calendar block approximately 4h10–4h20.

Do not expand to 5 hours merely to fit more topics. Move lower-value theory to pre/post-course reference layers instead.

The exact instructional allocation remains subject to G0 scientific/instructional validation.

## 3. Customer protection, cancellation and pre-enrolment architecture

### Competitive reference

Current competitors often protect the provider more than the customer:

- Tiny Hearts public courses generally do not offer refunds but work with customers to reschedule;
- Safe Beginnings allows refund with >48h notice and transfer with >24h notice, then forfeiture close to the event;
- Mini First Aid generally does not refund paid classes and may consider transfers;
- CRI Padova states no refund/date change for purchased in-person courses;
- CPR Kids explicitly supports illness rescheduling without penalty;
- Camillo differentiates more strongly through one free retraining opportunity within 12 months.

This leaves room for Formalife to create a visibly more customer-protective policy while still controlling no-show/capacity economics.

### RECOMMENDED POLICY FAMILY — `Formalife Total Protection`

#### P1 — Confirmed-course cancellation / transfer

Recommended operating terms to test:

- **more than 7 days before**: choice of full refund or free transfer;
- **7 days to 48 hours before**: one free transfer to another edition within 12 months; alternatively 100% Formalife credit if a suitable date is not yet available;
- **48 hours to course start**: one free transfer for genuine family/child illness or material emergency if communicated before course start; no medical documentation required in V1 unless abuse becomes material;
- **no-show without notice**: no cash refund; one recovery option at 50% of the then-current public price, subject to availability, can be tested instead of total forfeiture;
- **Formalife cancellation of a confirmed course**: automatic full refund OR customer-selected transfer; add a goodwill benefit because the failure is on Formalife's side.

Exact legal wording requires review before publication.

#### P2 — If Formalife cancels a confirmed edition

Recommended stronger customer treatment:

**automatic 100% refund + optional priority transfer + EUR 20 future Formalife credit**.

The credit compensates inconvenience without creating a cash payout beyond the refund. It is an HYPOTHESIS until economics are checked.

### Pre-enrolment / activation edition

Some editions may be opened only if a minimum viable class is reached.

Use a visibly different state from a confirmed course:

**`EDIZIONE IN ATTIVAZIONE — PRE-ISCRIZIONE PROTETTA`**

Customer communication must state before payment/commitment:

- the edition is not yet confirmed;
- the minimum organizational threshold for that edition;
- the exact confirmation deadline;
- what happens if the threshold is reached;
- what happens if it is not reached;
- whether money is charged now, later or as a refundable deposit;
- how quickly any refund is processed.

Do not hide the activation condition in terms and conditions.

### Pre-enrolment models

#### Model A — no-charge reservation

Customer reserves interest; no money is charged. When minimum is reached, Formalife opens a short purchase window.

Pros:

- maximum customer protection;
- no refund burden.

Cons:

- weak demand signal;
- higher drop-off when payment is later requested;
- apparent pre-registrations may overstate real demand.

Best use: early exploratory geography tests.

#### Model B — small refundable deposit

Customer pays a small deposit; if activated, deposit becomes course credit; if not activated, automatic full refund.

Pros:

- stronger intent signal;
- still highly protective.

Cons:

- two-step payment/admin.

#### Model C — **Guide-backed Better-Than-Risk-Free pre-enrolment** — recommended premium test after Guide economics are known

Customer pays the Guide price (currently EUR 19.90) as the pre-enrolment commitment and receives the Guide immediately.

If the edition activates:

- the amount becomes Training Credit toward the Full;
- customer pays the remaining amount.

If the edition does **not** activate by the published deadline:

- Formalife automatically refunds the EUR 19.90;
- customer keeps the Guide.

This creates a genuine better-than-risk-free activation offer: the customer cannot lose the reservation money and keeps a useful paid product if Formalife cannot activate the edition.

Economic warning:

- this converts Guide printing/fulfilment/shipping into an acquisition cost for failed activation editions;
- use only after exact Guide unit economics are known;
- limit to approved activation campaigns rather than making every course uncertain.

### Minimum participant threshold

Do not canonize a headcount until complete costs are available.

With current partial economics, **8 paid participants** is a reasonable provisional planning floor for low/zero-CAC editions, but not yet a final business rule. At 8 participants current normalized direct contribution ranges materially with Single/Couple mix and still excludes payment fees, travel/setup/admin, pack, retraining, CAC and overhead.

Final `N_min` should be set from:

**expected order mix -> revenue -> complete variable/fixed delivery cost -> required contribution buffer.**

## 4. Training Credit — stronger incentive options

Current asset: Guide price EUR 19.90 can generate credit toward Full under defined conditions.

The credit should preserve the Guide's standalone value while making the move to supervised/broader preparedness feel economically fair.

### Option TC-A — 100% Evergreen Credit

- every EUR 19.90 Guide purchase = EUR 19.90 credit toward Full;
- valid for 12 months;
- simple and transparent;
- lowest economic risk.

### Option TC-B — **150% Momentum Credit — recommended test**

- within the first 90 days after Guide purchase, EUR 19.90 becomes **EUR 29.90 Full credit**;
- days 91–365 revert to the ordinary EUR 19.90 credit;
- after 12 months the credit expires or becomes a separate founder decision.

Economic effect at current price:

- Single buyer pays EUR 19.90 Guide + EUR 50.10 balance = EUR 70 total customer cash outlay versus EUR 80 direct Full;
- Couple buyer with one Guide pays EUR 19.90 + EUR 90.10 = EUR 110 total versus EUR 120 direct Couple.

Thus the incremental incentive beyond 100% credit is only EUR 10, while giving the prospect a concrete reason to progress within a legitimate time window.

This should be tested, not assumed permanent.

### Option TC-C — Household Credit

When two separately identified caregivers each bought a Guide, allow both credits to apply to the 2-caregiver formula, subject to a cap.

Recommended initial cap:

- standard credit: up to EUR 39.80 total;
- Momentum variant: do not automatically double 150% credit until economics are proven; test a lower household cap such as EUR 40–50.

### Duplicate Guide problem

If a Guide buyer later buys Full and Full normally includes another Guide, do not make the customer feel they are buying the same thing twice.

Recommended choices:

1. keep the second Guide and give it to another caregiver;
2. redirect the additional copy to the second participant in a 2-caregiver order;
3. later test a small alternative pack component only if operationally simple.

Do not create cash refunds for the included Guide unless the complete economics justify it.

### Why this can be stronger than competitor discounting

Many players use group discounts or periodic promotions. Formalife can instead make prior educational spend **retain or increase its value** when the customer progresses to practice. That is more coherent with a preparedness system than a generic coupon.

## 5. Caregiver package / Single-Couple redesign

### RECOMMENDATION — customer-facing framing

Replace romantic-relationship language with functional care-network language:

- **1 Caregiver — EUR 80**
- **2 Caregivers — EUR 120**

Supporting explanation:

**Choose 2 Caregivers when two adults regularly look after the child: partner, grandparent, babysitter or another trusted caregiver.**

This makes the offer more inclusive and more directly connected to the job.

### Upgrade mechanic

A 1-Caregiver buyer should be able to add a second caregiver for **+EUR 40** until a defined cutoff before the course, subject to available seats.

Do not show fake scarcity; if fewer than two seats remain, the option legitimately becomes unavailable.

### Future flexibility test — not V1

Potential `2 Caregivers Flex`:

- two participants may attend different editions within a defined window;
- priced above same-date 2 Caregivers because it consumes capacity on two dates.

This could solve a real scheduling barrier, but it should not be introduced until fill-rate economics are known.

### Do not add 3/4-person discounts in V1

Camillo currently uses aggressive group discounts. Formalife should not copy them before capacity and contribution per seat are proven.

The stronger position is not “cheaper for groups”; it is **the right caregiver network can be prepared through a deliberately designed family offer**.

## 6. Reference pack — recommended low-cost / high-perceived-value architecture

Working name only:

**Formalife Family Emergency Pack**

Goal:

make the broader preparedness system physically visible and useful after the course without creating another expensive book.

### Included V1 components

1. **Guida Anti-Panico** — existing 176-page choking-specific book; already part of the current Full architecture.
2. **Emergency Action Cards** — small set of laminated/versioned quick-reference cards for approved emergency frameworks and selected high-value reminders. Clinical wording requires G0.
3. **Fridge Emergency Card / Magnet** — 112 + blank family emergency contacts + QR to the current Formalife reference hub. Avoid maintaining unverified local clinical numbers on a static card.
4. **Caregiver Handover Card** — blank template the family can complete with contacts, routines and approved emergency-plan fields for grandparents/babysitters. The family, not Formalife, enters any personal/health information.
5. **Family Safety Checklist** — concise prevention/readiness checklist linked to the Full scope.
6. **Practice-at-Home Scenario Cards** — a few simple, scientifically reviewed scenario prompts designed to rehearse the decision framework with another caregiver; not a substitute for manikin practice.
7. **QR / access card to an updated digital reference hub** — versioned resources, approved updates, course recap and future refresh booking.
8. **12-Month Practice Refresh Pass** — physical/digital proof of the included free retraining entitlement.
9. **Compact branded folder/envelope** containing the materials as one tangible preparedness kit.

### Cost discipline

Target incremental physical cost beyond the existing Guide:

**approximately EUR 4–7 per participant** as a design objective, not a confirmed cost.

Most perceived value should come from:

- utility;
- organization;
- versioned digital access;
- visual clarity;
- the refresh entitlement;

not expensive merchandise.

Do not assign fictional retail values to each insert.

### Better-than-Tiny-Hearts direction

Tiny Hearts currently includes an 81-page handbook plus 15 resources. Formalife should not win by counting one more PDF. The stronger design is a **smaller physical action system + updateable digital reference + free live skill refresh**, so the materials are connected to behavior rather than quantity.

## 7. Post-course support — FOUNDER DECISION

Founder decision 2026-09-22:

### 7.1 One free practical retraining within 12 months

Every Full participant receives **one free practical re-training opportunity within 12 months of the original course**.

The operating design should favor group refresh sessions rather than individual appointments.

Recommended V1 structure:

- 45–75 minute practice-focused session;
- core motor skills / decision rehearsal, especially choking and resuscitation-related practical elements approved by G0;
- no attempt to re-teach the full four-hour curriculum;
- scheduled refresh dates or designated refresh capacity inside selected sessions;
- one redemption per participant;
- transfer/no-show rules explicit;
- usage and cost tracked.

This decision is deliberately stronger than a simple post-course PDF and aligns with a current Italian competitor benchmark: Camillo currently offers one free repeat opportunity within 12 months. Formalife must differentiate through how the refresh is integrated, measured and combined with the broader customer system rather than claiming exclusivity.

### 7.2 Dedicated customer communication stream

Founder decision:

Full-course customers enter a dedicated Formalife customer relationship stream designed to:

- continue delivering useful pediatric-safety information;
- share relevant news, articles, updates, seasonal issues and adjacent expert content;
- keep Formalife mentally available to the family;
- stimulate appropriate referral;
- surface real backend/lifecycle needs;
- present relevant future products without generic catalog blasting.

### Governance boundary

Operationally distinguish:

1. **transactional/service communication** required to deliver the purchased experience and refresh entitlement;
2. **marketing/editorial customer communication** that includes ongoing articles, news and future offers.

Consent/legal basis, unsubscribe mechanics and CRM suppression must be verified before implementation. Do not assume that purchase authorizes every future promotional communication.

### Content architecture recommendation

Customer stream should include families such as:

- practical refresh/reminder content;
- child-safety alerts and product recalls where verified;
- seasonal risk/prevention content;
- evidence-based explanations of relevant news;
- new life-stage safety issues;
- specialist partner education;
- stories/customer proof when permission exists;
- direct invitations to the free refresh;
- relevant backend offers only when the need/trigger fits.

Cadence remains a test variable.

## 8. Better-Than-Risk-Free guarantee — Jay Abraham-inspired options

Jay Abraham's canonical risk-reversal principle is to identify the buyer's perceived risk, transfer it to the seller, and in a Better-Than-Risk-Free (BTRF) version compensate the buyer for the time/faith invested, often by letting them keep valuable materials even if refunded.

Relevant Abraham sources:

- https://jay.abraham.com/frameworks
- https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/jayabrahamassets/Books/abraham101.pdf
- https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/jayabrahamassets/Books/GettingEverythingNew.pdf

### Guarantee G-A — First-Break Guarantee

Attend the opening portion. If by the first defined break the participant concludes the course is not what was transparently promised, they can leave, receive a full refund and keep the Guide/materials already provided.

Pros: low abuse, operationally safe.

Cons: weaker because the participant cannot judge the complete experience.

### Guarantee G-B — **Full-Experience Better-Than-Risk-Free — RECOMMENDED TEST**

Working concept:

**Attend the complete Full Course. If within 24 hours you believe the experience did not justify what you paid, tell Formalife and receive a 100% refund. Keep the Guida Anti-Panico and Family Emergency Pack for the time and trust you invested.**

Important design points:

- feedback/reason may be requested for learning but should not become an obstacle to a legitimate refund;
- refund applies to the net Full amount actually paid after any Training Credit;
- no-show/cancellation is a different policy and does not trigger the satisfaction guarantee;
- materials do not need to be returned;
- historical refund/abuse data must be measured;
- customer-facing wording must not imply guaranteed emergency performance or medical outcomes.

This is genuinely better-than-risk-free because the customer can receive all money paid for the Full back while retaining tangible useful materials.

### Guarantee G-C — Full-Experience + refresh assurance

Combine G-B with the already founder-decided free 12-month practical refresh.

The promise becomes:

**financial risk removed at the first experience + one later opportunity to refresh practical skills at no additional fee.**

This is stronger and more relevant than simply adding a longer money-back period.

### Why not pay cash compensation for the customer's time in V1

Jay Abraham's strongest examples sometimes pay an additional amount beyond the refund. Formalife can create BTRF value through materials retained and free retraining without introducing a cash bounty that could attract adverse selection disproportionate to an EUR 80/120 purchase.

If conversion data later justifies a stronger test, an additional fixed goodwill credit can be tested.

## 10. Exact cost inputs needed to finalize offer economics

Current known partial costs are insufficient once the stronger offer is implemented.

Please provide or measure the following.

### A. Tax / price basis

- Are EUR 80 / EUR 120 customer prices VAT-inclusive? If yes, what VAT treatment applies to the course?
- VAT/tax treatment of Guide and any bundled physical materials.

### B. Existing Guide economics

Per copy:

- print/manufacturing cost;
- packaging;
- fulfilment handling;
- shipping cost for standalone Guide orders;
- any payment/platform fees;
- replacement/damage average if material.

### C. Full-course payment cost

- Stripe/payment processor percentage + fixed fee;
- chargeback cost/rate if known;
- refund fee treatment.

### D. Venue / edition costs

Per edition:

- room cost including VAT/fees;
- travel/mileage/parking/tolls;
- setup/cleanup/logistics cost;
- equipment transport or rental;
- consumables;
- refreshments if any;
- event insurance/incremental cover if any.

### E. Instructor cost

Confirm whether normalized EUR 120 + EUR 80 represents:

- gross company cost or cash compensation only;
- whether travel/preparation/setup/admin is included;
- whether scientific review/preparation time is included.

### F. Family Emergency Pack

Per participant:

- print/lamination/folder/magnet/card costs;
- digital platform/access cost if any;
- content update/review cost allocation.

### G. Free 12-month retraining

Need a planned refresh operating model:

- session duration;
- expected group size/max seats;
- instructor cost;
- room cost;
- materials/consumables;
- admin/booking cost;
- expected redemption rate;
- expected no-show rate.

Until observed, redemption rate is a test variable; model multiple scenarios rather than invent one.

### H. Administrative/customer-service burden

Estimate time and loaded cost for:

- registration/customer questions;
- payment support;
- reminder/rescheduling;
- post-course follow-up;
- refund/guarantee handling;
- refresh booking.

### I. Customer newsletter / content system

Monthly or per-edition incremental costs:

- Brevo/email platform cost attributable to scale;
- editorial production time;
- expert/scientific review time;
- design/video cost;
- automation/CRM maintenance.

### J. Acquisition cost by route

For each source once tests start:

- paid media;
- creative/agency/freelance production;
- landing/software allocation where material;
- partner commission/revenue share;
- human sales/follow-up time;
- front-end subsidy/failed pre-enrolment Guide cost.

### K. Refund / transfer / guarantee exposure

Track:

- cancellation rate;
- transfer rate;
- no-show rate;
- BTRF refund claim rate;
- cost of retained materials on refunded orders;
- Formalife-cancelled edition rate.

### Economics output once these numbers exist

Formalife should calculate at minimum:

**revenue net of tax/payment -> variable materials -> edition fixed delivery -> expected refresh liability -> expected refund/guarantee liability -> acquisition cost -> contribution -> CAC payback.**

Read both:

- **contribution per order**;
- **contribution per occupied live seat**.
