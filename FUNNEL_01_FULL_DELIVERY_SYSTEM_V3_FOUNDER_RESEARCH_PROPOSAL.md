# 2. Current external evidence that governs V3

## 2.1 ERC / IRC 2025 — paediatric FBAO

Current ERC 2025 Paediatric Life Support / IRC translation direction:

- if cough is effective -> encourage cough and monitor for deterioration;
- if cough is ineffective -> activate/call EMS as soon as possible;
- conscious infant -> alternating up to 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts;
- conscious child/adolescent -> alternating up to 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts;
- no blind finger sweeps;
- if unconscious -> begin paediatric BLS immediately, with ERC trained-PBLS sequence including initial rescue breaths;
- if obstruction is relieved -> urgent medical follow-up is indicated by the current ERC algorithm;
- call/have someone call EMS as soon as practical, preferably second rescuer or speakerphone, and at the latest when consciousness is lost.

Primary references checked 2026-09-22:

- ERC 2025 Paediatric Life Support: https://www.erc.edu/media/03xnpjmj/gl2025-09-pls-e.pdf
- ERC paediatric FBAO poster: https://www.erc.edu/assets/posters/3.PLSGL-Algorithms-1.pdf
- IRC 2025 guideline hub: https://www.ircouncil.it/linee-guida-rcp-2025/

## 2.2 ERC / AHA 2025 — paediatric CPR

Relevant design constraints:

- paediatric arrests are commonly respiratory/asphyxial and ventilations matter;
- ERC 2025 specifically trained PBLS rescuers use 5 initial rescue breaths and 15:2; rescuers not trained to that level use 30:2;
- AHA/AAP 2025 also encourages breaths plus compressions for paediatric OHCA;
- exact Formalife customer algorithm/ratio must be selected deliberately with Mafalda against the intended level of training and the Italian/ERC context;
- do not mix an AHA sequence and an ERC sequence casually within one consumer course;
- if a second rescuer is present, one starts CPR while the other calls EMS and retrieves/applies AED;
- a single rescuer with a mobile phone should prioritise calling EMS using speaker mode and starting CPR; ERC states that a single rescuer without a mobile phone should perform CPR for approximately 1 minute before leaving to seek help;
- AED use is relevant, but shockable rhythms are less common in younger paediatric arrests than in adults; the course should not imply that AED is the dominant intervention in typical asphyxial paediatric arrest.

References:

- ERC 2025 Paediatric Life Support above;
- AHA/AAP 2025 Pediatric BLS: https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-basic-life-support

## 2.3 Education science — current high-value principles

AHA 2025 Resuscitation Education Science supports for lay rescuers:

- CPR feedback devices during training;
- stepwise skill teaching;
- spaced learning / booster sessions;
- blended learning where useful;
- gamified elements as optional engagement tools;
- scripted debriefing as a consistency aid;
- active rather than exclusively passive instruction.

AHA 2025 strongly recommends CPR feedback devices for lay-rescuer skill training.

Reference:
https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/resuscitation-education-science

## 2.4 Attention boundary

Do not teach or market a fixed biological "10–15 minute attention span".

Use this design heuristic instead:

- no long passive monologues;
- change learner task approximately every 8–15 minutes;
- core learning loops should end in an observable action/retrieval;
- movement/practice naturally resets attention;
- use one real break + one short active reset;
- keep secondary-topic micro-lessons highly patterned.

# 4. ACT 0 — Open + Cold Start / Mirror Test — 10 min

## 4.1 Short human opening — ~90 sec

Job:

- who Formalife is;
- why the room exists;
- what participants should be able to do differently by the end.

Do not spend prime attention on long CVs, company history, administrative rules or agenda slides.

## 4.2 Rapid room calibration — ~2.5 min

Use fast hand-raise / visual polling, not 12 individual biographies.

Examples:

- child age band;
- currently starting solids / already eating broadly;
- first course vs prior first-aid/choking training;
- primary reason for attending.

Deeper context can come from the pre-course question already proposed in V2.

## 4.3 Cold Start experiential baseline — ~6 min

### Strong mechanic to retain

Use one consenting volunteer plus the whole room.

Preferred setup:

- volunteer is the caregiver/rescuer;
- use manikin / choking trainer / neutral simulation object, not the instructor physically pretending to choke in a way that invites unsafe maneuvers on a person;
- facilitator narrates observable cues progressively;
- no coaching during the baseline;
- maximum ~30–45 sec actual response window before stopping;
- ask the room what they noticed / what they would have done;
- explicitly state: **"non stiamo valutando te; stiamo fotografando da dove partiamo"**.

### Scenario

Start with ambiguity:

1. child is eating;
2. cough is initially effective;
3. cough becomes ineffective / child cannot vocalise or breathe effectively;
4. stop scenario before prolonged distress.

This makes the key recognition problem visible before teaching it.

### Mirror design

At ACT 6, use the **same volunteer if they consent**, but a **matched transfer scenario**, not a word-for-word replay. Change age/location/context or add a second rescuer/unresponsive progression.

Reason:

- exact repetition proves rehearsal;
- matched variation better demonstrates transfer.

### Proof boundary

A visible before/after improvement is excellent event theatre and internal learning evidence. It is not proof of long-term real-emergency competence.

Do not claim the mechanic is unique in education; pre/post simulation is established. Formalife's possible differentiation is the consistent signature choreography and customer experience around it.

# 5. ACT 1 — The Problem — 6 min

Purpose:

**make the problem proportionate and concrete, not frightening.**

Use a maximum of 3–4 current/traceable data points.

Recommended structure:

1. domestic paediatric injury is common enough to matter;
2. younger children have elevated home-injury risk;
3. choking is a recognised important public-health issue, particularly in young children;
4. prevention and first response both matter.

Candidate evidence pool:

- ISS 0–2 surveillance: 12.4% of mothers in participating regions reported having sought healthcare for a domestic accident involving their child in the 2022 survey;
- Ministry of Health continues to describe foreign-body choking as an important public-health problem and an important cause of death under age 3;
- Ministry 2017 prevention guidance estimated ~1,000 hospitalisations/year for paediatric choking in Italy and notes much larger numbers when lower-severity events are included — if used, label the year/source clearly rather than presenting it as a 2026 statistic.

Rules:

- every statistic shows source + year;
- no montage of dead children / fear imagery;
- no unsupported mortality arithmetic;
- the final sentence transitions to agency: **the point of the data is why preparation is worth learning, not why parents should panic.**

# 6. ACT 2 — The Mental Model — 7 min

Founder preferred framework:

**FERMA -> VALUTA -> AGISCI**

Design intent:

- give the learner one stable orientation model before procedures;
- reduce uncontrolled impulsive action;
- create a recurring verbal cue for every later scenario.

Important clinical/wording boundary:

**FERMA is a cognitive reset of seconds, not permission to delay time-critical action.**

Suggested teaching:

### FERMA

- interrupt the panic/impulse for a brief orientation moment;
- check scene safety / what is actually happening.

### VALUTA

- responsive or not?;
- effective cough/breathing or not?;
- what state am I actually looking at?;
- is this an immediate emergency?

### AGISCI

- perform the appropriate first action;
- activate/assign 112 when indicated;
- continue/reassess.

Do not promise that the framework "eliminates panic" or guarantees calm.

Operational repetition:

Every major later block should explicitly point back to F-V-A once, so it becomes a schema rather than an opening slogan.

# 7. ACT 3 — Choking Mastery — 70 min

This remains the signature depth block and should be one of the most memorable parts of the course.

## 7.1 Orientation / prevention / mechanism — ~10 min

### Prevention — concise

- food preparation risk principles such as shape / consistency / size where scientifically approved;
- behaviour/environmental prevention;
- point to the Guide for depth rather than reproducing the book.

### Mechanism

Use a simple visual/anatomical explanation sufficient to understand:

- why a foreign body can obstruct airflow;
- why young children are vulnerable;
- why effective coughing should not be interrupted;
- why ineffective cough/severe obstruction requires rapid action.

Avoid unnecessary anatomy detail.

## 7.2 Recognition — teach the decision state, not just labels — ~5 min

Primary customer distinction:

**COUGH EFFECTIVE vs COUGH INEFFECTIVE / SEVERE FBAO**

Effective cough:

- encourage cough;
- monitor for deterioration;
- do not perform unnecessary invasive manoeuvres.

Ineffective cough:

- activate EMS as soon as possible;
- begin approved disobstruction sequence according to age;
- monitor consciousness.

The terms "partial" / "complete" may still be explained as concepts, but should not be the main layperson decision test if they create false diagnostic certainty.

## 7.3 112 logic — teach once visually here

Build a simple slide with two columns:

### You are two rescuers

- one continues assessment/manoeuvres;
- second calls 112 immediately and follows dispatcher instructions.

### You are alone with a mobile phone

- use speaker mode to call 112 as soon as practical while continuing response.

### Minimum rule

For ineffective cough / severe FBAO, current ERC 2025: EMS activation **as soon as possible**, at the latest by loss of consciousness.

Do not teach a sequence that waits for all back blows/thrusts to fail before EMS activation if a second rescuer or speakerphone is available.

## 7.4 Child >1 year skill loop — ~22 min

Exact clinical steps subject to Mafalda/IRC review.

Educational sequence:

1. **full-speed demonstration** — no interruption, show what competent flow looks like;
2. **deconstruction** — explain recognition, body position, back blows, abdominal thrusts, reassessment, 112 role, stop conditions;
3. **guided learner practice** — slow initially;
4. **immediate correction** — one critical point at a time;
5. **repeat corrected attempt**;
6. **short random-state scenario** — effective cough / ineffective cough / resolution / deterioration;
7. **final no-prompt attempt**.

## 7.5 Infant <1 year skill loop — ~22 min

Same learning architecture, with infant-specific approved technique:

- head/body support;
- back blows;
- chest thrusts;
- no abdominal thrusts;
- no shaking;
- transitions / reassessment;
- 112 role.

Do not build an extended "shaken baby" lecture inside this skill block; the key practical safety message is enough unless scientific review identifies a stronger learning need.

## 7.6 Practice-station design

With 12 participants and 2 instructors:

- split into two groups of up to 6;
- one child station + one infant station, then rotate;
- target sufficient equipment so no learner spends long periods waiting;
- ideal operating direction: one functional trainer per 2–3 learners in a station where economically possible;
- use roles: active rescuer / observer-coach / reset-support, then rotate;
- observer can use an instructional checklist during training, but the checklist is not a real-emergency dependency.

### Practice rule

**practice until the critical error is corrected, not until everyone has merely "had a turn".**

Adapt rapid-cycle logic:

- critical error -> stop -> brief correction -> reset a few seconds before error -> repeat.

AHA's formal RCDP recommendation is stronger for healthcare personnel; for laypeople this is an instructional adaptation/test, not a guideline mandate.

## 7.7 Edge cases / post-relief / bridge to CPR — ~11 min

### After relief

ERC 2025 paediatric FBAO algorithm states urgent medical follow-up after obstruction is relieved.

Do not hard-code "doctor for child / emergency department for infant" until Mafalda defines a justified Italian customer rule.

### If consciousness is lost

Transition directly into the next major act:

**airway obstruction -> hypoxia -> unresponsiveness -> paediatric BLS.**

### Special cases

Pregnancy/obesity special circumstances should not consume core time unless Mafalda confirms relevance to the intended paediatric audience and exact technique.

Keep rare edge cases in Pack/reference/Q&A if their live value is low.

# 9. ACT 4 — Paediatric Resuscitation / QCPR — 60 min

## 9.1 Bridge / mechanism — ~6 min

Explain the causal logic, not an unsupported probability:

**severe unresolved airway obstruction -> hypoxia -> loss of responsiveness -> cardiac arrest can follow rapidly.**

Current evidence does **not** provide a defensible general percentage of all paediatric severe choking episodes that progress to arrest.

Do not invent one.

## 9.2 Survival / urgency visual — ~5 min

Do not automatically use an adult sudden-cardiac-arrest survival curve.

Before any graph is used, audit:

- population;
- arrest aetiology;
- age;
- intervention;
- source/year.

For paediatric education, the safer message from 2025 guidance is:

- early high-quality CPR matters;
- ventilations are particularly important in paediatric/asphyxial arrest;
- AED matters when a shockable rhythm is present, but shockable rhythms are a minority in younger children.

If the existing book graph is adult/general, replace or relabel it rather than presenting it as paediatric evidence.

## 9.3 One simple customer algorithm — ~8 min

Do not teach jargon for its own sake.

Possible teaching principle:

**recognise unresponsiveness / abnormal breathing -> activate 112 -> begin the selected paediatric CPR sequence -> use AED as soon as available/appropriate -> continue until help/signs of life.**

Exact Formalife sequence/ratio is a SCIENTIFIC DECISION still required:

- ERC specifically trained PBLS: five initial rescue breaths + 15:2;
- other trained/untrained context may use 30:2;
- AHA uses a different sequencing framework.

Because Formalife operates in Italy, the default scientific reference should be ERC/IRC unless Mafalda deliberately documents another rationale.

### Important founder wording clarification

If "no CAB, solo CPR" means:

**do not burden parents with the CAB acronym; teach one integrated sequence** -> supported.

If it means:

**compression-only CPR** -> not supported as the primary paediatric teaching direction because ventilation is important in paediatric arrest.

## 9.4 Demonstration — ~6 min

Show child and infant technique with minimal narration first, then identify key differences.

Do not make learners infer technique only from slides.

## 9.5 QCPR deliberate-practice lab — ~28 min

Formalife already owns QCPR manikins: use this as an instructional asset, not decoration.

Recommended cycle for each learner/manikin type:

1. **short baseline** — ~45–60 sec with feedback hidden or minimal;
2. **feedback cycle** — ~90–120 sec with QCPR visible;
3. identify the single weakest metric / technique error;
4. **targeted repeat** — ~60–90 sec focused on that error;
5. instructor confirms/corrects hand position, body mechanics, ventilations and transitions;
6. **final faded-feedback attempt** — screen hidden or feedback reduced so learner must internalise feel/rhythm;
7. brief score/result explanation.

AHA 2025 recommends CPR feedback devices during lay-rescuer training.

### Gamification rule

Use **beat your previous attempt**, not public humiliation/leaderboard as the default.

A score is coaching data, not a certificate of real-world competence.

## 9.6 AED orientation + 112 roles + micro-scenario — ~7 min

Include enough AED exposure that the course does not show a CPR+AED concept without teaching what the device is for.

At minimum:

- what an AED does / does not do;
- where it enters the sequence;
- second-rescuer role;
- follow device prompts;
- current paediatric pad/attenuation wording subject to Mafalda review.

Then run one short integrated scenario with:

- single rescuer vs two rescuers;
- phone/speaker role;
- CPR start;
- AED arrival.

# 11. ACT 5 — Other Emergencies — 50 min

Founder preference accepted:

**teach topics one by one**, not only as abstract case families.

The control against lecture sprawl is a fixed micro-lesson template.

## 11.1 Standard micro-lesson template

For every topic:

1. **micro-scenario** — what happened?;
2. **what it is** — lay explanation, no diagnostic lecture;
3. **prevention** — only high-value actionable points;
4. **recognition / red flags**;
5. **what to do**;
6. **what not to do**;
7. **when to call 112 / seek urgent care / use other appropriate service**;
8. **one retrieval question** before moving on.

Visual design:

- same slide architecture every time;
- 1–2 slides/topic where possible;
- icons/colors consistent with `FERMA -> VALUTA -> AGISCI`;
- avoid text walls.

## 11.2 Proposed V1 secondary set

Final set must be signed off by Mafalda against actionability and time.

Current recommended candidates:

1. foreign-body ingestion (distinct from airway obstruction);
2. head injury / significant fall;
3. febrile seizure / seizure first response;
4. poisoning / toxic exposure;
5. drowning / water incident;
6. burns/scalds;
7. severe allergic reaction / anaphylaxis or respiratory deterioration;
8. serious bleeding can be integrated into trauma if needed to protect time.

### Why keep anaphylaxis/respiratory deterioration in the set

It is high-consequence, parent-actionable and prominent in strong international parent first-aid curricula. Do not remove it simply because it was absent from the founder's example list.

### Ingestion warning

"Foreign-body ingestion" is clinically heterogeneous (eg, button battery, magnets, sharp object). If Formalife cannot teach one safe high-value decision set in a short micro-lesson, move depth to reference material instead of oversimplifying.

## 11.3 Time gate

Target average:

**~6–7 minutes per secondary topic.**

If a topic genuinely needs 15 minutes to be taught safely, either:

- remove another topic;
- move that topic to a specialist/reference layer;
- or explicitly extend the product duration.

Do not hide a 90-minute secondary syllabus inside a 50-minute slot.

# 12. ACT 6 — Integrated Scenarios — 27 min

Goal:

**force transfer, not repeat chapter labels.**

## 12.1 Scenario matrix

Vary deliberately across:

- infant / toddler / older child;
- home / restaurant / park / car / pool;
- one rescuer / two rescuers;
- mobile phone immediately available / not immediately available;
- effective cough / ineffective cough / unresponsive progression;
- isolated emergency vs mixed context;
- AED available later vs not immediately available.

## 12.2 Suggested choreography

### Scenario A — whole-room mental decision — 4 min

Slide/photo cue -> 20 sec individual answer -> pair discussion -> reveal/debrief.

### Scenario B — whole-room mental decision — 4 min

Different emergency family.

### Scenario C — role allocation / 112 scenario — 4 min

Who acts? Who calls? What information matters? What is first?

### Mirror Test — live transfer scenario — ~9 min

Return to the ACT 0 volunteer if they consent.

Use a **matched but varied** choking/emergency scenario.

Possible progression:

- child/toddler in a different setting;
- effective cough -> ineffective cough;
- second person becomes available;
- then simulated loss of responsiveness.

Observe whether the participant now:

- recognises state;
- applies F-V-A without visible paralysis;
- assigns/activates 112 correctly;
- chooses the correct manoeuvre sequence;
- transitions appropriately if state changes.

Stop before unnecessary extended motor performance; this is an integration check, not another full skills lab.

### Debrief — ~6 min

Questions:

1. What did you notice first this time?
2. What decision changed compared with the opening?
3. Which action became automatic/easier?
4. Where did you still hesitate?
5. What would you do differently with only one rescuer / with two?

Do not stage a fake perfect performance. If the learner still makes an error, use it honestly as one final correction opportunity.

# 13. ACT 7 — Retrieval Close + Continuity — 10 min

The emotional/memory close must happen **before paperwork destroys recency**.

## 13.1 Unaided retrieval — ~3 min

Ask participants, without showing the final summary slide first:

- What is your three-step mental model?
- In severe choking, what is the decision trigger that changes what you do?
- If the child becomes unresponsive, what changes?
- When/how does 112 enter?

Correct only critical errors.

## 13.2 24-hour family action — ~2 min

Each participant chooses one concrete action after returning home, for example:

- show the Pack / key learning to the other caregiver;
- decide who calls 112 / who acts if two adults are present;
- place the emergency contact card;
- review food-preparation risk points;
- identify when they intend to use the refresh.

This creates transfer to the household, not just applause in the room.

## 13.3 Continuity — ~2 min

Remind briefly:

- Guide;
- Family Emergency Pack / rehearsal job;
- dedicated customer updates;
- free practical refresh within 12 months.

Do not over-explain backend products here.

## 13.4 Close line / meaning — ~2 min

Founder/instructor closes on responsibility and preparation, not fear or heroism.

Desired emotional state:

**"non posso controllare tutto; posso essere più preparato a riconoscere e fare la prima cosa giusta."**

Exact wording later.

## 13.5 Documents / certificates / logistics — after the close

Distribute/complete:

- attendance documents/certificate;
- remaining materials;
- Pack;
- support instructions.

### Referral boundary

Do not let a referral pitch become the final emotional memory of the course.

G4 already requires satisfaction/service-recovery gating before referral.

If a physical referral/share pack exists, position it as a useful resource to share and activate the actual referral request after positive feedback, not as an aggressive stage pitch before satisfaction is known.

# 14. Practical design — choking vs CPR

## Choking practice

Recommended method:

**demonstration -> deconstruction -> learner performance -> immediate corrective feedback -> repeat -> varied scenario -> no-prompt attempt.**

Use 2 instructors to keep practice density high.

Measure after first editions:

- waiting time per learner;
- number of actual repetitions per participant;
- critical-error recurrence;
- whether child/infant rotation fits the 70-minute budget;
- participant-reported clarity.

## QCPR practice

Recommended method:

**baseline -> objective feedback -> one improvement goal -> repeat -> fade feedback -> integrated scenario.**

Track:

- depth/rate/recoil metrics where appropriate;
- ventilation feedback if available/relevant;
- improvement from first to corrected attempt;
- fatigue / floor layout bottlenecks;
- manikin-to-learner ratio.

Do not turn QCPR score into a marketing guarantee of clinical competence.

# 16. Evidence gaps / deliberate non-claims

## Choking -> cardiac arrest percentage

OPEN EVIDENCE GAP.

Current FBAO arrest studies do not provide a valid general denominator of all paediatric severe choking events from which to derive a defensible progression percentage.

Decision:

**do not use a percentage.**

Teach:

- unresolved complete/severe obstruction can cause rapid hypoxia;
- loss of responsiveness and cardiac arrest can follow within minutes;
- this is why early effective action and EMS activation matter.

## "Before/after proves the course works"

Do not overclaim.

The Mirror Test can demonstrate immediate in-session improvement on a simulated scenario. It does not demonstrate long-term retention or real-emergency outcome.

## Generic survival graph

Pending source audit.

Do not use if adult/general without clear labelling.

# 17. Event-production standards

These should be scripted/standardized so the experience can later transfer beyond founder delivery.

## Opening

- room ready before doors open;
- manikins/materials already staged;
- no searching for cables or forms during the opening;
- participant names visible where practical;
- start on time.

## Slides

- visual cue > text paragraph;
- one decision per slide where possible;
- scenario/image first, answer second;
- statistics source/year visible;
- no dense medical textbook screenshots.

## Instructor choreography

Primary instructor:

- narrative lead;
- explanation;
- plenary debrief.

Secondary instructor:

- observes participant errors;
- manages equipment;
- leads second station;
- captures questions/parking lot;
- flags fatigue/confusion.

Roles may swap to prevent one-voice fatigue.

## Psychological safety

- volunteer always opt-in;
- no ridicule;
- do not select the "worst" learner as entertainment;
- mistakes are reframed as the reason practice exists;
- instructors correct behaviour/technique, not identity.

## Energy management

- standing/movement in core skill blocks;
- real break;
- active reset;
- alternate voices/instructors;
- no long Q&A tangent;
- secondary micro-lessons use a repeated template to reduce cognitive switching cost.

## Debrief consistency

Use a short standard script:

1. What did you notice?
2. What did you decide?
3. What worked?
4. What would you change?
5. What is the one rule to carry forward?
