# Formalife Offer Universe Map

Status: PROVISIONAL STRATEGIC MAP — founder review pending
Date: 2026-09-21

Purpose: define the thematic universe Formalife may ultimately own, separate the safety/emergency territory from generic parenting, and provide a strategic map for future products, funnels, bundles and lifecycle paths before detailed product construction.

This document is a target map, not authorization to build all products at once. `FORMALIFE_BUILD_SEQUENCE.md` continues to govern execution order.

## 1. Strategic synthesis

The strongest current synthesis is:

- **Tiny Hearts** is the better reference for what Formalife should mean in the market: parent-first health/safety/emergency education with strong human authority, live + online delivery and repeatable products.
- **Genitori.app** is a useful reference for commercial architecture: lifecycle navigation, broad digital merchandising, bundles, bonuses, SOS tools, stage-based packaging and direct-response monetization.
- Formalife should not copy Genitori.app's generic parenting scope. Sleep coaching, language development, motor development, tantrums/emotions and similar domains would dilute the current Formalife meaning unless a future separate brand architecture justifies them.
- Formalife should instead become materially deeper than both Italian first-aid providers and generic parenting academies inside one coherent territory: **pediatric safety, injury prevention, health-risk recognition and emergency preparedness for parents/caregivers**.

## 2. Four thematic pillars of the Formalife territory

### Pillar A — Emergency response and lifesaving skills

Core topics:

- pediatric choking and disobstruction;
- CPR / pediatric resuscitation;
- AED / BLSD exposure and later deep proprietary BLSD training;
- severe bleeding;
- burns / scalds first response;
- head injury / major trauma recognition;
- poisoning / ingestion first response;
- anaphylaxis and severe allergic reaction;
- respiratory distress;
- seizures / febrile convulsions;
- drowning emergency response;
- emergency activation: recognizing severity, calling emergency services, communicating essential information.

This pillar supports the Full Course, BLSD Formalife and certified BLSD lane.

### Pillar B — Injury prevention and safe environments

Core topics:

- choking prevention and feeding-related safety;
- home safety / babyproofing;
- falls, windows, stairs, furniture/furniture-tip-over and environmental hazards;
- burns, hot liquids, fire and electrical hazards;
- poisoning prevention, medicines/chemicals and hazardous ingestion;
- button batteries, magnets and small-object hazards;
- bath and water safety;
- pool/beach/open-water drowning prevention;
- child passenger safety / car seats;
- road / pedestrian / bicycle safety as age-appropriate;
- product safety / recalls and unsafe consumer products;
- outdoor/playground safety;
- sun, heat and cold exposure where age-appropriate;
- travel / holiday / hotel safety;
- caregiver handover and making grandparents/babysitters/nursery contexts safer.

International evidence supports this broader safety territory. Safe Beginnings currently organizes its consumer offer around CPR/first aid, home safety and car-seat safety, with bundles across those domains. WHO identifies road injuries, drowning, burns/fire/heat, poisoning and falls among the major unintentional injury categories affecting children.

### Pillar C — Illness recognition and parent health navigation

Core topic family:

- red flags / critically unwell child;
- fever and when to worry;
- febrile convulsions;
- respiratory illness/distress recognition;
- croup/bronchiolitis-type recognition where scientifically appropriate;
- gastro/dehydration warning signs;
- serious rash / sepsis warning signs;
- allergic reactions/anaphylaxis;
- "home observation vs pediatrician vs urgent care/emergency" decision support within safe educational boundaries.

This pillar is conceptually distinct from emergency-procedure training. It is likely better suited to a substantial digital/reference product, ideally with a pediatrician as visible authority, similar in role to Tiny Hearts' Surviving Sickness course.

Formalife should not become a diagnostic or prescribing service and should avoid product architecture based on individual medical advice unless a properly governed clinical service is deliberately created.

### Pillar D — Life-stage safety transitions

Formalife should package safety needs around moments in which the family's risk environment changes.

Important transitions include:

- pregnancy / preparing the home and caregivers;
- newborn arrival;
- safe sleep / suffocation-risk reduction;
- starting solids / feeding safety;
- crawling and mobility;
- walking / climbing;
- nursery/daycare and new caregivers;
- travel / holidays / pools / beach;
- new sibling / new child;
- age-appropriate road/outdoor independence later.

This pillar does not require Formalife to teach every general parenting topic associated with the stage. It means using the life stage to package the safety/preparedness products relevant at that moment.

## 3. Important missing topic families identified

The current Formalife/Tiny Hearts/Genitori.app comparison reveals several safety domains that deserve explicit place in the long-term universe even though they need not enter the current live flagship:

- **water safety / drowning prevention and response**;
- **car-seat / child passenger safety**;
- **safe sleep / SIDS-suffocation risk reduction**;
- **deep home-safety / babyproofing**;
- **product safety / recalls / button batteries / magnets / tip-over / blind cords and similar hazards**;
- **road, pedestrian, bicycle and outside-the-home safety**;
- **travel / holiday / hotel safety**;
- **sun / heat / cold and seasonal safety**;
- **medication storage and medication-safety systems** distinct from individualized dosing advice;
- **caregiver safety transfer** — ensuring grandparents, babysitters and other carers understand the same safety system;
- **family emergency readiness** — emergency contacts, first-aid kit/reference system, who does what, what information to communicate.

These topics expand Formalife's depth without moving into generic parenting.

## 4. Domains adjacent to the audience but outside the current Formalife core

The following may be commercially attractive and share the same parent audience, but they do not currently reinforce Formalife's safety/emergency meaning strongly enough to become core Formalife-owned lines:

- broad antenatal/birth preparation;
- breastfeeding as a full discipline;
- general infant sleep / sleep coaching;
- crying/soothing as a general parenting topic;
- motor development;
- language development;
- tantrums, rules and emotional education;
- generic child development/pedagogy.

These may later be served through partnerships, cross-promotions, a separate brand or a broader company portfolio if economics justify it. They should not be added merely to imitate Genitori.app's catalog breadth.

Narrow safety sub-problems inside these domains can still fit Formalife: for example **safe sleep** fits, while general sleep training does not.

## 5. Implications for product architecture

The mature Formalife portfolio should not be organized as a flat list of unrelated courses. It can be structured around a few recognizable product families.

### Emergency Skills

- Full Course — pediatric safety/emergency preparedness;
- BLSD Formalife non-certified;
- BLSD certified;
- practical refresh/retraining;
- private/family/caregiver practical formats.

### Safe Home & Safe Mobility

Potential future digital/reference lines:

- home safety / babyproofing;
- car-seat / child passenger safety;
- water/drowning safety;
- travel/out-of-home safety;
- later age-stage road/play safety.

### Child Health Navigation

Potential digital/reference line:

- sickness/red-flags course with pediatric authority;
- structured SOS/reference tools explaining escalation logic within non-diagnostic boundaries.

### Feeding Safety

Potential partnership-led family:

- starting solids / complementary feeding;
- choking prevention and safe feeding;
- possible Formalife x Svezzy book/course/bundle rather than duplicating Svezzy's established competence.

### New Baby Safety

Potential lifecycle bundle, not generic preparto:

- safe sleep;
- car seat basics;
- home preparation;
- choking/CPR awareness;
- first-aid/emergency readiness;
- caregiver preparation.

This could become Formalife's way of entering before weaning without competing in the entire antenatal/parenting category.

## 6. Implications for funnels

The thematic pillars create multiple future entry funnels while keeping one coherent back-end.

Examples of demand triggers:

- fear of choking during starting solids;
- preparing for the arrival of a baby;
- child becomes mobile and the home suddenly becomes hazardous;
- first car seat / transition to another seat;
- first summer/pool/beach holiday;
- nursery/daycare / grandparents begin caring for the child;
- recent illness or uncertainty about red flags;
- customer wants deeper emergency-response competence.

Each trigger can have its own free resource, paid entry product or specialist course, but all routes should feed the same Formalife customer relationship and cross-sell into the relevant safety system.

## 7. Strategic boundary

The long-term objective is not to become "everything parents need".

The stronger target is:

**everything a parent/caregiver should understand, prevent, prepare for or know how to do to keep a child safer and respond appropriately when something goes wrong.**

This boundary is wide enough to support a substantial catalog, lifecycle CRM, bundles and high LTV while remaining far narrower and more defensible than a generic parenting academy.

## 8. Current open strategic decisions

Before converting this map into build commitments, Formalife still needs to decide:

1. which future safety vertical should become the first horizontal expansion after the emergency flagship;
2. which topics should be owned internally versus built through specialist partnership;
3. which life-stage bundle should become the first lifecycle acquisition funnel beyond choking/weaning;
4. whether health-navigation/sickness deserves to be an early pillar or should follow injury-prevention verticals;
5. the future role of an annual safety continuity product once enough recurring value exists.
