# Formalife Market Evidence

Status: CURRENT — market/customer evidence under zero-based reconstruction
Date: 2026-09-21

Purpose: record founder-reported observations and market evidence relevant to customer selection, trigger, alternatives and positioning. Observations are not automatically promoted to strategy decisions.

## B2C customer trigger evidence

### OBSERVATION — dominant problem salience

Founder-reported observation from approximately 70 historical B2C course participants:

- almost all participants expressed fear/concern around pediatric choking;
- the fear was not described merely as a generic interest in first aid, but as concern about the choking event itself.

This is qualitative founder evidence, not a measured survey result.

### OBSERVATION — weaning as recurring timing window

Founder estimates that approximately 50-60% of historical participants were in the weaning / complementary-feeding phase.

This suggests that the transition to solid foods may be a recurring life-stage trigger in which choking risk becomes more salient and the need for preparation moves higher in parental priorities.

The percentage is an informed founder estimate and should not be treated as audited cohort data.

### HYPOTHESIS — initial beachhead customer

A potentially stronger initial customer definition than generic "parents of children 0-6" is:

**parents entering or currently in weaning/complementary feeding who are materially concerned about pediatric choking and want to feel capable of recognizing and responding correctly.**

This is not yet a target decision. It must still be tested against:

1. reachability / identifiability;
2. propensity and capacity to pay;
3. actual alternative behavior before purchase;
4. whether demand is already active or becomes actionable mainly after trusted recommendation/education;
5. customer economics and conversion at the intended offer architecture.

### OBSERVATION — timing may matter more than broad demographics

The current evidence suggests that life-stage timing may explain purchase propensity better than the broad age label "parent of a child 0-6".

If confirmed, Formalife should treat the market as recurring cohorts entering a high-salience window rather than as one static undifferentiated parent audience.

## OPEN QUESTION — current next evidence

The next causal question is the customer's **current alternative / pre-purchase behavior**.

Need founder evidence on what participants typically did before Formalife became an option:

- were they already actively searching for a choking / pediatric first-aid course;
- had they watched videos or read online material;
- had they asked the pediatrician or another professional;
- had they considered Red Cross / hospital / other courses;
- had they experienced or witnessed a choking episode;
- or did the trusted pediatrician invitation itself convert an existing fear into action for people who were not searching.

This distinction is material because it determines whether the beachhead contains primarily active demand, problem-aware latent demand, or a mixture requiring different paths.
