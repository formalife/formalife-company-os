# Funnel 01 — Full Delivery System V4 — Founder-Directed Working Architecture

Status: CURRENT WORKING FOUNDER-DIRECTED ARCHITECTURE — supersedes V3 as the active delivery design; exact clinical wording/procedures and data slides require Dott.ssa Mafalda Camposarcone review before customer use
Date: 2026-09-22

Purpose: consolidate founder decisions from the V3 review into the current live-course architecture, while preserving 2025 ERC/IRC/AHA evidence boundaries, attention management, deliberate practice and live-event design.

This file supersedes `FUNNEL_01_FULL_DELIVERY_SYSTEM_V3_FOUNDER_RESEARCH_PROPOSAL.md` as the active working architecture. V3 remains historical research/provenance.

Commercial architecture remains:

**choking/weaning trigger -> broader parent-first preparedness system -> BEFORE -> LIVE -> AFTER / HOME REFERENCE + REFRESH.**

Instructional architecture:

**impactful baseline -> problem salience -> mental framework -> deep choking learning -> break -> paediatric CPR learning -> reset -> structured secondary emergencies -> complex transfer scenarios -> continuity.**

---

# 1. Founder decisions incorporated

## ACT 0

- opening includes short Formalife/instructor introduction;
- rapid room-calibration questions are retained;
- use an impactful experiential baseline with a consenting volunteer;
- no humiliation or tricking;
- ACT 6 repeats the experiential test with a **more complex transfer scenario**, not the same scenario, to show both basic improvement and transfer to greater complexity.

## ACT 3 — choking

- retain explanation of partial/complete obstruction as a clinically useful concept;
- operational recognition is taught through the observable distinction the founder already uses: **effective cough / audible airflow vs ineffective or absent cough / little or no sound**, subject to scientific wording review;
- retain only selected edge cases that are relevant to the caregiver context: adolescent with obesity / rescuer who is pregnant or materially unable to perform normal abdominal-thrust positioning;
- full-speed demonstration + deconstruction are shown to everyone first;
- only then split into two practical groups/stations: child and infant;
- groups rotate;
- after both practical stations everyone reunites for the post-event pathway: obstruction relieved vs deterioration/unresponsiveness;
- 112 timing is integrated explicitly rather than treated as an afterthought.

## ACT 4 — paediatric resuscitation

- teach a simple CAB framing rather than omitting it;
- consumer track uses **5 initial rescue breaths + 30:2** as the current working ERC/IRC-aligned direction for rescuers not trained to ERC PBLS-course level;
- ventilations remain an essential taught/practised element;
- QCPR compression drill is intentionally brief because Full is not a BLSD course;
- compression micro-drill = **60 seconds total**: first 20 seconds no coaching, next 20 seconds one priority correction, final 20 seconds all necessary coaching; then inspect QCPR result/graph;
- no public leaderboard;
- the point is visible improvement from feedback, not competition;
- full CPR sequence is still demonstrated and participants execute the integrated 30:2 sequence at least at a basic level; the QCPR 60-second drill is only the compression-quality sub-exercise.

## ACT 5

- secondary emergencies are taught **one by one**, directly, not opened with individual micro-cases;
- every topic uses the same stable content grammar:
  **what it is -> prevention -> recognition -> what to do -> what NOT to do -> escalation / 112 / urgent evaluation**;
- current scope includes foreign-body ingestion, head trauma, febrile seizures, poisoning, drowning, burns, anaphylaxis/severe breathing difficulty and severe bleeding/major trauma as appropriate;
- exact scope remains subject to time and scientific responsibility: if a topic cannot be responsibly taught in the allocated depth, it must move to reference/post-course rather than be rushed.

## ACT 7

- final memorable close is still OPEN for later design;
- administrative handoff, certificate, Pack, customer stream and refresh remain part of conclusion operations;
- referral is handled through a separate referral system rather than a hard-sell closing speech.

# 2. Current calendar architecture

Working target remains approximately **4h10–4h20 total calendar time**.

Recommended V4 planning allocation:

| Act | Working duration | Job |
|---|---:|---|
| ACT 0 — Open + experiential baseline | 12 min | human connection, calibration, cold-start experience |
| ACT 1 — The Problem | 6 min | proportional epidemiological/statistical framing |
| ACT 2 — The Mentality / F-V-A | 7 min | common mental orientation framework |

| BREAK | 10 min | real recovery |
| ACT 4 — Paediatric resuscitation / QCPR | 55 min | mechanism + 112/AED + breaths + 30:2 + brief feedback-based compression practice |

This is an operating allocation, not a sacred minute budget. First deliveries should produce timing evidence.

# 3. ACT 0 — Open + Experiential Baseline

## 3.1 Human opening

~2 minutes maximum:

- who we are;
- why this course exists;
- what the participant should be able to recognise/do differently by the end.

## 3.2 Room calibration

~3 minutes using quick hand-raise / fast answers:

- age of child/children;
- starting solids / current feeding stage where relevant;
- prior choking/first-aid training;
- what brought them to the course.

Do not spend 12 x 2 minutes on biographies.

## 3.3 Experiential baseline — founder signature mechanic

~7 minutes.

Use one consenting volunteer.

Preferred structure:

1. facilitator describes a realistic child context;
2. initial cue: child coughs effectively / audible cough;
3. progression: cough becomes ineffective / no meaningful sound or ventilation;
4. volunteer reacts without coaching for a short safe window;
5. stop;
6. ask volunteer and room what they noticed / what drove the decision;
7. state explicitly that this is a baseline, not an exam or humiliation.

Use a manikin/trainer for any physical action.

At ACT 6 use a **different and more complex transfer scenario** with the same volunteer if willing. Add at least one complexity variable such as different age, different location, second rescuer, phone availability, deterioration to unresponsiveness or mixed cue sequence.

Learning/proof boundary:

- the visible change is powerful event evidence of immediate learning/transfer;
- do not convert it into a claim of guaranteed long-term real-emergency competence.

# 4. ACT 1 — The Problem

Keep short and evidence-dense.

Use a small number of sourced data points on:

- domestic paediatric injury burden;
- choking relevance in young children;
- ED/hospital utilisation where traceable;
- prevention + early action as the practical reason for training.

Every number must show source/year.

Do not use fear montage or mortality arithmetic without defensible denominators.

Transition:

**the problem is large enough to deserve preparation, but the purpose of the course is agency, not panic.**

---

# 5. ACT 2 — The Mentality

Introduce:

**FERMA -> VALUTA -> AGISCI**

Founder intention:

- reduce the participant's fear of mentally freezing;
- create a repeatable orientation cue used throughout the rest of the course;
- details live in the Guide/materials rather than a long opening lecture.

Boundary:

`FERMA` = a seconds-long cognitive orientation/reset, never permission to delay a time-critical intervention.

Repeat the language later in scenarios so it becomes a real schema.

# 6. ACT 3 — Choking

Working duration: ~72 min.

## 6.1 Prevention + anatomy/physiology — ~10 min

Prevention:

- eating context;
- safe food preparation principles: shape, consistency, size where scientifically approved;
- behaviour/environmental hazards;
- deeper prevention remains in Guide.

Mechanism:

- simple airway/anatomy illustration;
- why foreign body obstruction occurs;
- why young children are especially vulnerable;
- enough physiology to make later decisions intuitive, not an anatomy lecture.

## 6.2 Recognition and child sequence — ~10 min

Explain partial/complete obstruction and connect it to the practical recognition cue:

- effective cough / meaningful sound-airflow;
- ineffective/absent cough / inability to vocalise or breathe effectively.

Child >1 year:

- what to do / not do with effective cough;
- severe/ineffective-cough sequence;
- 112 logic;
- full-speed demonstration;
- deconstruction.

## 6.3 Infant sequence — ~10 min

Infant <1 year:

- effective vs ineffective cough;
- what to do / not do;
- positioning/support;
- back blows + chest thrusts under approved current sequence;
- no abdominal thrusts;
- avoid shaking / unsafe handling;
- 112 logic;
- full-speed demonstration;
- deconstruction.

## 6.4 Selected special cases — ~3 min

Only useful edge cases:

- adolescent with obesity / inability to encircle abdomen normally;
- rescuer who is pregnant or materially unable to perform standard abdominal-thrust positioning.

Exact technique/wording requires Mafalda/ERC/IRC review.

## 6.5 Practical station lab — ~30–32 min

### Whole-room steps already completed

1. full-speed demonstration;
2. deconstruction.

### Split after theory

- Group A starts child station;
- Group B starts infant station;
- rotate after first station;
- two instructors, one per station.

### Equipment-density target

To make 15-minute stations meaningful with six learners, target approximately **one functional trainer/manikin per 2 learners** where feasible. If equipment density is lower, increase practice time or reduce group size; do not let five people watch one person for most of the station.

### Inside each station

3. guided practice;
4. immediate correction — one critical issue at a time;
5. immediate corrected repetition;
6. short variable-state scenario;
7. final no-prompt attempt.

Use paired roles when equipment allows:

- active rescuer;
- observer using a very short skill checklist;
- swap quickly.

Instructor owns safety/clinical correction; peer checklist is not a clinical pass/fail tool.

## 6.6 Rejoin: what happens next — ~7–9 min

After both stations reunite the room.

Cover explicitly:

### Obstruction resolves

- stop disobstruction when appropriate;
- reassess child;
- current ERC direction indicates urgent medical follow-up after successful relief; exact Italian customer instruction requires Mafalda review.

### Obstruction does not resolve / child becomes unresponsive

- transition into PBLS/RCP;
- this becomes the narrative bridge to ACT 4.

### 112

For severe/ineffective-cough FBAO:

- activate EMS as early as practical;
- two rescuers: one acts, one calls;
- alone with mobile: speakerphone while continuing response where feasible;
- do not deliberately wait until every manoeuvre has failed if communication can occur without abandoning care.

# 7. ACT 4 — Paediatric Resuscitation / QCPR

Working duration: ~55 min.

## 7.1 Narrative mechanism — ~5 min

Teach the causal bridge:

**unresolved severe airway obstruction / severe respiratory problem -> hypoxia -> loss of consciousness -> cardiac arrest can follow.**

Do not give a percentage for how often pediatric FBAO progresses to cardiac arrest unless a denominator-valid paediatric source is later identified.

## 7.2 Survival evidence slide — revised architecture

Do **not** present an adult CPR/AED graph as if its numeric curves transfer to children.

Preferred two-panel slide:

### PANEL A — Adult OHCA / shockable-arrest principle

Use traceable adult evidence to show:

- immediate CPR improves survival;
- early defibrillation is critical when VF/pVT is present;
- AHA 2025 adult BLS cites ARREST registry data where each minute of delay to first shock in witnessed VF was associated with ~6% lower probability of survival to discharge;
- AHA public education states immediate CPR can roughly double or triple adult OHCA survival.

Label population clearly:

**adult out-of-hospital cardiac arrest; defibrillation benefit especially relevant to shockable rhythms.**

### PANEL B — Pediatric translation

Teach the clinically different pediatric message:

- most pediatric OHCA are respiratory/asphyxial and initially non-shockable;
- breaths + compressions matter;
- bystander CPR is associated with better pediatric survival;
- conventional CPR with rescue breaths has shown better neurologically favorable outcomes than no CPR and, in several observational datasets, better outcomes than compression-only CPR;
- AED remains lifesaving when a shockable rhythm exists, but shockable rhythms are a minority in infants/children and become more common with age/adolescence.

Therefore do not say the adult curve is quantitatively “indicative for children”. Say:

**the adult graph illustrates the general time-critical nature of CPR/defibrillation, while pediatric physiology and rhythm distribution differ and are taught separately.**

## 7.3 Customer CPR algorithm — current working direction

Formalife consumer Full is not an ERC PBLS certification course.

Current evidence supports:

- **5 initial rescue breaths**;
- then CPR **30:2** for rescuers not specifically trained to ERC PBLS-course/equivalent level;
- ventilations must be included because pediatric arrests are commonly respiratory/asphyxial.

This is the current working direction to be approved by Mafalda before use.

## 7.4 112 / AED logic

Teach visually:

### Two rescuers

- one starts CPR;
- second activates 112 and retrieves AED.

### One rescuer with phone

- prioritise calling 112 using speaker mode and starting CPR.

### One rescuer without phone

ERC 2025 states approximately one minute of CPR before leaving to seek help.

AED:

- attach as soon as available without compromising CPR;
- follow device prompts;
- make clear AED shocks only shockable rhythms.

## 7.5 Demonstration — ~8 min

Demonstrate complete simplified sequence for child and infant, including breaths and 30:2.

Avoid spending Full-course time on deep BLSD detail.

## 7.6 QCPR compression micro-drill — founder decision

Each learner performs **60 seconds total** of compressions:

- **0–20 sec:** no coaching;
- **20–40 sec:** instructor gives one highest-priority correction;
- **40–60 sec:** instructor gives all essential corrections required for the drill.

Then review QCPR result/graph briefly.

Purpose:

- experience objective feedback;
- make improvement visible;
- calibrate depth/rate/recoil according to the device/approved technique;
- not certify skill mastery.

No leaderboard.

If equipment allows, learners work in parallel to maintain density.

## 7.7 Ventilation + 30:2 integration

Because Full is not BLSD, keep this concise but real:

- learner must practise the taught ventilation technique;
- learner must execute at least a basic integrated 30:2 sequence after the component demonstration;
- do not let the 60-second compression drill accidentally teach that pediatric CPR is compression-only.

Exact child/infant technique and minimum repetitions require Mafalda review.

# 9. ACT 5 — Other Emergencies

Working duration: ~48 min.

No micro-case opening requirement.

Teach each topic directly with identical grammar:

1. **Cos'è / cosa sta succedendo**;
2. **Come prevenirlo** where meaningful;
3. **Come riconoscerlo / red flags**;
4. **Cosa fare**;
5. **Cosa NON fare**;
6. **Quando chiamare 112 / quando serve valutazione urgente**.

Current topic pool:

- foreign-body ingestion;
- head injury / falls;
- febrile seizures;
- poisoning / toxic exposure;
- drowning / water incident;
- burns/scalds;
- anaphylaxis / severe allergic reaction;
- severe breathing difficulty;
- serious bleeding / major injury first response.

Not every topic must consume identical minutes.

Rule:

**if scientific review shows a topic cannot be taught responsibly at a first-action/red-flag level within the available time, move it to post-course/reference instead of compressing it into unsafe oversimplification.**

Use visual simplicity and the same slide grammar for every emergency to reduce cognitive switching cost.

# 10. ACT 6 — Integrated Scenarios + Complex Mirror Test

Working duration: ~25 min.

## 10.1 Scenario set

Use several short mental/visual scenarios varying:

- infant / toddler / older child/adolescent;
- home / restaurant / car / park / water;
- one rescuer / two rescuers;
- phone immediately available or not;
- stable vs deteriorating child;
- recognition-only vs motor action;
- choking -> unresponsive -> CPR transition where appropriate.

Participants apply:

**FERMA -> VALUTA -> AGISCI**

with concrete questions:

- what do you see/hear?;
- what state is this?;
- what do you do first?;
- what must you not do?;
- who calls 112 and when?;
- what changes if the child deteriorates?

## 10.2 Complex Mirror Test

Return to the ACT 0 volunteer if willing.

The final scenario must be **more complex** than the baseline, not merely repeated.

Example structure:

- different age or setting;
- initially effective cough;
- deterioration to severe ineffective cough;
- second rescuer arrives or phone constraint is introduced;
- obstruction persists and child becomes unresponsive;
- volunteer must recognise state change, allocate 112, perform correct choking response and transition conceptually/physically into CPR.

The room observes the difference between baseline behaviour and post-course behaviour.

Debrief:

- what did the learner recognise earlier?;
- which decision became clearer?;
- where did F-V-A help?;
- what still needs correction?

Do not script a fake perfect ending. If the participant makes an error, correct it openly and positively.

# 11. ACT 7 — Conclusion / Continuity

Working duration: ~10 min.

Current required operations:

- short retrieval of critical anchors;
- transition to Guide / Family Emergency Pack;
- explain customer update stream;
- explain free refresh within 12 months;
- attendance/certificate/document operations;
- referral system seeded through Pack and later follow-up.

**Memorable final close remains OPEN.**

Do not fill this gap with a generic motivational speech merely to finish the run-of-show.

# 12. Attention choreography

Apply throughout:

- no prolonged passive monologue;
- change learner mode regularly;
- use visual contrast, demonstration, physical practice, question/retrieval and feedback;
- the long choking/CPR acts are not long lectures: they alternate explanation -> demo -> practice -> correction -> repetition -> integration;
- ACT 5 uses a stable repeated slide/teaching pattern so topic changes do not create structural confusion.

# 13. Equipment/capacity gates still open

Before final run-of-show record:

- number/type of child choking trainers;
- number/type of infant choking manikins/trainers;
- number of QCPR child manikins;
- whether QCPR infant feedback is available;
- ventilation barriers/masks required;
- AED trainers available;

Practice time is only credible if equipment density supports the planned simultaneous work.
