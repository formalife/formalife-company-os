# Funnel 01 — ACT 4 CPR / QCPR Run-of-Show V1

Status: CURRENT PRODUCTION PROPOSAL — derived from founder-approved V5 architecture, current ERC 2025 guidance and actual Formalife equipment; exact clinical wording/technique requires Dott.ssa Mafalda Camposarcone sign-off before customer delivery
Date: 2026-09-22

Purpose: convert ACT 4 `La Rianimazione` from architecture into a rehearsable 55-minute live operating sequence that teaches one simple pediatric-resuscitation path, makes the importance of ventilations/AED/112 understandable, and gives every participant objective QCPR compression feedback without turning the Full into a BLSD course.

Parent architecture:

- `FUNNEL_01_FULL_DELIVERY_SYSTEM_V5_CANONICAL.md`
- `FUNNEL_01_DELIVERY_EQUIPMENT_INVENTORY.md`

Current founder decisions governing this run-of-show:

- do **not** teach CAB as a mnemonic;
- teach the simple working sequence **5 initial rescue breaths -> 30:2 -> AED as soon as available -> continue according to 112/AED instructions**;
- the scored QCPR learner exercise is **compression-only** so ventilation activity does not contaminate the compression graph;
- the compression drill is **60 seconds total: 20 sec no coaching -> 20 sec one correction -> 20 sec full essential correction**;
- use current 2 Little Junior QCPR + 2 Little Baby QCPR in parallel;
- Full remains broader parent preparedness, not BLSD mastery.

---

# 1. ACT 4 outcome

At the end of ACT 4 a participant should, at layperson level and subject to scientific validation, be better able to:

1. understand why severe respiratory failure/choking can progress to unconsciousness and cardiac arrest;
2. recognise the practical transition into the pediatric-resuscitation pathway;
3. recall the Formalife consumer sequence:
   **5 initial rescue breaths -> 30 compressions : 2 breaths -> AED as soon as available -> continue following 112/AED**;
4. understand why pediatric CPR includes ventilations and why adult survival graphics cannot simply be transferred numerically to children;
5. understand the operating role of 112 and AED with one versus two rescuers;
6. see and feel how compression quality changes with objective feedback;
7. perform the 60-second compression drill on both a child and infant QCPR manikin;
8. interpret at a basic level what QCPR feedback says about rate, depth and release;
9. understand that the exercise is an introduction to quality CPR, not BLSD certification or proof of long-term mastery.

# 3. 00:00–00:04 — Narrative bridge: from choking to arrest

## Job

Continue the story from ACT 3 instead of starting a new chapter.

## Teaching line

The obstruction has not resolved, oxygen delivery falls, the child can become unresponsive and cardiac arrest may follow.

Do not teach a fabricated percentage for how often pediatric choking progresses to arrest unless denominator-valid pediatric evidence is later identified.

## Learner mode

Listen -> answer one transition question.

Suggested question:

**“La situazione cambia completamente quando il bambino non risponde più: qual è adesso il problema che dobbiamo affrontare?”**

Use the answer to move from disobstruction into resuscitation.

## Slide job

One simple causal visual:

**severe unresolved airway/respiratory problem -> hypoxia -> loss of consciousness -> cardiac arrest can follow.**

---

# 4. 00:04–00:09 — Survival evidence: adult principle vs pediatric reality

## Job

Show why immediate action matters without misusing adult data as pediatric evidence.

## Two-panel slide

### Panel A — adult OHCA / shockable-rhythm principle

Use a clearly labelled adult graph/evidence to communicate:

- early CPR matters;
- when a shockable rhythm exists, earlier defibrillation matters;
- delay worsens outcome.

Do not label the adult numeric curve as a pediatric survival curve.

### Panel B — pediatric translation

Explain concisely:

- pediatric arrests are commonly respiratory/asphyxial and often initially non-shockable;
- ventilations therefore matter;
- AED remains important when a shockable rhythm is present;
- pediatric physiology/rhythm distribution differs from the adult population represented by many familiar CPR/AED graphs.

Teaching line:

**“Il grafico adulto ci fa vedere quanto il tempo conti. Nel bambino il meccanismo è spesso diverso: per questo nel nostro algoritmo le ventilazioni hanno un ruolo centrale e il DAE va usato appena disponibile quando indicato.”**

Scientific/statistical wording must be approved by Mafalda before slide production.

# 5. 00:09–00:15 — Recognition + simple pediatric sequence

## Job

Give one sequence a parent can retrieve without teaching a BLSD taxonomy.

## Recognition before the algorithm

Before starting the sequence, make clear that the participant has already identified an unresponsive child who is not breathing normally / meets the approved resuscitation trigger.

Exact recognition wording requires Mafalda/IRC sign-off.

## Founder-approved working sequence

Teach visually as one vertical path:

**5 ventilazioni iniziali**

↓

**30 compressioni : 2 ventilazioni**

↓

**DAE appena disponibile**

↓

**continua secondo indicazioni 112 / DAE**

Do not introduce CAB as a mnemonic.

## Why 30:2

Current ERC 2025 direction:

- 15:2 for rescuers specifically trained to ERC PBLS-course/equivalent level;
- otherwise 30:2.

Formalife Full is a consumer preparedness course, not an ERC PBLS certification course; current working direction is therefore 30:2.

## Attention design

Do not spend six minutes talking continuously.

Use:

1. reveal the sequence;
2. ask the room to repeat it once;
3. hide it;
4. ask one participant to reconstruct it verbally;
5. reveal/correct.

This creates retrieval before the physical demo.

---

# 6. 00:15–00:20 — 112 + AED logic

## Job

Remove ambiguity over who calls, when, and what the AED actually does.

## Slide architecture

### Two rescuers

- rescuer 1 starts/continues CPR;
- rescuer 2 activates 112 and retrieves AED;
- AED attached as soon as practical without unnecessary interruption.

### One rescuer with phone

- activate 112 using speaker mode according to the approved current sequence;
- begin/continue CPR without unnecessary delay.

### One rescuer without phone

Current ERC direction includes approximately one minute of CPR before leaving to seek help in the relevant single-rescuer/no-phone pediatric scenario.

Exact customer wording requires Mafalda/IRC sign-off.

## AED concept

Explain only what the parent needs:

- power on / follow prompts;
- expose chest / place pads in approved position;
- nobody touches during analysis/shock command;
- the device decides whether a shock is indicated;
- resume CPR when instructed.

One Laerdal AED Trainer is sufficient for demonstration/integrated scenario use.

### Pre-rehearsal equipment check

Before customer use verify exact Formalife AED Trainer model and whether pediatric training pads/activator/accessory required by that model are actually present.

# 7. 00:20–00:29 — Whole-room demonstrations

## Job

Show the complete flow before asking participants to isolate compression quality.

The participant should understand **where the compression drill sits inside the real sequence**.

## 7.1 Child demonstration — ~4.5 min

Instructor performs the complete simplified sequence at realistic rhythm:

- recognition/positioning;
- 5 initial rescue breaths;
- 30:2;
- 112 role;
- AED arrival/application at the appropriate point;
- continue according to AED/112 prompts.

Then a very short deconstruction highlighting only the most important physical landmarks/technique.

## 7.2 Infant demonstration — ~4.5 min

Repeat the full sequence for infant, highlighting only the differences that the lay learner must retain:

- approved infant ventilation technique;
- approved infant compression technique;
- appropriate AED/pad approach according to validated guidance/device context.

Do not create a second mini-BLSD lecture.

## Founder boundary on learner exercise

The following QCPR learner drill is **compression-only**.

Ventilations are part of the taught/demo sequence but are not mixed into the scored 60-second QCPR drill because the founder wants the compression trace to remain clean and comparable across the three 20-second phases.

Any future hands-on ventilation practice must be a separate unscored exercise and requires a deliberate time decision rather than being smuggled into the QCPR drill.

---

# 8. 00:29–00:31 — Practical-lab briefing

## Room configuration

Maximum class: 12.

Split into:

- **Group A — 6 learners:** 2 Little Junior QCPR;
- **Group B — 6 learners:** 2 Little Baby QCPR.

Each station therefore has:

- 2 manikins;
- 3 learners per manikin;
- 1 instructor responsible for that age station.

After first station, groups rotate.

## Learner roles per manikin

Three-person pod:

1. active rescuer;
2. observer/timekeeper;
3. next learner / reset support.

Roles rotate every 60-second drill.

No peer “certification”. Instructor owns correction.

---

# 9. 00:31–00:44 — Parallel QCPR lab + rotation

## Design principle

All four pediatric QCPR manikins run simultaneously.

This is the key efficiency gain from Formalife's current equipment.

## Station 1 — approximately 5.5–6 min

Both age stations run in parallel.

Each manikin has three learners.

Each learner performs one **60-second compression-only drill**:

### 0–20 sec — baseline

- no coaching;
- no visible learner feedback if operationally possible;
- instructor observes and QCPR records.

### 20–40 sec — one correction

Instructor gives exactly **one highest-priority correction** based on the learner's dominant error.

Examples of correction category, subject to device/guideline settings:

- rate;
- depth;
- recoil/release;
- hand/finger position where observable/relevant.

Do not unload three corrections at second 20.

### 40–60 sec — full essential correction

Instructor now gives the remaining essential corrections necessary to improve the drill.

Goal:

**feel the difference between uncalibrated effort and coached compressions.**

## Changeovers

Keep each learner swap to roughly 15–20 seconds.

With two simultaneous manikins per station:

- round 1 = learner A on each manikin;
- round 2 = learner B;
- round 3 = learner C.

Pure compression time = 3 minutes per station.

The remaining station time covers positioning, swap and one-line result capture.

## Rotation — ~1 min

Group A moves to infant.

Group B moves to child.

Manikins stay in place.

Instructors stay with the **age station**, not the learner group, to reduce setup variability and improve specialist observation.

## Station 2 — approximately 5.5–6 min

Repeat the same protocol.

Therefore every participant completes:

- one 60-second child compression drill;
- one 60-second infant compression drill.

Total individual scored compression exposure = **120 seconds per learner** across the two ages.

This is intentionally short and feedback-dense because the Full is not BLSD.

# 10. QCPR feedback operating rules

## App configuration

Before rehearsal:

- pair/check all four pediatric QCPR manikins;
- verify batteries;
- verify guideline/settings profile;
- verify naming/identification so results are not confused across learners;
- test simultaneous four-manikin monitoring with the chosen Laerdal app/device.

Laerdal supports multi-manikin monitoring and post-session debriefing. Little Junior QCPR can be monitored with multiple learners at once; Little Baby QCPR is compatible with QCPR feedback applications.

## Timeline use

The QCPR ecosystem includes timeline/debrief views showing performance over time.

Formalife should use the **timeline**, not only one final overall score, because the teaching job is to make the three phases visible:

**baseline -> one correction -> fuller correction.**

Do not promise that every app/version will automatically label the 20/20/20 phases; instructor timing markers and the timeline must be tested during rehearsal.

## No leaderboard

Do not rank participants publicly.

The comparison is primarily:

**learner vs learner's own baseline.**

---

# 11. 00:44–00:49 — QCPR debrief

## Job

Convert the physical sensation into explicit learning without drowning the group in metrics.

## Debrief structure

Project/show one or two representative anonymised traces/results, or use the instructor device visibly if practical.

Ask:

1. **“Cosa è cambiato quando hai ricevuto la prima correzione?”**
2. **“Quale correzione ti ha fatto cambiare di più il risultato o la sensazione?”**
3. **“Cosa pensavi di fare bene prima che il manichino te lo mostrasse?”**

Then explain only the core metrics:

- rate;
- depth;
- complete release/recoil.

Do not turn this into a QCPR analytics lecture.

## Proof boundary

The visible within-session improvement is instructional evidence.

It is **not** proof that the participant will perform correctly months later or in a real emergency.

---

# 12. 00:49–00:53 — Full-sequence retrieval / decision rehearsal

The compression drill deliberately isolates one component.

Now reconnect it to the full mental sequence without adding another long physical exercise.

Hide the algorithm slide.

Use fast room prompts:

- **“Bambino incosciente, non respira normalmente: da dove parti?”**
- **“Dopo le cinque ventilazioni?”**
- **“Rapporto?”**
- **“Quando arriva il DAE?”**
- **“Siete in due: chi fa cosa?”**
- **“Se il DAE dice che non è indicato lo shock?”**

Participants answer verbally / by pointing to a visual sequence.

Then reveal the simple path again:

**5 ventilazioni -> 30:2 -> DAE -> continua secondo 112/DAE.**

This prevents the memorable QCPR drill from replacing the actual resuscitation algorithm in memory.

---

# 13. 00:53–00:55 — Close / transition

One final retrieval question:

**“Qual è la cosa più importante che cambia, nel bambino, rispetto all'idea intuitiva di fare solo compressioni?”**

Expected teaching point:

**ventilation matters in pediatric resuscitation; compressions are not the whole algorithm.**

Then stop.

No extra lecture.

Move into the planned 5-minute mini-break / active reset before ACT 5.

# 15. Instructor choreography

## Instructor 1

During theory/demo:

- primary explanation / one of the demonstrations.

During lab:

- owns **child QCPR station**.

## Instructor 2

During theory/demo:

- supports demonstration/AED setup / second age demonstration.

During lab:

- owns **infant QCPR station**.

Instructors stay with their station across rotation.

Reason:

- less equipment/app switching;
- easier calibration;
- each instructor sees six learners on the same age mechanics rather than changing technique halfway through.

After rehearsal, swap instructor age ownership in a second internal run to make the delivery transferable rather than person-dependent.

## Learning friction

Observe:

- confusion about 5 breaths;
- confusion about 30:2;
- confusion about 112 timing;
- confusion about AED/shock decision;
- infant vs child compression-technique confusion;
- whether QCPR score distracts from the algorithm;
- whether 60 seconds feels too short/too long for the instructional job.

# 17. Go / revise criteria after rehearsal

ACT 4 can move toward release candidate when:

1. total duration stays within approximately 55 minutes without rushing;
2. every participant completes both child and infant compression drills;
3. the 20/20/20 coaching sequence is operationally manageable with two instructors and four manikins;
4. QCPR timeline/result is readable enough to support the visible-feedback learning moment;
5. learners can reconstruct the simple 5-breaths -> 30:2 -> AED pathway immediately after practice;
6. AED/112 teaching does not create major unresolved confusion;
7. Mafalda signs off clinical wording, technique and device-specific pediatric instructions;
8. the block clearly feels like meaningful emergency-preparedness training without becoming a compressed BLSD certification course.

If a criterion fails, revise the smallest causal element first: timing, app choreography, demo, learner grouping or content density.
