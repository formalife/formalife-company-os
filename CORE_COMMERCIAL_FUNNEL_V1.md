# Formalife Core Commercial Funnel V1

Status: PROVISIONAL OPERATING ARCHITECTURE — founder review pending
Date: 2026-09-21

Purpose: turn the current choking/weaning wedge, Guide, Light, Training Credit and rebuilt Full Course into the **first complete measurable commercial engine** for Formalife.

This file is deliberately narrower than `FORMALIFE_COMMERCIAL_ARCHITECTURE.md`. It specifies the first engine Formalife should prove before building broader lifecycle automation or adjacent funnels.

It does **not** authorize unverified clinical claims, new guarantees, new prices, paid-media scale or a mandatory Guide -> Light -> Full staircase.

## 1. Diagnosis

The previous diagrams correctly mapped customer needs but mixed three different things:

1. demand/source;
2. product/capability nodes;
3. commercial offers and routing.

The current business needs one end-to-end commercial system before it needs more products.

The first engine must therefore answer, for every material step:

- who enters;
- why the need is salient now;
- where the person came from;
- what demand/awareness state they are in;
- what action Formalife asks them to take;
- what offer they receive;
- what proof reduces uncertainty;
- what happens if they do not buy;
- what happens after delivery;
- what the next economically relevant state is;
- what number determines whether the step deserves to remain.

## 2. Current evidence and decisions

### FORMALIFE EVIDENCE

- choking fear/concern was common among historical B2C participants;
- approximately 50-60% were founder-estimated to be in the weaning/complementary-feeding phase;
- approximately 90% of historical/current participants came through one pediatrician relationship and approximately 10% through word of mouth;
- direct cash media spend on those observed sources was approximately EUR 0;
- the historical course commonly ran with 4-8 participants and around 6 on average;
- existing assets include Guide, Light pilot, Training Credit, live teaching/practice capability, customer proof, partner-hosted capability and transactional infrastructure.

### CURRENT FOUNDER DECISIONS

- choking/weaning remains the primary acquisition wedge;
- Guide remains choking-specific;
- Light remains choking-specific and especially suitable for partner-hosted acquisition;
- rebuilt Full Course becomes the broader parent-first pediatric safety/emergency-preparedness flagship;
- Full Course target remains EUR 80 Single / EUR 120 Couple and 10-12 paying participants per edition;
- BLSD Formalife is later specialist depth, not a mandatory immediate upsell;
- certified BLSD remains a separate credential lane.

### OPEN EVIDENCE

- active-search vs latent-demand mix is still unknown;
- final market-facing differentiation of the rebuilt Full Course is not yet proven;
- revised Full Course still requires scientific curriculum validation before public sale;
- current Guide/Light unit economics are incomplete;
- exact customer-facing Training Credit conditions need standardization/reconciliation;
- effective mailing-list/consent/customer counts are not yet reliable enough to assume an owned-distribution channel;
- end-to-end checkout/attribution still requires verification.

## 3. Governing funnel decision — no mandatory low-ticket staircase

Formalife should **not** force every prospect through:

**Guide -> Light -> Full Course.**

That would add friction to people already ready to buy and could make Guide/Light look like deliberately incomplete toll booths.

The V1 funnel is instead a **state router with three legitimate first-purchase routes**:

### Route A — Direct Full Course

For high-intent prospects who already want substantial practical preparation.

Typical sources:

- trusted pediatric/healthcare referral;
- word of mouth/referral;
- direct/product-aware search;
- retargeted or returning prospect who is already ready.

Primary CTA:

**book the next appropriate Full Course edition.**

This is the first route Formalife should prove because historical trust-transfer already generated direct course buyers.

### Route B — Light first

For a prospect who wants supervised practical exposure but is not yet ready for the broader flagship, or where a partner-hosted event is the natural access mechanism.

Current pilot offer:

- approximately 2 hours;
- EUR 30 Single / EUR 40 Couple;
- Guide included;
- focused on choking prevention/recognition and brief supervised practice;
- Training Credit can bridge into the Full Course under defined conditions.

Primary CTA:

**reserve a Light/partner-hosted place.**

Light remains a distinct useful experience. It must not be intentionally crippled to force the Full Course.

### Route C — Guide first

For a prospect who wants focused home reference/self-education, is not ready for an event, or prefers a low-friction paid first step.

Current offer:

- physical Guida Anti-Panico al Soffocamento Pediatrico;
- EUR 19.90;
- reported final internal version 176 pages;
- Training Credit toward Full Course under defined conditions.

Primary CTA:

**buy the Guide.**

The Guide is a real paid product and authority/reference object, not merely a disguised lead magnet.

### Route D — no current paid step

If the person is problem-aware but not ready for Guide, Light or Full, the correct outcome can be:

**useful content/reference + lawful permission + waiting state.**

Do not manufacture urgency or repeatedly send the same direct offer.

## 4. Demand and routing logic

The funnel must store **source** and **state** separately.

Source answers:

**where did this person come from?**

State answers:

**what do they understand, want and need now?**

### Primary source hierarchy for V1

Before cold paid acquisition, prioritize:

1. existing trusted pediatric/healthcare/family distribution;
2. referral from satisfied customers/caregivers;
3. existing identifiable prospects/buyers where lawful consent and relevance are verified;
4. direct active-intent demand;
5. only later broader paid/interruptive demand.

The reason is not ideological. Formalife already has evidence of trust-transfer distribution and no proven cold CAC.

### Demand-state routing

#### High intent / product-aware

Signal examples:

- explicitly asks for the practical course;
- wants a date/price/place;
- trusted referral has already pre-educated the person;
- searches for a relevant parent first-aid/emergency course and is ready to compare offers.

Route:

**Direct Full Course.**

Do not slow this prospect with mandatory Guide/Light steps.

#### Problem-aware but lower commitment

Signal examples:

- strong choking concern but reluctance about a four-hour broader course;
- partner-hosted educational context;
- wants first practical exposure before committing further.

Route:

**Light when operationally available and appropriate.**

#### Self-education/reference preference

Signal examples:

- wants a concrete choking resource at home;
- cannot attend now;
- is willing to make a paid first transaction but not a live commitment.

Route:

**Guide.**

#### Not ready / insufficient priority

Route:

**useful content + follow-up permission + wait.**

If later behavior shows stronger intent, accelerate. If repeated direct selling fails, backtrack to the actual awareness/timing problem rather than increasing pressure.

## 5. The Full Course offer — what is currently known

### Current commercial facts

- in-person;
- approximately 4 hours historically/currently, subject to final rebuilt delivery design;
- EUR 80 Single;
- EUR 120 Couple;
- current active architecture includes Guide(s) with the live offer;
- maximum contractual capacity 12 participants;
- attendance certificate only, not professional certification.

### Current rebuilt customer job

The flagship should prepare a parent/caregiver, at layperson level, to:

**prevent what is realistically preventable -> recognize when something is seriously wrong -> take the correct immediate first action -> activate appropriate professional help.**

Choking remains one of the deepest practical blocks; pediatric resuscitation receives meaningful practical introduction but not the depth intended for later BLSD Formalife.

### What is NOT yet authorized

Do not claim, unless separately evidenced/approved, that Formalife is:

- clinically superior to competitors;
- uniquely effective;
- proven to eliminate panic;
- proven to save lives;
- the best/most complete course;
- medically certified when the product is not;
- superior merely because it uses practice, mannequins, small groups or healthcare professionals.

Those category features are not defensible differentiation by themselves.

## 6. Upstream gates before the rebuilt Full Course is publicly sold

### Gate A — scientific/product validation

`FLAGSHIP_SCOPE_V1.md` requires scientific review of curriculum, wording, red flags, actions/prohibitions, age boundaries, CPR/AED content, anaphylaxis/device wording, poisoning instructions and claims.

Until this gate is closed, the rebuilt Full Course is architecture, not a fully launch-ready customer offer.

### Gate B — sellable operational difference

Formalife's current direction is parent-first consumer preparedness rather than certification-led training. The course architecture also uses a focused choking wedge, durable Guide/reference layer and a broader practical emergency flagship.

These are real architecture choices, but they are **not yet proof of a durable market position**.

Before cold acquisition scale, Formalife must be able to answer with evidence:

**why should the economically desirable parent choose Formalife instead of a credible local/free/institutional alternative?**

The first trusted-distribution tests should therefore collect not only conversion, but also the buyer's stated reason for choosing Formalife and what they perceived as different.

If customers repeatedly answer only "because my pediatrician told me" or "it was nearby/convenient" with no Formalife-specific value, trust transfer may still be commercially useful but the brand-positioning problem remains open.

## 7. Proof architecture

Proof must be attached to the claim/risk it is supposed to reduce.

### Proof job 1 — "Is this credible and scientifically responsible?"

Use only verified/authorized:

- visible scientific responsibility;
- accurate professional bios/credentials;
- current guideline/reference governance;
- transparent distinction between consumer education and certification.

Current credential wording still requires documentary reconciliation before aggressive use.

### Proof job 2 — "Is this really practical, not a passive lecture?"

Use:

- authentic class/practice photography or video;
- visible supervised practice/repetition;
- clear explanation of what participants actually do during the live experience.

Do not convert "we use mannequins" into a superiority claim; it is proof of the delivery format, not differentiation by itself.

### Proof job 3 — "Is this relevant to a parent like me?"

Use real parent/caregiver testimonials selected for:

- choking/weaning relevance;
- similarity of concern/context;
- specificity about the experience and decision;
- credibility and verifiability.

Generic five-star praise is secondary proof, not the whole proof system.

### Proof job 4 — "Will the broader Full Course be worth moving beyond choking?"

Use:

- transparent curriculum boundary;
- explanation of the distinct job of Guide/Light versus Full;
- product demonstration/experience where appropriate;
- customer proof only after the rebuilt course has actually been delivered.

Do not use historical choking-course testimonials as if they proved outcomes of a materially rebuilt flagship that customers have not yet experienced.

## 8. Offer and CTA rules

Every funnel asset must be designed backwards from one observable next action.

### Direct Full page/material

Primary CTA:

**book/select the next Full Course date.**

Must make visible:

- target/customer job;
- date/location/duration;
- what is included;
- Single/Couple prices;
- real capacity limit;
- cancellation/rescheduling/payment terms once standardized;
- proof appropriate to the claims;
- scientific/human authority;
- what the attendance certificate is and is not;
- why Full differs in job from Guide/Light;
- direct purchase path with minimal friction.

### Light page/material

Primary CTA:

**reserve the Light/hosted session.**

Must make clear:

- focused choking job;
- approximately two-hour format;
- current pilot price architecture;
- Guide inclusion;
- what supervised practice means;
- what the Light does not attempt to cover;
- Training Credit conditions once standardized;
- next available broader Full Course without implying Light was incomplete.

### Guide page/material

Primary CTA:

**buy the Guide.**

Must make clear:

- focused choking/reference job;
- physical format and price;
- what it can and cannot replace versus supervised practice;
- delivery/shipping terms;
- Training Credit conditions once standardized;
- optional direct path to Full for people who are already ready.

### Urgency/scarcity

Use only real constraints:

- course date;
- maximum 12 participants;
- actual partner-event window;
- actual stock/fulfillment limit if one exists.

Do not create evergreen fake countdowns or arbitrary urgency.

### Guarantee/risk reversal

No new guarantee is defined in this architecture.

Before adding one, Formalife must identify the actual purchase risk and verify that the guarantee is legally, operationally and economically sustainable.

Training Credit is a bridge/ascension mechanism, not automatically a guarantee.

## 9. Training Credit — role and unresolved rules

Training Credit has one strategically useful job:

**reduce the feeling of paying twice when a customer moves from a legitimate focused entry product into the broader flagship.**

The system should preserve it only if the economics and customer understanding support it.

Before the next test, customer-facing rules must be standardized for at least:

- eligible purchase(s);
- credit amount;
- whether credits can stack;
- whether Single/Couple variants change eligibility;
- validity/expiry, if any;
- how the credit is redeemed;
- what happens if the customer already owns a Guide included in the Full Course;
- refund/cancellation interaction.

Do not invent these rules in copy or automation.

## 10. Follow-up and fallback architecture

A funnel without explicit handling of non-buyers is incomplete.

### Explicit inquiry / hot prospect

If a prospect asks a real purchase question or needs human help, assign a human-response owner and minimize avoidable delay.

The objective is not to pressure; it is to resolve the question and move to the appropriate next step while intent is current.

### Full Course page/checkout but no purchase

Where lawful and technically possible:

- remind;
- resolve FAQ/objections;
- reinforce the relevant proof;
- make the next course/date visible;
- offer human help when the decision requires clarification.

Fallback if the person is not ready:

- Light if the barrier is commitment/format and Light is operationally appropriate;
- Guide if the person wants focused self-education/reference;
- otherwise useful content/wait.

Do **not** automatically discount the Full Course merely because the prospect did not buy.

### Guide buyer

Post-purchase sequence should:

1. fulfill and onboard the Guide correctly;
2. explain how to use it as reference;
3. make supervised practice/broader preparedness visible without implying the Guide is inadequate;
4. explain Training Credit clearly;
5. offer the next appropriate Full Course when the customer is ready;
6. stop acquisition messages for the Guide once purchased.

### Light registrant/participant

Pre-event:

- confirmation;
- logistics;
- expectation setting;
- reminder/no-show prevention.

Post-event:

- complete the promised Light job;
- recap/reference;
- explain the distinct broader Full Course job;
- make Training Credit usable;
- offer next Full date;
- collect reason for acceptance/refusal when practical.

### Non-responder

Do not send the same direct offer forever.

Classify whether the problem is:

- timing;
- awareness;
- price/value perception;
- trust/proof;
- format/geography;
- non-fit.

Then either backtrack to useful education, route to another legitimate entry product or enter a waiting state.

## 11. Delivery and post-delivery

The commercial promise must survive the experience.

### Full Course delivery job

Delivery must confirm:

- parent-first relevance;
- scientific responsibility;
- substantial practical value;
- clear difference between broad preparedness and credential training;
- clear distinction between Full and later deep BLSD.

### Immediate post-delivery system

After a successful experience:

- capture completion/attendance;
- request specific feedback/testimonial at an appropriate moment;
- capture what the customer found most valuable and what made them choose Formalife;
- make referral/caregiver sharing easy where appropriate;
- identify whether a distinct next need already exists;
- otherwise route to `WAITING FOR TRIGGER` rather than forcing another paid offer.

## 12. Second purchase — designed but not forced

For V1, the important decision is that **the funnel does not end at the Full Course**.

Possible later states include:

- deeper resuscitation intent -> future C05 BLSD Formalife;
- another real caregiver lacks the skill -> relevant caregiver participation;
- later skill decay -> future refresh/reassessment;
- new lifecycle/exposure need -> relevant future vertical;
- no current paid need -> WAITING FOR TRIGGER.

None of these routes is assumed to convert until measured.

BLSD Formalife should not be inserted into the first proof test as a mandatory back-end before that product is actually built and economically validated.

## 13. V1 tracking model

Minimum customer/funnel fields, where lawful and useful:

- source;
- partner/campaign identifier;
- first observed demand/trigger;
- voluntary broad child life-stage signal if it changes routing;
- entry route: Guide / Light / Full direct;
- product purchased;
- amount paid;
- Training Credit eligibility/use;
- checkout started/completed where available;
- attendance/completion;
- Full Course upgrade from Guide/Light;
- time from first transaction to Full purchase;
- reason for choosing Formalife / perceived difference;
- reason for non-purchase when voluntarily known;
- referral/caregiver source;
- marketing/communication consent state.

Do not collect data merely because software allows it.

## 14. Economics and KPI stack

### Full Course operating gate already decided

Target:

- 10-12 paying participants;
- EUR 80 Single / EUR 120 Couple.

Using current normalized direct costs, estimated contribution before acquisition/overhead is currently modeled at:

- 10 participants: EUR 250-450;
- 12 participants: EUR 352-592.

These are not full profits because payment fees, travel/setup, administration, taxes, overhead and acquisition cost are excluded.

### Funnel KPIs by source and route

Measure:

- first-purchase count by source;
- first-purchase contribution by source;
- Full Course buyers by source;
- Guide -> Full attach rate;
- Light -> Full attach rate;
- direct Full conversion where measurable;
- time to Full purchase;
- no-show/cancellation/refund rate;
- complete acquisition cost, including media, materials, partner cost, follow-up, human time and tools where material;
- contribution after complete acquisition cost;
- payback period;
- partner-sourced economics;
- second-purchase/referral behavior when actually offered;
- capacity consumed per edition and per euro of contribution.

Do not invent universal target percentages for Guide -> Full or Light -> Full before Formalife has a baseline.

## 15. Recommended proof sequence — reduce variables

### Test 0 — close prerequisites

Before using the rebuilt Full Course as the core commercial offer:

1. complete scientific/product validation of the rebuilt curriculum;
2. standardize Training Credit terms;
3. reconcile customer-facing authority/credential wording;
4. verify checkout/payment/attribution for the tested path;
5. ensure the offer page/material contains an observable CTA and appropriate proof.

### Test 1 — simplest existing-value route

Test:

**trusted partner/referral -> Direct Full Course.**

Why first:

- closest to historical evidence;
- fewest new variables;
- fastest way to learn whether the rebuilt broader Full Course can fill at the target price;
- produces baseline conversion/economics before inserting front-end steps.

Primary gate:

- ability to reach the 10-12 paying-participant target at the current price architecture;
- positive and legible contribution after including the source-specific acquisition/delivery costs actually incurred;
- reliable attribution and reason-for-choice data.

If this route fails, do **not** respond by adding more funnel steps. Diagnose target, positioning/difference, offer, proof, partner execution and delivery economics first.

### Test 2 — Light as a front-end

Only after a direct route baseline exists, test:

**trusted partner -> Light -> Full Course.**

Question:

**does the Light create incremental Full buyers/contribution or access a segment that direct Full does not reach, after accounting for Light delivery cost and complexity?**

Keep Light as a core front-end only if it earns its role economically or strategically.

### Test 3 — Guide as a paid front-end

Test:

**relevant source -> Guide -> Training Credit/follow-up -> Full Course.**

Question:

**does the Guide acquire/qualify the right customers at acceptable economics and produce profitable movement into the broader relationship?**

Guide can remain a valuable publishing/reference product even if its Full Course attach rate is modest. Its role must be judged by total contribution, authority/referral value and customer quality, not one metric in isolation.

### Test 4 — paid/cold acquisition only after the engine is legible

Do not use cold media to hide unresolved positioning, offer or funnel defects.

When the trusted/direct engine works, paid acquisition can test active-intent demand first and only later broader latent demand that requires more education.

## 16. Failure conditions and corrective routing

### Failure A — trusted demand will not buy Full at target price

Do not build more products.

Investigate in order:

- target/timing;
- perceived problem priority;
- credible alternative;
- operational difference/positioning;
- offer/value/risk;
- proof;
- friction/checkout;
- partner endorsement quality.

### Failure B — Full fills but economics remain weak

Do not celebrate volume.

Recalculate:

- complete acquisition cost;
- normalized delivery cost;
- contribution per edition/customer;
- payback;
- capacity burden.

Then repair economics before scaling.

### Failure C — direct Full works but Light/Guide do not improve economics

Do not protect sunk costs.

Keep Guide/Light only in roles where they independently create customer value or profitable acquisition/distribution. They are assets, not sacred steps.

### Failure D — one pediatrician works but other partners do not

Do not call the channel scalable.

The pediatrician relationship remains evidence of trust transfer, not proof of a repeatable partner system.

### Failure E — customers buy only because of the referrer

Do not mistake borrowed trust for solved positioning.

Keep the channel if profitable, but continue solving why Formalife itself should be chosen when the endorsement is weaker or absent.

## 17. What not to build now

Do not build yet:

- a large lifecycle CRM;
- twelve course funnels;
- a membership;
- a broad digital library;
- a complex lead-scoring machine;
- cold paid acquisition at scale;
- a mandatory Guide -> Light -> Full -> BLSD staircase;
- fake scarcity/urgency;
- new medical/clinical claims before scientific approval;
- automated sequences whose routing, state and fallback have not been defined.

## 18. Immediate next decision block

After the architecture is accepted, the next work should be **Test 0**, not copywriting or ads.

The first operational checklist is therefore:

1. scientific validation status of rebuilt Full Course;
2. exact sellable Full Course bundle/components;
3. exact Training Credit rules;
4. verified public authority/credential wording;
5. working checkout/attribution;
6. proof inventory mapped to specific claims/objections;
7. partner/referral cohort for the first Direct Full test.

Only after those are resolved should Formalife build the customer-facing page, follow-up sequence and campaign materials for Test 1.
