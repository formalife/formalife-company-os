# Formalife Lifecycle State Timeline

Status: PROVISIONAL LIFECYCLE STATE ARCHITECTURE — founder review pending
Date: 2026-09-21

Purpose: translate the provisional Formalife course map and lifecycle paths into a trigger-based customer-state system from pregnancy through roughly the preschool years, without turning child age into a rigid marketing calendar.

Founder direction on 2026-09-21: proceed from the substantially sound 12-node map toward concrete multi-month/multi-year lifecycle journeys. The postmortem immediately preceding this document established an important constraint: the correct architecture is **need state + real trigger + appropriate offer + next state + evidence required**, not `age -> course -> course`.

`FORMALIFE_BUILD_SEQUENCE.md` remains authoritative for what Formalife builds first. This document defines the mature lifecycle logic and the minimum information that should eventually let Formalife recognize the right demand window.

## 1. The architecture contains three different graphs

The system must keep three layers separate.

### Graph A — need states

This is the customer's reality.

A need state exists when a parent/caregiver has a concrete safety responsibility, uncertainty, exposure or capability gap that is currently salient enough to justify attention.

Examples:

- a baby is about to arrive and safe sleep/home readiness becomes urgent;
- complementary feeding is about to start;
- the parent fears choking;
- the child starts crawling and the home risk environment changes;
- a pool/sea/villa creates meaningful water exposure;
- a new caregiver begins looking after the child;
- a parent who has seen CPR once wants much deeper practice.

Need states are upstream of products.

### Graph B — product/capability nodes

`COURSE_FUNNEL_GRAPH.md` currently proposes 12 substantive topic nodes, C01-C12.

Those nodes are candidate ways Formalife or a specialist partner can solve a need state. They are **not evidence that Formalife should build all twelve**, and they are not all equally core.

The current strategic distinction is:

- **Formalife core / strongest current direction:** C03 Choking & Disobstruction, C04 Pediatric Emergency Preparedness, C05 BLSD Formalife;
- **high-coherence adjacent verticals:** C01 Newborn Safety & Safe Sleep, C07 Sickness & Red Flags, C08 Home Safety, C09 Water Safety;
- **specialist / partnership-heavy candidates:** C02 Starting Solids, C10 Car Seat Safety, C11 Food Allergy & Anaphylaxis, C12 Child + Pet Safety;
- **separate credential lane:** C06 BLSD Certified.

This grouping is strategic synthesis, not a final ownership decision for every node.

### Graph C — commercial offers and funnels

A topic node can be monetized or delivered through different offer forms:

- free open utility/resource;
- high-intent lead magnet;
- book/Guide;
- Light/hosted session;
- digital standalone product;
- live practical course;
- private/family format;
- specialist/co-created product;
- bundle;
- refresh/reassessment;
- certified course where the job is credential acquisition.

Therefore the rule is:

**do not infer the next offer from the last product purchased. First identify the active need state; then choose the lowest-friction economically coherent offer for that state.**

## 2. Evidence discipline — candidate transitions are not observed conversion

The prior `LIFECYCLE_FUNNEL_PATHS.md` uses labels such as `Strong` and `Very strong` to describe causal fit. After postmortem, those labels must be interpreted only as **strategic plausibility when the trigger exists**, not as evidence of observed Formalife conversion.

No future edge should be treated as commercially proven until Formalife has actual data.

Use these evidence classes:

- **FORMALIFE EVIDENCE** — observed current/historical behavior or measured result in Formalife;
- **FOUNDER DECISION** — explicit current strategic choice;
- **EXTERNAL EVIDENCE** — recurring demand and/or independent product precedent outside Formalife;
- **CANDIDATE TRANSITION** — plausible causal bridge that must be tested;
- **CONDITIONAL TRANSITION** — relevant only when a subgroup-specific trigger exists;
- **RESULT** — measured Formalife outcome after a test.

At present, choking/weaning has materially more Formalife evidence than the other lifecycle branches. Most other node-to-node transitions remain candidate transitions.

## 3. Lifecycle state machine

The future commercial system should reason in states rather than newsletters or fixed drip sequences.

### S0 — UNKNOWN / GENERAL RELATIONSHIP

Formalife knows the person but does not know a currently relevant safety need.

Action: remain useful; do not force a paid recommendation.

### S1 — UPCOMING TRIGGER

There is a credible upcoming transition or exposure, but the need may not yet be active.

Examples: solids are approaching; summer/pool exposure is coming; a car-seat transition is expected; a new baby is due.

Action: useful orientation, optional self-identification, low-friction content. Avoid false urgency.

### S2 — TRIGGER ACTIVE / NEED SALIENT

The customer explicitly experiences or declares the problem.

Action: route directly to the relevant useful resource or paid solution according to intent. High-intent customers should not be forced through unnecessary lead magnets.

### S3 — PRODUCT / INTERVENTION ACTIVE

The person has purchased or is completing the relevant product.

Action: deliver the promised job completely. Do not deliberately leave a gap to manufacture the next sale.

### S4 — NEED SERVED / ASSESS NEXT STATE

The immediate job has been completed.

Action: determine whether a distinct depth need already exists, another caregiver has the same gap, or the customer should simply wait.

### S5 — WAITING FOR TRIGGER

No additional paid need is currently active.

This is a normal and important state, not a failure state.

Action: maintain useful relationship and permission; do not promote unrelated courses simply because time has passed.

### S6 — REACTIVATED BY NEW TRIGGER

A life-stage, exposure, role, specialist need or competence-decay signal creates a new need state.

Action: return to S2 with a newly relevant problem and offer.

### S7 — REFRESH / REASSESSMENT NEED

A previously learned practical skill may require renewed practice, confidence or updated guidance.

Action: offer an appropriate refresh/reassessment format only when the scientific/product rationale is defined.

## 4. Trigger taxonomy

Formalife should recognize six trigger families.

1. **development/life-stage trigger** — baby arrives, solids begin, mobility increases, child grows into a new restraint stage;
2. **environment/exposure trigger** — pool, sea, holiday villa, new home, pet interaction, new outside-care setting;
3. **problem/event trigger** — illness uncertainty, choking fear, a near miss, explicit safety question;
4. **capability-depth trigger** — customer explicitly wants substantially more competence than a prior broad product provided;
5. **care-network trigger** — another parent, grandparent, babysitter, nanny or caregiver becomes responsible for the same child;
6. **skill-decay/renewal trigger** — time, loss of confidence, changed guidance or renewed exposure creates a reason to practice again.

The trigger must be more important than the mere passage of calendar time.

## 5. Lifecycle timeline — windows are orientation, not automation rules

The following windows describe likely clusters of need. They are not medical schedules and must not be converted mechanically into age-based sales automations.

### Window A — pregnancy / preparing for arrival

**Typical natural triggers**

- preparing sleeping environment;
- buying/installing the first child restraint;
- thinking about emergency readiness for the first time;
- another caregiver or pet will share the home with the new baby.

**Possible need states**

- N01 — `Is the newborn sleep/home environment actually safe?`
- N02 — `Is the car seat appropriate and correctly used?`
- N03 — `If something serious happens to the baby, would I know what to do?`
- N04 — `How do we safely prepare a dog/pet for the baby's arrival?`

**Candidate product routes**

- N01 -> C01 Newborn Safety & Safe Sleep;
- N02 -> C10 Car Seat Safety / specialist route;
- N03 -> direct C04 Pediatric Emergency Preparedness when broad emergency intent is already high;
- N04 -> C12 Child + Pet Safety / specialist route.

**Candidate free/entry roles**

- evidence-based newborn-safety reference;
- safe-sleep/home-readiness checklist or tool;
- car-seat selection/fit orientation where specialist capability exists;
- choking/emergency preparedness content for high-intent families.

**Do not assume**

- that every pregnant customer wants C01;
- that C10/C12 deserve Formalife-owned products;
- that pregnancy automatically creates high willingness to pay for safety education.

**Evidence required before scale**

- Italian demand and willingness-to-pay by route;
- conversion from trusted perinatal/healthcare distribution;
- product differentiation versus high-quality free guidance;
- specialist-partnership economics for C10/C12.

### Window B — newborn / first months

**Typical natural triggers**

- actual sleep/environment questions replace abstract prenatal preparation;
- first episodes of fever, breathing concerns or uncertainty about when to call for help;
- caregiver network expands to grandparents/babysitters;
- parent recognizes a broader emergency-preparedness gap.

**Possible need states**

- N05 — `I need confidence that our newborn safety setup and routines are safe.`
- N06 — `When my baby seems ill, how do I know what can wait and what cannot?`
- N07 — `I want broader emergency competence, not only newborn-environment guidance.`
- N08 — `Other caregivers need to follow the same critical safety rules.`

**Candidate product routes**

- N05 -> C01;
- N06 -> C07 Sickness & Red Flags;
- N07 -> C04;
- N08 -> caregiver expansion into the relevant substantive node, not a separate meta-course.

**Waiting rule**

If C01 has solved the current problem and no new trigger exists, the correct commercial action may be S5 `WAITING FOR TRIGGER` until feeding, illness, mobility or another exposure becomes salient.

### Window C — complementary feeding / starting solids

This is currently the most strategically important lifecycle window because it is adjacent to Formalife's strongest observed wedge.

**Typical natural triggers**

- family is preparing to start complementary feeding;
- fear of choking becomes concrete;
- parent wants guidance on textures/cuts/gagging/self-feeding;
- another caregiver will feed the child.

**Possible need states**

- N09 — `How do I introduce food safely and correctly?`
- N10 — `If my child chokes, would I know exactly what to do?`
- N11 — `Who else feeds the child and lacks this competence?`
- N12 — specialist allergy/anaphylaxis need only if a real specific trigger exists.

**Candidate product/offer routes**

- N09 -> C02 Starting Solids, preferably specialist/partner-led;
- N10 -> current choking-specific assets: Guide, Light and/or the C03 Choking capability;
- high broad-emergency intent -> direct C04 Full Course;
- N11 -> relevant caregiver receives C03/C04 according to actual gap;
- N12 -> C11 specialist route only when truly relevant.

**Important product-design constraint**

C02, C03 and C04 must not become three overlapping versions of the same choking content.

The jobs must remain distinct:

- C02 = feed safely;
- C03 = master the focused choking/disobstruction job;
- C04 = become broadly prepared for serious pediatric emergencies.

If a partner-led C02 already teaches meaningful rescue, the incremental job and value of a separate C03 must be tested rather than assumed.

**Near-term Formalife evidence opportunity**

This is the first place to measure a real bridge:

**choking-specific acquisition asset / Light / Guide -> broader Full Course**.

Do not assume the attach rate. Measure it.

### Window D — mobility changes the home

**Typical natural triggers**

- rolling/crawling/pulling to stand/walking;
- child reaches previously inaccessible objects/rooms;
- family moves or spends substantial time in another home;
- pet-child interaction changes because the child becomes mobile.

**Possible need states**

- N13 — `What risks in the home am I not seeing?`
- N14 — `How do I manage pet + mobile child safely?`
- N15 — `If prevention fails, do I know how to respond?`

**Candidate routes**

- N13 -> C08 Home Safety & Babyproofing;
- N14 -> C12 specialist route;
- N15 -> C04 if broader emergency action becomes desired.

**Candidate free/utility layer**

- room-by-room safety audit;
- product/recall reference;
- medication/poison-storage checklist;
- visiting/holiday-home adaptation resource.

These utilities may be stronger recurring relationship assets than additional micro-courses.

### Window E — expanding environments / toddler years

The child's world broadens. The relevant branch is selected by actual exposure, not age alone.

**Typical natural triggers**

- pool, beach, boat, holiday villa or recurring water environment;
- car-seat fit/transition question;
- nursery/daycare or more external caregivers;
- recurrent illness uncertainty;
- new travel/home context.

**Possible need states**

- N16 — `We now have meaningful water exposure; how do we prevent and respond to drowning risk?`
- N17 — `The restraint/seat setup has changed; is it still correct?`
- N18 — `More people care for the child; are critical safety skills/rules consistent?`
- N19 — `Illness and escalation questions are recurring.`

**Candidate routes**

- N16 -> C09 Water Safety;
- N17 -> C10 specialist route;
- N18 -> caregiver expansion into relevant C01/C03/C04/C11 rather than a generic caregiver course;
- N19 -> C07;
- if water/emergency consequence increases demand for action competence -> candidate C04/C05 route, to be tested.

**Seasonality rule**

Seasonality can make an existing need more salient, but Formalife should activate the specific hazard (e.g. water) rather than create a generic seasonal-safety product.

### Window F — preschool years / continued relationship

Formalife should not invent new products merely because the child becomes older.

Likely value comes from **reactivation of existing domains** when responsibilities change.

**Typical natural triggers**

- new car-seat stage or mobility context;
- repeated water seasons/exposure;
- new school/nursery/caregiver network;
- reduced confidence in previously learned practical skills;
- new sibling restarts newborn/feeding lifecycle;
- new home, travel or environment creates a new prevention problem.

**Possible routes**

- C10 reactivation when restraint needs change;
- C09 reactivation around meaningful water exposure;
- C03/C04/C05 refresh/reassessment if a valid skill-decay rule is established;
- caregiver replication when responsibility changes;
- new sibling -> re-enter C01/C02/choking lifecycle as relevant.

The target is a long relationship with repeated relevance, not a requirement that every year contain a paid transaction.

## 6. Cross-cutting emergency mastery spine

The emergency depth path can activate at many life stages.

### Need-state progression

E1 — `I fear one specific event, especially choking.`

Candidate solution: focused C03 / choking-specific offer.

E2 — `I want to be prepared across the main serious pediatric emergencies.`

Candidate solution: C04 Pediatric Emergency Preparedness.

E3 — `I want substantially deeper resuscitation competence and deliberate practice.`

Candidate solution: C05 BLSD Formalife.

E4 — `My practical confidence has decayed / I need to rehearse again.`

Candidate solution: refresh/reassessment overlay.

This is **not a mandatory staircase**.

C03 -> C04 and C04 -> C05 are candidate transitions until Formalife measures actual demand, conversion, contribution and timing.

The product scopes must make the distinction real enough that customers do not feel they are paying repeatedly for the same content.

## 7. Separate credential spine

C06 BLSD Certified starts from a different job:

**`I need a valid credential.`**

The default next state after successful certification may simply be completion/renewal later.

Entry into C04 or C05 should occur only if a separate consumer-learning job emerges:

- broader pediatric preparedness -> C04;
- deeper deliberate practice beyond credential requirements -> C05.

Do not treat possession of the customer's email address as evidence of consumer-course intent.

## 8. Caregiver role is independent of parent lifecycle

The system must not assume the entrant is a parent.

A grandparent, babysitter, nanny or other caregiver can enter directly because they have responsibility for a child.

Useful customer-state dimensions include:

- caregiver role;
- which safety responsibilities they actually hold;
- which substantive skills they have already learned;
- whether they are part of a family/customer already known to Formalife.

Example:

**grandparent begins caring for a weaning child -> choking need becomes active -> C03/appropriate choking offer**, even though the grandparent never passed through C01 or C02.

## 9. Next-best-action rule

Choose the next commercial action in this order:

1. explicit active problem or search intent;
2. trigger already declared by the customer;
3. imminent life-stage/exposure transition with clear relevance;
4. explicit request for greater depth after a completed product;
5. another caregiver now responsible for the child;
6. valid refresh/reassessment trigger;
7. useful non-promotional relationship content;
8. otherwise: **WAIT**.

The last option is deliberate. No offer is often better than an irrelevant offer.

## 10. Offer depth should match intent

### High active intent

If the person already wants the solution, route to the paid product directly.

Examples: explicit choking-course search, direct request for BLSD certification, car-seat consultation intent.

### Problem-aware but not product-ready

Use focused education, useful reference or a lower-friction first transaction when it genuinely helps the decision.

### Upcoming but inactive trigger

Use utility and orientation; do not pretend the need is already urgent.

### No trigger

Do not use generic catalog promotion as the default lifecycle mechanism.

## 11. Minimum future CRM state model

Collect only information that improves service/routing enough to justify holding it, and only where lawful and appropriately consented.

Minimum candidate fields:

- person/customer ID;
- caregiver role (parent, grandparent, babysitter/nanny, other caregiver where voluntarily provided);
- source / partner / campaign;
- products/offers purchased and completed;
- broad child/family life stage where voluntarily provided and useful;
- self-declared upcoming transition (e.g. starting solids);
- self-declared relevant non-sensitive exposure (e.g. pool/sea context, pet, seat transition) where useful;
- current need-state / trigger status;
- last practical training date for Formalife skills;
- known additional caregiver gap where the customer voluntarily identifies it;
- communication/consent state.

### Data-minimization rule

Do not collect precise child data or sensitive health information merely because it could improve cross-sell.

In particular, allergy/medical information may be sensitive. The system should prefer customer-initiated routing and the minimum information required to serve the request rather than building unnecessary clinical profiles.

## 12. What should be implemented in the first commercial engine

The mature lifecycle system belongs later in the build sequence, but the first engine should avoid destroying future lifecycle visibility.

For the current choking-led Guide/Light -> Full Course system, capture at minimum:

- source/partner;
- purchased offer/product;
- caregiver role where useful;
- the trigger/reason that brought the customer in, using a small controlled set plus free text when needed;
- broad relevant stage signal, especially whether starting-solids/weaning is active when voluntarily provided;
- purchase/completion history;
- consent state.

After delivery, test one or two lightweight questions rather than building a complex CRM:

- what other emergency/safety problem is currently most important to the customer, if any;
- whether another regular caregiver lacks the relevant skill.

This creates evidence for the graph without requiring the full lifecycle infrastructure now.

## 13. First tests and economics

The timeline is strategically useful only if it creates measurable additional contribution without excessive complexity.

### Test 1 — choking-led entry -> broader Full Course

Status: highest-priority candidate transition because Formalife already has choking/weaning evidence and current Guide/Light assets.

Measure:

- percentage of Guide/Light/choking-entry buyers who are offered the broader Full Course;
- acceptance rate;
- time to purchase;
- incremental contribution;
- reason for acceptance/refusal;
- whether the Full Course scope feels meaningfully distinct from the choking entry product.

### Test 2 — customer-state capture

Measure whether a minimal trigger question at purchase/delivery produces usable segmentation without harming conversion or creating operational burden.

### Test 3 — caregiver expansion

After a strong experience, identify whether another regular caregiver genuinely lacks the skill and measure uptake of an appropriate invitation.

### Later tests

Only after the products exist:

- C04 -> C05 depth conversion;
- C01 -> C02 lifecycle reactivation;
- mobility -> C08;
- water exposure -> C09;
- specialist partner paths C02/C10/C11/C12.

### Economic guardrail

Do not increase allowable CAC today on the basis of theoretical future lifecycle purchases.

Projected LTV becomes economically usable only as observed repeat purchase, contribution and payback data accumulate.

## 14. Metrics for the mature lifecycle engine

Track by entry need state and entry offer:

- first-purchase conversion;
- first-purchase contribution;
- second-purchase rate;
- time to second purchase;
- transition/edge conversion by actual trigger;
- contribution per family/customer over time;
- caregiver-expansion rate;
- refresh/reassessment uptake;
- partner-sourced lifecycle value;
- percentage of paid offers sent with a known relevant trigger;
- unsubscribe/complaint signals from lifecycle communication;
- operational cost/complexity per additional euro of contribution.

A high number of automated messages is not a lifecycle KPI.

## 15. What this timeline explicitly does not authorize

It does not authorize:

- building all 12 course nodes;
- treating candidate transitions as conversion facts;
- age-only automated selling;
- collecting unnecessary child/health data;
- all-to-all product recommendations;
- forcing every choking customer through C04 and C05;
- forcing specialist customers through the flagship;
- building a subscription before recurring value exists;
- using projected long-term LTV to justify acquisition economics today.

## 16. Current operating implication

The near-term job remains narrow:

**prove one choking-led acquisition -> broader flagship customer path end to end, while collecting just enough state/trigger information to learn whether the next lifecycle bridges are real.**

The mature lifecycle map should influence data design and follow-up now, but not product proliferation now.

## Revision conditions

Revise this architecture when:

- Formalife data contradicts a candidate transition;
- customers perceive two adjacent products as duplicated rather than distinct;
- a specialist partnership makes an owned node unnecessary;
- a trigger cannot be detected economically or lawfully;
- the incremental contribution from a path does not justify its operational complexity;
- scientific/product evidence requires a different timing or refresh rule.
