# Formalife Flagship Scope V1

Status: CURRENT — founder-approved strategic direction; curriculum V1 subject to scientific validation
Date: 2026-09-21

Purpose: define what belongs in the first Formalife Full Course and what should remain outside the live flagship. This is a product-architecture document, not a clinical protocol. Every clinical sequence, wording, threshold and practical exercise requires scientific review before customer use.

## 1. Founder decision now in force

The Formalife Full Course will **not** remain a choking-only course.

The flagship becomes a broader parent-first course in **pediatric safety and emergency preparedness**, while:

- choking / weaning remains the primary commercial wedge;
- the Guide remains focused on pediatric choking;
- the Light format remains focused on choking and first supervised practice;
- choking remains one of the deepest practical blocks inside the Full Course;
- pediatric resuscitation is introduced in the Full Course but receives its deeper proprietary treatment later in **BLSD Formalife non-certified**;
- certified BLSD remains a separate credential product.

Working internal descriptor only, not final product name:

**Formalife Full Course — Sicurezza ed Emergenze Pediatriche.**

## 2. Design rule

The flagship must not be designed as a catalogue of diseases or a competition over who lists the most topics.

A topic earns live-course time only when most of the following are true:

1. a parent/caregiver can materially affect the situation before professional help takes over;
2. recognition or first response is teachable to a layperson without turning the course into medical diagnosis;
3. the situation has high consequence, high parent salience or meaningful prevention value;
4. live teaching, demonstration, discussion or practice adds material value over simply reading/watching later;
5. the topic fits the Formalife territory of pediatric safety, prevention and emergency preparedness;
6. adding it does not materially reduce practice on higher-priority skills.

The live course has a fixed attention budget. **A new topic should normally displace another topic rather than simply extend the list.**

## 3. Core learning job

The Full Course should prepare a parent/caregiver to do four things reliably at a layperson level:

**prevent what is realistically preventable -> recognize when something is seriously wrong -> take the correct immediate first action -> activate appropriate professional help.**

The product is therefore broader than choking, but narrower than a general pediatric medicine course.

## 4. Live flagship core

### A. Recognize the emergency and escalate correctly

This is the common operating layer across all modules.

The participant should leave with a simple, scientifically reviewed framework for:

- scene / immediate safety;
- recognizing an unwell, injured or unresponsive child who needs urgent action;
- recognizing broad red flags without attempting diagnosis;
- understanding when to activate emergency services or seek urgent professional care;
- avoiding dangerous delay and common inappropriate actions.

This should be taught as a reusable decision framework, not as a long theoretical chapter.

### B. Choking and airway obstruction — HIGH DEPTH

This remains a signature practical block because it is the strongest observed customer trigger and already has Formalife assets/proof.

Scope, subject to scientific validation:

- prevention of choking hazards relevant to children;
- distinction between normal protective responses and true airway obstruction where appropriate;
- recognition of severity;
- what not to do;
- age-appropriate disobstruction response;
- supervised hands-on practice, correction and repetition;
- progression if the child becomes unresponsive, linked to the resuscitation block.

The objective is not merely awareness. This is one of the areas where live motor practice must remain substantial.

### C. Unresponsive child and pediatric resuscitation — HIGH DEPTH INTRODUCTION

The Full Course must contain a real practical introduction to pediatric basic resuscitation rather than only slides.

Scope, subject to current ERC/IRC guidance and Formalife scientific governance:

- recognizing unresponsiveness / abnormal breathing at a layperson level;
- activating help;
- basic pediatric CPR exposure and supervised practice;
- AED awareness/use concept where appropriate;
- connection between choking, drowning/respiratory events and resuscitation.

Boundary: the Full Course introduces and practices the essential sequence but does **not** attempt to reproduce the future proprietary **BLSD Formalife** product, which will be designed for much greater repetition, scenario work, AED depth, feedback, refresh and household sharing.

### D. Breathing and severe allergic emergencies — MEDIUM DEPTH

The parent should be able to recognize situations where breathing is becoming dangerous and know the immediate layperson response / escalation logic.

Core territory:

- respiratory distress / difficulty breathing;
- severe allergic reaction / anaphylaxis;
- use of any emergency device only within scientifically and legally appropriate layperson education and with validated wording.

The course should not attempt to teach differential diagnosis of respiratory illnesses.

### E. Seizures / febrile convulsions — MEDIUM DEPTH

This belongs in the flagship because it is a high-anxiety event where parent behaviour before professional assessment matters.

The live job is:

- recognize the event at a layperson level;
- know what to do and what not to do;
- understand escalation/red-flag logic.

General fever management is **not** the main live-course job and should not consume disproportionate classroom time.

### F. High-consequence injury and toxic exposure — CURATED, NOT EXHAUSTIVE

The flagship should provide a concise first-response framework for the highest-value injury/exposure situations rather than a complete first-aid syllabus.

Initial V1 territory:

- serious bleeding;
- burns/scalds;
- significant head injury / fall red flags;
- poisoning / dangerous ingestion;
- drowning/water incident as a prevention + emergency-response context, integrated where possible with the resuscitation sequence.

Each topic should focus on **first action, dangerous mistakes, escalation and prevention**, not detailed medical management.

## 5. Prevention is a thread, not a giant standalone module

Formalife should not spend a large section of the flagship on generic babyproofing theory.

Prevention should be attached to the emergencies where it has immediate meaning:

- choking -> food/object/environment risks;
- burns -> heat/liquid/environment risks;
- poisoning -> storage/access risks;
- falls/head injury -> age-stage/environment risks;
- drowning -> supervision/environment risks;
- allergic emergencies -> known-risk preparation where appropriate.

A future dedicated home-safety product can go much deeper without stealing live practice time from the flagship.

## 6. What should stay outside the live flagship V1

These topics may be valuable, but they should primarily live in digital/reference products or later specialist modules unless scientific review shows a compelling reason to promote them into the live core:

- routine fever management beyond red flags / seizure context;
- common childhood illnesses such as colds, gastroenteritis, ear infections and similar sickness-management topics;
- rashes and sepsis as standalone classroom chapters, beyond concise red-flag education;
- detailed fractures / limb injuries;
- bites, stings and envenomation as a full topic, except where relevant to severe allergy/anaphylaxis;
- detailed home babyproofing / room-by-room safety;
- medication dosing or treatment plans;
- detailed wound care;
- dental trauma;
- rare/specialist emergencies;
- deep BLSD/AED training;
- any content whose main job is diagnosis rather than safe layperson recognition and first response.

The long tail belongs to the future Formalife digital safety library, books/guides and specialist products.

## 7. Live-vs-digital architecture

The strongest international references do not require every topic to receive equal classroom time. CPR Kids, for example, concentrates live value on CPR/choking and places some lower-priority first-aid subjects online; Tiny Hearts combines a broad flagship with durable online/reference education.

Formalife should adopt the principle:

**use live time where physical practice, decision feedback and human clarification create the most value; use digital/editorial products for knowledge that benefits from replay, reference and updating.**

For the first implementation, Formalife does not need to build a large digital library before launching the revised flagship. The curriculum should simply be designed so lower-priority theory can progressively move into pre-course/post-course digital/reference layers without changing the core promise.

## 8. Attention allocation rule

The precise minutes require instructional and scientific design, but the live-course priority should be visible in the allocation:

- approximately **half or more of the meaningful teaching/practice time** should remain concentrated on choking + unresponsive child/resuscitation + integrated scenarios/practice;
- the remaining time should cover recognition/escalation and the curated emergency families above;
- Q&A and debrief should reinforce the decision framework rather than expand the syllabus opportunistically.

This prevents the broader scope from destroying the practical advantage of the course.

## 9. Offer-ladder consequence

The roles are now deliberately different:

**Guide** -> focused choking education/reference.

**Light** -> focused choking prevention + recognition + first supervised practice, especially useful for partner-hosted acquisition.

**Full Course** -> broader pediatric safety/emergency-preparedness flagship with deep choking and meaningful resuscitation introduction.

**BLSD Formalife non-certified** -> later intensive proprietary resuscitation learning product: repetition, scenarios, AED depth, individual correction, refresh and household-sharing logic.

**BLSD certified** -> separate credential path.

This gives each product a distinct customer job instead of selling the same choking content at three depths.

## 10. Strategic rationale

This structure keeps the market entry specific without teaching the market that Formalife only means choking.

It also makes the Full Course representative of the target company: a parent-first pediatric-safety business rather than a single-procedure course provider.

Layer 1 supports this architecture because market focus and product breadth are not the same thing, while excessive topic count increases operational variability and weakens execution. The flagship therefore uses **focused market entry + curated broader value + concentrated practical depth**.

## 11. Scientific gate before productization

Before the revised Full Course is sold publicly, Formalife must complete a scientific curriculum review covering at least:

- clinical correctness and current guideline alignment;
- wording of red flags and escalation;
- exact layperson actions / prohibitions;
- age boundaries and differences;
- resuscitation/AED content and relationship with ERC/IRC guidance;
- anaphylaxis / device-use wording;
- poisoning and emergency-number / poison-centre instructions;
- claims made about learning, confidence, preparedness or outcomes.

Current ERC Guidelines 2025 and the Italian adaptation/review by IRC are the appropriate current resuscitation reference layer, alongside other specialist guidance selected by Formalife's scientific direction.

## External benchmark references checked 2026-09-21

- Tiny Hearts — 4-hour Baby & Child First Aid: red flags, CPR/AED, choking, respiratory distress, fever/seizures, anaphylaxis, injuries, burns, poisoning and other emergency topics.
- CPR Kids — public parent course: CPR, choking, drowning, burns, fever, head injury, seizures, poisoning, allergy/anaphylaxis and red flags; some secondary topics are delivered online.
- Safe Beginnings — concentrated CPR/choking/injury-prevention model with reference material beyond the core course.
- European Resuscitation Council Guidelines 2025 and Italian Resuscitation Council 2025 translation/adaptation pages for current resuscitation guidance context.
