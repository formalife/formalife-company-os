# Funnel 01 — ACT 0 + ACT 6 Mirror Test Run-of-Show V1

Status: CURRENT PRODUCTION PROPOSAL — derived from founder-approved V5 architecture; exact clinical scenario wording/technique requires Dott.ssa Mafalda Camposarcone sign-off before customer delivery
Date: 2026-09-22

Purpose: convert the opening experiential baseline and final complex transfer test into one coherent signature learning mechanism that is impactful without humiliation, shows visible change without staging a fake perfect ending, and reinforces `FERMA -> VALUTA -> AGISCI` under increasing complexity.

Parent architecture:

- `FUNNEL_01_FULL_DELIVERY_SYSTEM_V5_CANONICAL.md`
- `FUNNEL_01_ACT3_CHOKING_RUN_OF_SHOW_V1.md`
- `FUNNEL_01_ACT4_CPR_QCPR_RUN_OF_SHOW_V1.md`
- `FUNNEL_01_ACT5_OTHER_EMERGENCIES_RUN_OF_SHOW_V1.md`

Current founder decisions governing this file:

- ACT 0 includes a short introduction, rapid participant calibration and an impactful experiential baseline;
- ACT 6 repeats the learning test with the same volunteer if willing, but the final scenario is **different and materially more complex**;
- the experience must be memorable and consequential but never humiliating;
- the final test must show both correction of the original gap and transfer to a harder situation;
- the result is immediate simulation evidence, not proof of future real-emergency competence.

Educational basis checked 2026-09-22:

- INACSL 2025 simulation standards: explicit prebriefing, purposeful scenario design, facilitation, professional integrity and planned debriefing;
- AHA 2025 resuscitation education science: scripted debriefing and feedback/debriefing can support consistency and learning; rapid-cycle deliberate practice supports repeated action-feedback cycles.

---

# 1. Signature mechanic

The experience is one arc:

**BASELINE -> LEARN -> APPLY -> COMPLEX TRANSFER -> DEBRIEF.**

The purpose is not to manufacture a dramatic marketing moment.

The purpose is to make the participant and the room experience the difference between:

**reacting from intuition / fragmented knowledge**

and

**recognising the state -> making a decision -> acting -> adapting when the state changes.**

The visual before/after is a product experience and a teaching tool.

---

# 2. Non-negotiable safety / integrity rules

1. Volunteer participation is explicit and voluntary.
2. Renew consent before ACT 6; consent at ACT 0 does not obligate final participation.
3. No ridicule, sarcasm, applause at mistakes or language such as `vedete cosa avrebbe fatto?`.
4. No surprise physical contact with a real person.
5. Physical manoeuvres are performed only on the appropriate trainer/manikin.
6. Scenario cues must be fair and based on content actually taught.
7. Do not script the volunteer into a perfect ending.
8. If the volunteer makes an error at the end, stop/correct/debrief constructively.
9. Do not publicly score the participant numerically.
10. Do not use ACT 0 vs ACT 6 as a clinical efficacy claim.
11. If photos/video are captured for proof, obtain separate explicit media permission; course participation/refund rights must not depend on media consent.

Facilitator framing:

**“Non è un esame e non ci interessa metterti in difficoltà. Ci serve una fotografia di come una persona normale reagisce prima di avere un sistema. Alla fine torneremo su una situazione diversa e vedremo cosa cambia.”**

# 4. ACT 0 — 00:00–00:02 Human opening

## Job

Establish human credibility and why the room exists without wasting prime attention on biographies.

Cover only:

- who the two instructors are;
- why Formalife teaches this course;
- what kind of change the participant should experience today.

Suggested direction:

**“Oggi non cercheremo di farvi memorizzare un manuale. L'obiettivo è arrivare alla fine sapendo riconoscere meglio quello che avete davanti, decidere la prima cosa utile e agire senza perdere tempo prezioso.”**

No company history.

No long credential recital.

---

# 5. ACT 0 — 00:02–00:05 Room calibration

## Job

Humanise the room, calibrate examples and obtain live market evidence.

Use short responses / hand raises only.

Suggested questions:

1. **“Quanti anni ha il bambino o i bambini di cui vi occupate?”**
2. **“Chi è in fase di svezzamento/alimentazione complementare o ci arriverà a breve?”**
3. **“Chi ha già fatto un corso di disostruzione o primo soccorso pediatrico?”**
4. **“In una frase: cosa vi ha portato qui oggi?”**

Do not run twelve mini-interviews.

Instructor notes only high-value signals that may change emphasis.

---

# 6. ACT 0 — 00:05–00:06 Simulation prebrief

Choose one willing volunteer.

Preferred profile:

- representative lay caregiver;
- no need to choose the person who appears least competent;
- prior training is noted because it changes interpretation of the baseline.

Explain:

- this is a simulation;
- the participant can stop at any time;
- no one expects a correct answer;
- physical action occurs on trainer/manikin only;
- facilitator will give only scenario information, not coaching.

## Equipment rule

### Current pilot / before pediatric choking trainer arrives

Use the **Act+Fast Red** as the temporary physical trainer and set the simulated casualty age/body size consistently with the equipment limitation.

Do not pretend it is an anatomically exact toddler simulator.

### Production target after child trainer acquisition

Use the child-specific choking trainer for the age range it supports.

---

# 7. ACT 0 — 00:06–00:08 Cold baseline scenario

## Scenario design job

Test only the core decision transition that the course will later teach deeply.

Do **not** include CPR, AED, multiple rescuers or another emergency at baseline.

### Scenario A0-B1 — simple choking transition

Setting:

**meal/snack context.**

Facilitator cue 1:

- child is conscious;
- suddenly coughs strongly and audibly;
- continues moving air;
- no coaching.

Allow ~5–8 seconds.

Facilitator then changes state:

- cough becomes absent/ineffective;
- almost no sound;
- cannot effectively breathe/speak/cry;
- visible distress.

Allow a short safe response window (~10–15 seconds) on the trainer.

Stop the scenario before it becomes a prolonged performance test.

## What the instructor silently observes

Do not display a score.

Internal baseline dimensions:

1. Does the participant recognise effective cough as a different state from severe obstruction?
2. Do they avoid immediately interfering with effective cough?
3. Do they notice the state change?
4. Do they move toward an appropriate severe-obstruction response?
5. Do they think about activating/assigning 112?
6. Do they perform any unsafe/prohibited action?

Record only enough to support debrief and product learning.

---

# 8. ACT 0 — 00:08–00:11 Descriptive debrief

Important: **do not teach the correct algorithm yet.**

The value of ACT 0 is the unresolved gap.

Use a short script:

1. **“Che cosa hai visto o sentito all'inizio?”**
2. **“In quale momento hai capito che la situazione era cambiata?”**
3. **“Qual è stato il punto in cui eri meno sicuro di cosa fare?”**

Then ask the room one neutral question:

**“Quanti di voi avrebbero avuto lo stesso dubbio?”**

Do not ask:

- `chi avrebbe fatto meglio?`;
- `chi sa la risposta?`;
- anything that turns the moment into status competition.

Facilitator closes:

**“Non correggiamo ancora la scena. La teniamo aperta e ci torniamo alla fine.”**

---

# 9. ACT 0 — 00:11–00:12 Open-loop transition

Bridge into ACT 1:

**“La domanda adesso non è se siamo stati bravi o meno. È capire quanto spesso situazioni come questa capitano, quali sono quelle che davvero meritano preparazione e come costruire una risposta semplice prima che serva.”**

Then ACT 1.

# 11. ACT 6 — 00:00–00:01 Transfer rules

No new teaching.

Show only:

**FERMA -> VALUTA -> AGISCI**

Tell the room:

**“Adesso non vi aggiungo contenuti. Vi do delle situazioni. Il lavoro è riconoscere cosa avete davanti, scegliere la prima azione e capire quando cambia lo stato.”**

---

# 12. ACT 6 — 00:01–00:10 Scenario carousel

Use **three** short visual/mental scenarios per edition.

Each takes ~3 minutes:

1. reveal the situation;
2. 5–10 seconds individual thinking;
3. ask one participant / pair for F-V-A response;
4. room confirms/corrects;
5. instructor gives only the key correction/anchor.

Do not turn these into new lectures.

## Scenario bank

Rotate cases across editions so the course does not become a memorised script.

### S1 — Button battery / ingestion

Child is initially well after suspected ingestion of a button battery.

Test:

- do not wait for symptoms;
- urgent pathway;
- no induced vomiting/home remedy improvisation.

### S2 — Head injury

Child falls, initially seems okay, then develops a material red flag.

Test:

- recognising change in urgency;
- escalation;
- not focusing only on the visible bump.

### S3 — Febrile seizure

Young child develops a convulsion with fever.

Test:

- time it;
- protect;
- nothing in mouth;
- escalation threshold / recovery.

### S4 — Anaphylaxis

Known allergy + rapid airway/breathing/circulation pattern.

Test:

- adrenaline when indicated/prescribed;
- 112;
- positioning;
- no delay for antihistamine.

### S5 — Drowning

Child removed from water, unresponsive and not breathing normally.

Test:

- rescuer safety;
- 5 rescue breaths;
- CPR sequence / 112 / AED retrieval.

### S6 — Burn

Hot-liquid scald.

Test:

- remove source;
- 20 minutes cool running water;
- no ice/creams;
- escalation by severity.

### S7 — Severe bleeding

Major external bleed with embedded object or strong flow.

Test:

- direct pressure;
- 112;
- do not remove embedded object.

### Edition-selection rule

Choose three cases that vary:

- mechanism;
- cognitive demand;
- `do` versus `do not do`;
- escalation route.

Avoid choosing three scenarios that are all CPR/choking variants because ACT 6 must test broader transfer too.

---

# 13. ACT 6 — 00:10–00:11 Mirror-test prebrief

Return to the ACT 0 volunteer **only if still willing**.

If they decline:

- choose another volunteer;
- do not frame the outcome as a within-person before/after comparison;
- keep the learning moment as a whole-room transfer test.

Tell the volunteer:

**“Questa volta la situazione è diversa e più complessa. Non cerchiamo la perfezione: vogliamo vedere se riesci a orientarti quando cambiano più cose insieme.”**

No advance disclosure of the scenario sequence.

---

# 14. ACT 6 — 00:11–00:17 Complex Mirror Test

## Design principle

The final scenario should be recognisably in the same learning family as ACT 0 but should add variables that require transfer rather than replay.

It should test:

- state recognition;
- choking response;
- delegation / 112;
- adaptation to deterioration;
- transition to CPR;
- AED integration if available;
- communication under pressure.

## Recommended V1 scenario — `Restaurant / two-caregiver deterioration`

### Starting state

Setting:

**restaurant / family meal.**

Casualty:

child >1 year within trainer-supported age range.

People present:

- volunteer = primary caregiver;
- second caregiver/bystander = played by instructor or another participant;
- phone is available;
- AED can be introduced later as an environmental resource.

### Stage 1 — effective cough

Child coughs strongly and audibly.

Expected orientation:

- recognise effective cough;
- do not immediately perform severe-obstruction manoeuvres;
- monitor / encourage cough as taught.

### Stage 2 — deterioration

Cough becomes ineffective/absent; child cannot effectively breathe/speak/cry.

Second caregiver says something emotionally plausible such as:

**“Che faccio?”**

Expected transfer:

- recognise state change;
- issue a clear instruction to activate 112 / get appropriate help;
- begin approved child severe-obstruction response;
- avoid unsafe improvisation.

Do **not** have the actor intentionally give misleading medical advice purely to trick the volunteer in V1. Complexity should come from the emergency, not a gotcha.

### Stage 3 — unresolved obstruction / unresponsive

After the planned manoeuvre cycle(s), facilitator announces:

**“Il corpo estraneo non esce. Il bambino perde coscienza e non risponde.”**

Transition to child QCPR manikin if needed.

Expected transfer:

- place/position safely;
- recognise entry into resuscitation pathway;
- begin approved pediatric sequence;
- **5 initial rescue breaths -> 30:2**;
- continue according to 112 instructions.

For timing, the facilitator may stop once the participant has correctly entered the compression cycle; the purpose is decision transfer, not another full QCPR drill.

### Stage 4 — AED arrives

Instructor/second caregiver brings AED trainer.

Expected transfer:

- recognise its role;
- power on / use according to prompts or delegate appropriately;
- avoid unnecessary CPR interruption;
- continue according to device/112.

Stop the scenario once the participant has demonstrated the decision sequence sufficiently for debrief.

# 15. What makes the final test genuinely harder

ACT 0 tests roughly:

**effective cough -> ineffective cough -> what do I do?**

ACT 6 adds:

1. different environment;
2. another caregiver requiring delegation;
3. explicit 112 responsibility;
4. persistent obstruction;
5. state transition to unresponsiveness;
6. transition from choking to CPR;
7. AED arrival;
8. need to maintain a coherent sequence under more variables.

Therefore the before/after contrast is not based on repeating the answer to the same test.

---

# 16. Internal observation checklist — not a public score

The facilitator uses a simple observation sheet.

## Baseline dimensions

- identifies effective cough state;
- avoids inappropriate intervention during effective cough;
- notices deterioration;
- initiates appropriate severe-obstruction response;
- considers 112;
- avoids unsafe action.

## Final-transfer dimensions

- identifies effective cough;
- recognises transition to ineffective cough;
- delegates/activates 112 appropriately;
- performs approved severe-obstruction sequence;
- recognises unresponsiveness / new state;
- enters pediatric CPR sequence;
- recalls 5 breaths + 30:2;
- integrates AED when available;
- communicates clearly with second caregiver;
- avoids unsafe/prohibited action.

Do not calculate a public percentage improvement because baseline and final scenario complexity differ.

For internal product learning, note:

- which decisions improved visibly;
- which errors remain;
- where facilitator cues were ambiguous;
- whether scenario complexity was appropriate;
- actual scenario duration.

# 17. ACT 6 — 00:17–00:22 Scripted debrief

The debrief should create the memorable contrast without congratulatory theatre.

Use three phases.

## 17.1 Describe

Ask volunteer:

**“Qual è stato il primo momento in cui hai capito che la situazione era cambiata?”**

**“Quando il bambino ha perso coscienza, cosa ti ha fatto cambiare algoritmo?”**

## 17.2 Compare

Facilitator explicitly reconnects ACT 0:

**“All'inizio avevamo una situazione più semplice e il punto difficile era capire quando la tosse non era più efficace e cosa fare dopo. Adesso hai dovuto riconoscere lo stesso cambio di stato, coordinare un'altra persona, far attivare i soccorsi e poi cambiare completamente risposta quando il bambino ha perso coscienza.”**

Name only observable differences.

Do not say:

- `adesso sapresti sicuramente salvare tuo figlio`;
- `sei diventato competente`;
- `la prova dimostra che il corso funziona clinicamente`.

## 17.3 Correct / transfer

If an error occurred:

**“Qui hai fatto X; il punto da correggere è Y. Rifacciamo solo quel passaggio una volta.”**

Use a brief rapid-cycle correction when useful.

Then ask:

**“Qual è la cosa che adesso ti sembra più chiara rispetto a quattro ore fa?”**

This may also produce useful qualitative product evidence, but do not pressure the participant into a testimonial.

---

# 18. ACT 6 — 00:22–00:25 Whole-room retrieval

Bring the whole room back in.

No slide initially.

Ask rapid prompts:

1. **“Tosse efficace: prima cosa?”**
2. **“Tosse inefficace: cosa cambia?”**
3. **“Perde coscienza: qual è la nuova sequenza?”**
4. **“Siete in due: cosa deve fare l'altra persona?”**
5. **“Il DAE arriva: cosa fate?”**

Then reveal one compact slide with the high-level anchors.

Bridge to ACT 7:

**“Il punto non è ricordare tutto senza mai ripassare. Il punto è avere un sistema chiaro oggi e continuare a tenerlo vivo dopo il corso.”**

This leads naturally into Pack / customer stream / refresh / Pass Sicurezza.

---

# 19. Slide / room design

ACT 0:

- minimal visual environment;
- no algorithm visible during baseline;
- ideally blank/neutral slide during scenario;
- no countdown timer visible to the volunteer.

ACT 6 scenario carousel:

- one photo/illustration + only the information the participant would realistically know;
- reveal state changes progressively where needed.

Final Mirror Test:

- no algorithm slide;
- trainers already positioned to avoid dead time;
- phone/AED/bystander roles physically preassigned;
- second instructor controls scenario cues and timing while first instructor observes.

Recommended instructor roles:

### Instructor A — facilitator / observer

- briefs volunteer;
- watches decisions/actions;
- stops scenario;
- conducts debrief.

### Instructor B — scenario controller

- provides casualty state cues;
- plays/coordinates second caregiver;
- introduces AED;
- transitions from choking trainer to QCPR manikin;
- protects timing and safety.

Do not make one instructor simultaneously act as casualty, judge, 112 operator, AED provider and debriefer if it can be avoided.

# 20. Rehearsal metrics

During internal rehearsal capture:

## ACT 0

- actual opening time;
- calibration overrun;
- volunteer-selection friction;
- baseline scenario duration;
- whether scenario cue changes are obvious but not leading;
- emotional tone / psychological safety.

## ACT 6

- time per mental scenario;
- whether three cases fit in 9 minutes;
- final scenario duration;
- trainer transition time;
- clarity of 112/bystander cue;
- AED arrival timing;
- debrief duration;
- whether final complexity overwhelms rather than tests transfer.

## Revision rule

If ACT 6 exceeds 25 minutes:

1. reduce verbal debrief redundancy;
2. reduce carousel to 2 scenarios for that edition if necessary;
3. simplify final-scenario environmental detail;
4. **do not** remove the state transition from choking -> unresponsive -> CPR, because that is the highest-value integration test.

# 22. Evidence / claim boundary

Permitted interpretation:

- the participant demonstrated different decisions/actions during a later, more complex simulation after training;
- the course deliberately includes transfer practice and debriefing.

Not permitted without stronger evidence:

- the participant is now clinically competent;
- the participant will perform the same way in a real emergency;
- the course has proven a quantitative improvement in emergency outcomes;
- one dramatic before/after volunteer is representative of all customers.

If used in marketing, the safest representation is the **design of the experience**, not an exaggerated efficacy claim.
