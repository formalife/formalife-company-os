# Funnel 01 — ACT 5 Other Emergencies Run-of-Show V1

Status: CURRENT PRODUCTION PROPOSAL — derived from founder-approved V5 architecture and current first-aid/pediatric evidence; exact medical/scientific wording, thresholds and escalation instructions require Dott.ssa Mafalda Camposarcone sign-off before customer delivery
Date: 2026-09-22

Purpose: convert ACT 5 `Altre Emergenze` into a rehearsable 48-minute live operating sequence that gives parents/caregivers clear first-action competence without pretending to teach full diagnosis or treatment for nine separate emergencies.

Parent architecture:

- `FUNNEL_01_FULL_DELIVERY_SYSTEM_V5_CANONICAL.md`
- `FUNNEL_01_MASTER_OPERATING_SYSTEM_V1.md`

Founder decisions governing this run-of-show:

- teach emergencies **one by one**, not through micro-cases;
- use the same stable grammar for every topic:
  **cos'è -> prevenzione -> riconoscimento -> cosa fare -> cosa NON fare -> escalation / 112 / urgent evaluation**;
- include all currently selected topics;
- if a topic cannot be taught responsibly inside the available time, move depth to Guide/Pack/post-course rather than compressing it into unsafe oversimplification.

---

# 1. ACT 5 outcome

At the end of ACT 5 a participant should not believe they can diagnose or independently manage nine medical emergencies.

The intended layperson outcome is narrower and more useful:

for each selected emergency, the participant should be better able to:

1. recognise the broad problem category;
2. remember one or two prevention principles where meaningful;
3. recognise the main danger/red-flag pattern;
4. take the appropriate immediate first action;
5. avoid one or two high-risk mistakes;
6. know whether the next step is **112**, urgent medical evaluation / Pronto Soccorso, Centro Antiveleni, or monitored follow-up under medical advice.

Canonical teaching principle:

**ACT 5 teaches first-action orientation, not mini-specialist medicine.**

---

# 2. Attention / pacing architecture

ACT 5 comes after the two deepest practical acts. It must therefore feel faster, clearer and more structured rather than becoming a 48-minute lecture.

Use one visual grammar throughout.

Every topic slide or slide-pair must preserve the same sequence:

1. **COS'È** — one sentence, not a pathology lecture;
2. **PREVIENI** — one or two useful prevention actions;
3. **RICONOSCI** — the signs that change urgency;
4. **FAI** — first action;
5. **NON FARE** — dangerous/common error;
6. **ESCALA** — 112 / PS / Centro Antiveleni / other approved route.

Each topic closes with one **ANCHOR LINE**: the single sentence the participant should remember if everything else fades.

No individual micro-case openings.

No open Q&A inside each topic. Questions that need more than a clarification are parked for the end / appropriate follow-up, otherwise the 48-minute contract collapses.

# 4. 00:00–00:02 — Scope / grammar reset

## Job

Tell the room exactly what this act is and is not.

Suggested operating direction:

**“Adesso cambiamo ritmo. Non cercheremo di trasformarvi in medici su nove argomenti diversi. Per ciascuna emergenza useremo sempre la stessa domanda: che cos'è, come la prevengo, come riconosco che è seria, cosa faccio subito, cosa non devo fare e quando chiedo aiuto.”**

Show the six-part grammar once and leave a small persistent visual marker in subsequent slides.

This is the attention aid: the topic changes, the cognitive structure does not.

# 5. 00:02–00:08 — Ingestione di corpo estraneo

Working duration: **6 min**.

## COS'È

Distinguish immediately:

- **inalazione / airway foreign body** -> already handled in ACT 3;
- **ingestione / gastrointestinal foreign body** -> a different problem.

Most ordinary small blunt objects may pass spontaneously, but several object classes carry materially higher risk.

## PREVIENI

Prioritise:

- button-battery compartments secured and inaccessible;
- batteries new/used kept out of reach;
- small magnets / magnetic toys controlled;
- sharp/small objects kept away from young children.

## RICONOSCI — high-risk object classes

Teach four categories visually:

1. **button battery**;
2. **multiple magnets or magnet + metallic object**;
3. **sharp/pointed object**;
4. **symptomatic ingestion / unknown high-risk object**.

Possible concerning symptoms include drooling, swallowing difficulty/pain, vomiting, chest/abdominal pain, blood, respiratory symptoms or significant distress. Absence of symptoms does **not** make a suspected button battery safe.

## FAI

Customer-facing direction after Mafalda review:

- suspected button battery -> **immediate hospital/urgent evaluation; do not wait for symptoms**;
- multiple magnets / magnet + metal / sharp object -> urgent medical evaluation;
- bring or photograph an identical object/package where useful;
- if uncertain about toxicity/material, use the appropriate medical/poison-information route rather than improvising.

For button batteries, European guidance treats oesophageal impaction as a time-critical emergency and severe injury can develop rapidly.

## NON FARE

- do not induce vomiting;
- do not wait for symptoms in suspected button-battery ingestion;
- do not invent food/drink/home-remedy instructions — follow the approved medical/CAV/PS pathway.

Do not teach honey as a generic public rule inside this Full unless Mafalda specifically adopts an Italian protocol with the exact eligibility conditions. The evidence exists in selected button-battery algorithms, but the conditional complexity can create dangerous delay/misuse if taught casually.

## ESCALA

Mafalda to approve exact Italian route/wording.

Core distinction to preserve:

**high-risk object or significant symptoms -> urgent evaluation now; button battery -> emergency mindset even if child initially looks well.**

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Non tutto ciò che viene ingerito è uguale: pila a bottone, magneti e oggetti appuntiti cambiano immediatamente il livello di urgenza.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- SIGENP current educational material on pediatric foreign-body ingestion and high-risk objects;
- ESPGHAN button-battery position paper / parent awareness material;
- Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù current/recent material.

# 6. 00:08–00:13 — Trauma cranico / caduta

Working duration: **5 min**.

## COS'È

A head impact ranges from minor injury to brain injury; external appearance or mechanism alone does not reliably determine severity.

## PREVIENI

Keep prevention short and age-relevant:

- changing-table / high-surface fall prevention;
- appropriate restraints in car / stroller / high chair;
- helmet where indicated for age/activity.

## RICONOSCI

Teach the major red flags rather than a long symptom catalogue:

- loss of consciousness / difficult to wake;
- repeated vomiting;
- seizure;
- unusual confusion/behaviour or neurological deficit;
- worsening or severe headache in an older child;
- major/high-energy mechanism;
- concerning infant signs such as marked abnormal behaviour or tense/bulging fontanelle only if Mafalda wants this retained.

## FAI

- if severe red flags / major mechanism / impaired consciousness -> 112 / urgent emergency evaluation;
- after apparently minor injury, monitor according to medical advice and seek evaluation when concerning symptoms appear;
- preserve history: mechanism, height/speed, time, symptoms, loss of consciousness, vomiting.

## NON FARE

- do not dismiss a significant head injury merely because the child initially looks better;
- do not unnecessarily move a child after major trauma if spinal injury is plausible, unless needed for immediate safety/resuscitation.

Do not teach rigid home-observation rules or “wake the child every X hours” unless Mafalda approves a specific evidence-based discharge-style protocol; those instructions depend on clinical assessment.

## ESCALA

112 / urgent PS for major neurological/red-flag pattern.

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Dopo un colpo alla testa non guardo solo il bernoccolo: guardo coscienza, comportamento, vomito, convulsioni e segni neurologici.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù trauma-cranico guidance;
- NHS head-injury red-flag guidance.

# 7. 00:13–00:18 — Convulsioni febbrili

Working duration: **5 min**.

## COS'È

Typical simple febrile seizures occur in young children, commonly between about 6 months and 5 years, are usually brief and can be extremely frightening without automatically implying epilepsy or permanent injury.

## PREVIENI

Key myth correction:

- antipyretics may improve comfort but **do not reliably prevent febrile seizures**.

Do not promise prevention through aggressive fever control.

## RICONOSCI

Typical features may include:

- loss/reduction of responsiveness;
- stiffening and/or generalised jerking;
- eye deviation/fixed gaze;
- post-event sleepiness.

Teach the parent to **time the seizure**.

## FAI

- protect the child from injury / remove dangerous nearby objects;
- protect the head;
- do not restrain;
- observe duration and features;
- place/recover on the side when appropriate and monitor breathing after the convulsion;
- use previously prescribed rescue medication only according to the child's established medical plan.

## NON FARE

- nothing in the mouth;
- do not force the mouth open;
- do not restrain movements;
- no oral medicines/fluids during the seizure;
- do not cold-sponge/undress aggressively as a strategy to stop/prevent the seizure.

## ESCALA

Working direction for Mafalda sign-off:

- 112 for seizure lasting >5 minutes, breathing difficulty/cyanosis, repeated seizure without adequate recovery, major focal features, prolonged altered consciousness or very unwell child;
- first-ever seizure requires prompt medical evaluation even if it stops.

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Cronometro, proteggo, non metto nulla in bocca e chiamo aiuto se dura troppo o il bambino non recupera bene.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù, updated 2024;
- NHS / current pediatric-hospital guidance using the >5-minute emergency threshold.

# 8. 00:18–00:23 — Avvelenamento / esposizione tossica

Working duration: **5 min**.

## COS'È

Poisoning can involve swallowing, inhalation, skin/eye exposure or medication dosing errors. Severity cannot be inferred safely from taste, smell, product familiarity or absence of early symptoms.

## PREVIENI

- medicines/chemicals locked and in original containers;
- no decanting into beverage bottles;
- dosing devices and weight-based medicine instructions used correctly;
- store household products outside sight/reach.

## RICONOSCI

Important trigger is often **the exposure itself**, not a symptom.

Emergency signs:

- altered consciousness/collapse;
- seizure;
- breathing difficulty;
- significant burns/caustic exposure;
- severe systemic symptoms.

## FAI

- ensure rescuer safety;
- identify substance/product if possible;
- keep packaging/name/amount/time available;
- for suspected exposure without immediate life threat, contact a recognised **Centro Antiveleni** promptly for substance-specific advice;
- if collapse/seizure/breathing problem -> 112 first.

ISS maintains a list of Italian 24-hour poison centres.

## NON FARE

- do not induce vomiting;
- do not give improvised “antidotes” or home remedies;
- do not automatically give milk/water/food unless specifically instructed for that substance by a qualified source;
- do not wait for symptoms before seeking expert advice after a potentially dangerous exposure.

## ESCALA

**life threat -> 112**

**suspected poisoning without life threat -> Centro Antiveleni / approved urgent pathway -> PS when advised/required.**

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Negli avvelenamenti non improvviso: identifico il prodotto e chiedo subito indicazioni specifiche.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- Istituto Superiore di Sanità — Centri Antiveleni;
- Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù;
- Poison Control first-aid guidance used only as supporting international evidence, not as Italian routing authority.

# 9. 00:23–00:28 — Ustione / scottatura

Working duration: **5 min**.

## COS'È

Thermal injury continues to damage tissue after contact ends; immediate cooling is a high-value first-aid action.

## PREVIENI

Keep high-yield:

- hot drinks/liquids away from table edges and children;
- bath-water / kitchen / iron/hair-tool control;
- secure electrical/chemical hazards.

## RICONOSCI

Do not teach parents to formally grade burn depth.

Urgency rises with:

- larger area;
- face/airway, hands, genital/perineal area, major joints;
- electrical/chemical mechanism;
- deep/white/charred appearance;
- breathing/airway concern;
- very young child or major associated trauma.

Exact escalation thresholds require Mafalda review.

## FAI

Core sequence:

1. stop/remove from burning source safely;
2. remove clothing/jewellery near the burn if not stuck;
3. cool under **cool running water for 20 minutes**, as soon as possible;
4. keep the rest of the child warm;
5. after cooling, cover loosely with appropriate clean/non-adherent material according to approved local advice;
6. obtain medical advice / urgent evaluation according to severity.

## NON FARE

- no ice / iced water;
- no butter, oil, toothpaste, creams or improvised products;
- do not pull off clothing stuck to skin;
- do not burst blisters.

## ESCALA

112 for severe/life-threatening context; urgent pediatric/PS evaluation for significant burns according to the approved Italian threshold.

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Ustione: fermo la fonte, acqua corrente fresca per 20 minuti, niente ghiaccio o creme.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- NHS/British Burn Association-aligned 20-minute cooling guidance;
- 2025 UK burn pathway;
- Bambino Gesù current pediatric accident guidance.

# 10. 00:28–00:34 — Anafilassi / reazione allergica grave

Working duration: **6 min**.

## COS'È

Anaphylaxis is a sudden severe systemic allergic reaction that can rapidly involve airway, breathing and/or circulation. Skin symptoms can occur but should not be required before considering severe anaphylaxis.

## PREVIENI

For known allergy:

- individual emergency/action plan;
- allergen avoidance appropriate to specialist advice;
- prescribed adrenaline auto-injectors immediately available and in date;
- caregivers know the specific device.

## RICONOSCI

Teach a simplified **A-B-C danger pattern** without turning this into the CPR mnemonic:

- **Airway:** throat/tongue swelling, hoarse voice, difficulty swallowing;
- **Breathing:** wheeze, noisy/difficult breathing, persistent severe respiratory symptoms;
- **Circulation/brain:** pallor, dizziness, collapse, marked sleepiness/confusion/floppiness.

May coexist with rash/hives, flushing, vomiting/abdominal symptoms.

## FAI

Working sequence for Mafalda sign-off:

1. if anaphylaxis is suspected and the child has the appropriate prescribed auto-injector / trained caregiver: **adrenaline IM auto-injector without delay**;
2. call 112 immediately and state suspected anaphylaxis;
3. position appropriately — generally lying down; if severe breathing difficulty, allow supported sitting as needed without sudden standing/walking;
4. if no improvement after **5 minutes**, use the second auto-injector if available/appropriate under the child's plan/current guideline;
5. monitor and start CPR if the child becomes unresponsive and is not breathing normally.

## NON FARE

- do not delay adrenaline while waiting to see if an antihistamine works;
- antihistamines/corticosteroids are not substitutes for adrenaline in life-threatening anaphylaxis;
- do not make the child stand/walk suddenly after suspected anaphylaxis.

Do not teach universal auto-injector hold-time mechanics because brands/devices differ; teach the actual device instructions where Formalife chooses to demonstrate a trainer.

## ESCALA

**suspected anaphylaxis = adrenaline when indicated + 112.**

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Nell'anafilassi l'errore pericoloso è aspettare: adrenalina quando indicata e 112 subito.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- ERC 2025 First Aid — anaphylaxis / second dose after 5 min;
- UK MHRA auto-injector safety guidance;
- Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù allergy/anaphylaxis material.

# 11. 00:34–00:39 — Grave difficoltà respiratoria

Working duration: **5 min**.

## COS'È

“Difficulty breathing” is a danger state, not one diagnosis. Causes may include asthma, infection/bronchiolitis, croup, allergy, airway disease and others.

The parent's job in this course is not to distinguish every diagnosis; it is to recognise **respiratory effort/failure risk**.

## PREVIENI

For children with known respiratory disease:

- follow the individual action plan;
- prescribed rescue medication/device available;
- caregivers know correct use.

General prevention detail belongs outside this 5-minute block.

## RICONOSCI

High-yield red flags:

- clearly increased work of breathing / retractions below ribs;
- grunting or marked noisy breathing;
- severe tachypnoea / gasping;
- unable to feed/speak/cry normally because of breathlessness;
- pale/blue/grey colour;
- exhaustion, marked drowsiness or altered responsiveness.

## FAI

- keep the child calm and in the position in which breathing is easiest;
- activate 112 for severe respiratory distress/red flags;
- if the child has a **prescribed** emergency medication/action plan (for example asthma), follow that plan while help is activated;
- monitor for deterioration to unresponsiveness / abnormal breathing -> ACT 4 pathway.

## NON FARE

- do not force a severely breathless child flat if that worsens breathing;
- do not delay 112 because a home device/medicine is being tried when severe red flags are present;
- do not use someone else's prescription medication.

## ESCALA

Severe work of breathing, cyanosis/pallor, exhaustion/altered consciousness -> **112**.

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Non devo conoscere la diagnosi per riconoscere che un bambino sta lavorando troppo per respirare.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù emergency respiratory guidance;
- NHS / pediatric-hospital respiratory-distress red flags.

# 12. 00:39–00:44 — Annegamento / incidente in acqua

Working duration: **5 min**.

## COS'È

Drowning is a respiratory impairment caused by submersion/immersion. It may be quiet and visually subtle rather than dramatic.

## PREVIENI

Use the four highest-value ERC-style messages:

- close/arm's-reach supervision of young children in/near water;
- safe/lifeguarded swimming areas;
- appropriate lifejacket/flotation on watercraft;
- water-safety/swimming education appropriate to age.

## RICONOSCI

Potential drowning may be silent.

Watch for:

- person not making effective progress;
- vertical bobbing;
- head tilted back / gasping;
- unusual silence or inability to respond normally;
- any respiratory symptoms after submersion.

## FAI

1. call/activate help;
2. **do not enter dangerous water if untrained** — use flotation/reach/throw rescue where possible;
3. safely remove from water;
4. if unresponsive and not breathing normally -> **5 rescue breaths then standard CPR**, directly retrieving ACT 4;
5. if breathing -> recovery/appropriate position, keep warm, seek urgent professional evaluation when symptoms/concerns exist.

Bambino Gesù current guidance supports immediate EMS activation in non-fatal drowning with respiratory impairment.

## NON FARE

- do not become the second victim by entering water beyond your ability/training;
- do not delay ventilation/CPR to perform rituals intended to “drain water”.

The latter wording requires Mafalda review before public use, but the educational principle is that resuscitation takes priority.

## ESCALA

Respiratory impairment, altered responsiveness or significant submersion event -> **112 / urgent professional evaluation** according to approved pathway.

## ANCHOR LINE

**“In acqua prima non divento la seconda vittima; fuori dall'acqua, se non respira normalmente, torno subito alle 5 ventilazioni e alla RCP.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- ERC 2025 First Aid — drowning algorithm;
- ERC 2025 Special Circumstances — ventilation-first rationale;
- Ospedale Pediatrico Bambino Gesù current accident guidance.

# 13. 00:44–00:48 — Emorragia grave / trauma maggiore — first response

Working duration: **4 min**.

## COS'È

The Full does not teach advanced trauma management. It teaches recognition and the first response to obvious life-threatening external bleeding.

## PREVIENI

No generic safety lecture. One line only:

**scene safety first — road, glass, machinery, violence, electricity or other hazards can injure the rescuer too.**

## RICONOSCI

Life-threatening pattern:

- heavy/uncontrolled bleeding;
- blood rapidly soaking material/clothing;
- gushing/continuous major flow;
- associated pallor, collapse, confusion, weakness or shock features.

## FAI

- expose/identify bleeding source if safely possible;
- apply **firm direct pressure** immediately;
- call 112 / delegate the call;
- maintain pressure until professional help takes over;
- keep child warm and monitor responsiveness/breathing;
- if an object is embedded, do not remove it — pressure around it.

## NON FARE

- do not repeatedly release pressure just to check the wound;
- do not remove an embedded object;
- do not spend time cleaning a catastrophic bleed before controlling it.

## Tourniquet boundary

ERC 2025 includes tourniquets as an option for life-threatening bleeding, but this Full does **not** have enough time to teach and practise correct pediatric tourniquet use.

Therefore V1 rule:

**do not add tourniquet technique to the customer skill promise unless Formalife allocates proper instruction/practice and Mafalda approves it.**

It may later belong in deeper trauma/BLSD/first-aid training.

## ESCALA

Life-threatening bleeding / major trauma -> **112 immediately**.

## ANCHOR LINE

**“Se il sangue è davvero importante, prima fermo il flusso con pressione diretta e faccio attivare il 112.”**

Evidence basis checked:

- ERC 2025 First Aid — life-threatening bleeding;
- British Red Cross pediatric severe-bleeding guidance.

# 14. Slide / visual design contract

ACT 5 should visually feel like one system, not nine unrelated PowerPoints.

Recommended V1:

- one persistent six-step header/icon strip;
- one main decision slide per topic;
- a second slide only when a high-risk distinction genuinely needs a visual.

Likely second-slide candidates:

- foreign-body ingestion: **button battery / magnets / sharp object**;
- head trauma: **red flags**;
- anaphylaxis: **A/B/C danger signs + adrenaline pathway**;
- drowning: **safe rescue + 5 breaths/CPR bridge**.

Avoid dense bullet lists projected as reference documents. The Family Emergency Pack / digital reference layer carries detail.

---

# 15. Attention / event-delivery rules

Even without micro-cases, ACT 5 should not become passive.

Use the following low-friction interactions:

- after each topic, ask the room to say the **one prohibited action** or **one escalation trigger** aloud;
- use hand raises only for binary misconception checks, not open discussion;
- vary visual mode: icon, photograph/diagram, red-flag list, simple action arrow;
- instructor changes physical position or speaker at selected transitions if useful;
- no single explanation stretch > ~5–6 minutes because the topic itself changes the learner's frame.

Do not add nine mini quizzes; the stable grammar and anchor lines provide the cognitive repetition.

# 16. ACT 5 rehearsal scorecard

For the internal full-course rehearsal record:

## Timing

For each topic:

- planned time;
- actual time;
- question overrun;
- transition time.

## Comprehension

Immediately after ACT 5, before scenarios, sample the room without slides:

- button battery: wait or urgent?;
- seizure: what must never go in the mouth?;
- poisoning: vomit or call for substance-specific advice?;
- burn: how long under cool running water?;
- anaphylaxis: what treatment should not be delayed?;
- respiratory distress: what signs make this 112-level?;
- drowning: what changes if not breathing normally?;
- severe bleeding: first physical action?

Do not score this as certification. Use misses to identify unclear teaching.

## Attention

Instructor notes:

- where the room visibly drops attention;
- which topics generate excessive questions;
- where slide density is too high;
- whether the sequence feels repetitive in a helpful or dull way.

## Revision rule

If ACT 5 exceeds 48 minutes consistently:

1. remove detail that belongs in Guide/Pack;
2. simplify slide density;
3. reduce low-consequence prevention explanation;
4. only then consider removing a topic.

Do **not** solve overrun by rushing the high-risk first-action / don't-do / escalation content.
