---

# 1. ACT 3 outcome

At the end of ACT 3 a participant should, at layperson level and subject to scientific validation, be better able to:

1. understand the main prevention principles relevant to choking;
2. understand enough airway mechanism to make the response intuitive;
3. distinguish the practical state of **effective cough / meaningful airflow** from **ineffective or absent cough / severe obstruction** while retaining the partial/complete-obstruction concept taught by Formalife;
4. know what **not** to do when coughing remains effective;
5. recognise when the situation requires the severe-obstruction sequence;
6. perform the approved child/adolescent and infant manoeuvres at the level practised in class;
7. know how 112 activation changes with one vs two rescuers / phone availability;
8. know the immediate next pathway when the obstruction resolves versus when the child becomes unresponsive;
9. understand that unresponsiveness creates the bridge into ACT 4 paediatric CPR.

ACT 3 does **not** claim mastery of every foreign-body-airway scenario or long-term emergency performance.

# 3. 00:00–00:05 — Prevention

## Job

Give enough preventive logic to reduce avoidable risk without duplicating the Guide.

## Learner mode

Listen -> visually compare -> answer one quick recognition question.

## Content

Founder direction:

- focus especially on eating/feeding context;
- shape;
- consistency;
- dimension/size;
- behavioural/environmental prevention where material.

Use highly visual examples rather than dense text.

## Slide job

Maximum 2–3 slides:

1. `PREVENIRE PRIMA DELLE MANOVRE`
2. visual contrast of safer vs riskier preparation examples;
3. optional single principle summary.

## Instructor rule

Do not turn this into a mini weaning course.

Transition line/job:

**“Per capire perché forma e consistenza contano, dobbiamo vedere per due minuti che cosa succede dentro le vie aeree.”**

---

# 4. 00:05–00:09 — Anatomy / physiology

## Job

Explain enough mechanism for later decisions to make sense.

## Learner mode

Observe visual -> point/identify path -> answer one contrast question.

## Core concepts

Subject to Mafalda review:

- airway path and obstruction location at useful layperson depth;
- why young children are particularly vulnerable;
- why effective cough is useful and should not be interrupted unnecessarily;
- why loss of effective airflow changes the response.

## Visual

One clean child-airway illustration / animation.

Avoid decorative anatomy labels that do not change action.

Transition:

**“Quindi la domanda pratica non è solo ‘c’è qualcosa in gola?’. È: l’aria sta ancora passando in modo efficace oppure no?”**

---

# 5. 00:09–00:14 — Recognition state

## Job

Create the primary decision fork used in the rest of ACT 3.

## Teaching structure

Retain Formalife concept:

- **ostruzione parziale**;
- **ostruzione totale/severa**.

Translate immediately into observable cues:

### Effective cough / partial obstruction pattern

- meaningful cough / sound;
- meaningful airflow;
- participant should understand the approved observe/encourage/no-unnecessary-manoeuvre logic.

### Ineffective/absent cough / severe obstruction pattern

- cough absent or ineffective;
- little/no meaningful sound or airflow;
- inability to vocalise/breathe effectively;
- this triggers the severe-obstruction pathway.

## Attention mechanic

Use 3 very short cue examples and ask the room:

**“Intervengo con le manovre oppure no?”**

No long discussion.

## Error to prevent

Participants performing back blows/thrusts simply because the child is coughing dramatically.

Transition:

**“Ora vediamo che cosa cambia sopra l’anno.”**

---

# 6. 00:14–00:23 — Child >1 year / adolescent

## Job

Teach the whole-room mental and physical sequence before split practice.

## 00:14–00:17 — Explain sequence

Cover, subject to Mafalda/ERC/IRC wording:

- effective cough: what to do / not do;
- severe/ineffective cough;
- approved back-blow / abdominal-thrust progression;
- reassessment between actions;
- stop conditions;
- 112 role;
- no blind finger sweep / other relevant prohibited actions.

Keep the 112 element visible rather than adding it after the physical technique.

## 00:17–00:19 — Full-speed demonstration

Instructor performs the approved sequence at realistic flow.

Rules:

- no pausing for explanations;
- participants first see what competent flow looks like;
- use appropriate trainer/equipment, not a participant's real body for forceful practice.

## 00:19–00:23 — Deconstruction

Repeat in slow motion.

Explain only action-changing points:

- position;
- support;
- where/why back blows;
- hand placement/direction for abdominal thrusts;
- reassessment;
- what changes if effective cough returns or consciousness is lost;
- 112 assignment.

Use visual arrows/hand-placement image if helpful.

---

# 7. 00:23–00:32 — Infant <1 year

## Job

Teach the whole-room infant sequence before split practice.

## 00:23–00:26 — Explain sequence

Cover, subject to Mafalda/ERC/IRC wording:

- effective vs ineffective cough;
- safe support/positioning;
- back blows;
- chest thrusts;
- no abdominal thrusts;
- no shaking / unsafe handling;
- reassessment;
- 112 logic;
- transition if unresponsive.

## 00:26–00:28 — Full-speed demonstration

Use Little Baby QCPR.

Where useful, the QCPR app's infant foreign-body-removal / audible-cry functionality may support feedback because Laerdal explicitly provides this feature.

Do not let the app become the learning objective.

## 00:28–00:32 — Deconstruction

Slow demonstration with emphasis on:

- head/body support;
- orientation and gravity;
- location/direction of back blows;
- location/direction of chest thrusts;
- safe turns between positions;
- reassessment.

# 8. 00:32–00:35 — Selected cases + practice briefing

## Selected cases

Keep only the founder-selected short edge cases:

- adolescent with obesity / body size preventing ordinary encirclement;
- rescuer who is pregnant, obese or otherwise unable to use standard abdominal-thrust positioning.

Exact instruction requires Mafalda sign-off.

Do not allow this section to expand beyond ~2 minutes.

## Practice briefing — ~1 min

Tell participants exactly how the lab works:

- two groups;
- two stations;
- each group rotates after 14 minutes;
- practice is for errors and correction;
- no embarrassment;
- instructor correction overrides peer observation;
- nobody should wait passively if they can observe against a checklist or rehearse the decision sequence.

---

# 9. 00:35–01:03 — Practical lab

## Structure

- **Group A — max 6:** child/adolescent station;
- **Group B — max 6:** infant station;
- 14 minutes each;
- rotate at minute 49 relative to ACT start;
- two instructors, one per station.

The current equipment inventory creates **asymmetric fidelity**. This is explicit below.

---

# 10. Infant station — 14-minute production choreography

## Equipment

- 2 x Laerdal Little Baby QCPR;
- 6 learners maximum;
- 3 learners per manikin;
- QCPR app optional for foreign-body-removal feedback / audible cry.

## Roles per triad

- rescuer;
- observer with micro-checklist;
- reset/112 role;
- rotate.

## 00:00–00:01 station reset

Instructor repeats only 3 critical cues.

No second lecture.

## 00:01–00:06 guided first attempts

Each learner performs a first short sequence.

Instructor priority:

- positioning/support;
- back-blow mechanics;
- chest-thrust mechanics;
- transitions.

Correct one critical issue at a time.

## 00:06–00:10 immediate corrected repetition

Each learner repeats after correction.

Target:

**every learner must receive at least one instructor correction or explicit confirmation of the most important observed point.**

## 00:10–00:12 variable-state micro-scenario

Instructor changes one variable:

- effective cough -> do not intervene with manoeuvres;
- cough becomes ineffective -> act;
- obstruction resolves -> stop/reassess;
- deterioration -> identify next state.

Participants should respond, not merely repeat mechanics.

## 00:12–00:14 no-prompt attempt + station debrief

Short final attempt without instructor narration.

Debrief question:

**“Qual è il segnale che ti fa cambiare strada?”**

Then rotate.

### Completion evidence

At minimum record during rehearsal whether all six learners actually achieved:

- one guided attempt;
- one corrected repeat;
- one state-recognition decision.

If not, 14 minutes is too short or equipment density is insufficient.

# 11. Child/adolescent station — current-equipment pilot choreography

## Equipment currently available

- 1 x Act+Fast Red — manufacturer age range 12+; supports back blows + abdominal thrust simulation;
- 2 x Little Junior QCPR — CPR devices, not manufacturer-specified choking trainers.

## Important boundary

With current equipment this station is **not yet equivalent to the infant production station for child 1–11 choking mechanics**.

The goal of the internal pilot is therefore to test timing/choreography while exposing the equipment gap, not to pretend the gap does not exist.

## Three concurrent jobs

While one learner uses Act+Fast Red for actual adolescent-sized mechanics, other learners should not stand in line.

Use:

### Job A — Act+Fast mechanics

- back blows;
- abdominal-thrust placement/direction;
- immediate instructor correction.

### Job B — Little Junior visual/sequence rehearsal

No forceful choking manoeuvres unless manufacturer documentation is later found to authorize them.

Use for:

- recognition cue;
- positioning discussion;
- verbal sequence;
- deterioration-to-unresponsive transition;
- assigning 112 role.

### Job C — observer / 112 decision role

Use micro-checklist:

- effective vs ineffective cough?;
- call/assign 112 when?;
- correct sequence?;
- stop/reassess when?;
- what if child becomes unresponsive?

Rotate roles quickly.

## Internal-rehearsal metric

Measure actual **forceful hands-on seconds per learner** on the Act+Fast Red.

If learners receive only a token attempt with no corrected repetition, the station fails the intended product standard.

# 12. Child station — preferred production configuration

Production target:

**2 child-proportioned choking trainers** for the six-person station.

Then mirror the infant choreography:

- 3 learners/device;
- guided attempt;
- immediate correction;
- corrected repeat;
- variable-state scenario;
- no-prompt attempt.

Current benchmark reference is Act+Fast Yellow (manufacturer ages 5–11) because it supports back blows and abdominal-thrust simulation. This is an equipment option, not yet a purchase decision.

Residual issue:

- 5–11 sizing does not exactly represent a toddler;
- instructor must explicitly teach body-size adaptation;
- Mafalda should validate how to explain the technique across the broader >1-year age range.

# 13. 01:03–01:12 — Rejoin / outcome / 112 / bridge to CPR

This is essential. Do not finish the practical stations and immediately call break.

The room needs one coherent ending to the choking story.

## 01:03–01:06 — If obstruction resolves

Subject to Mafalda/local-pathway validation:

- stop manoeuvres appropriately;
- reassess;
- explain required urgent medical follow-up / evaluation wording;
- identify symptoms that require immediate escalation where approved.

## 01:06–01:09 — If obstruction persists / child becomes unresponsive

Show the state transition visually:

**ineffective cough -> worsening hypoxia -> unresponsiveness -> begin paediatric CPR pathway.**

No fabricated percentage for progression to arrest.

## 01:09–01:11 — 112 recap

One final contrast slide:

### Two rescuers

one acts / one calls.

### Alone with mobile

speakerphone / act under approved current sequence.

### Loss of consciousness

transition immediately to the approved CPR pathway.

Exact wording requires Mafalda/IRC review.

## 01:11–01:12 — Narrative bridge

End on a question, not a summary:

**“E se, nonostante tutto questo, il bambino smette di rispondere e non respira normalmente: cosa fate nei primi secondi?”**

Then real break.

After break ACT 4 answers that question.

# 14. Slide architecture

Do not build 30 slides because there are many subpoints.

Recommended range:

**approximately 10–14 ACT 3 slides/visual states**, including:

1. prevention principles;
2. prevention visual contrasts;
3. airway mechanism;
4. partial/complete + effective/ineffective cough contrast;
5. child algorithm;
6. child 112 decision;
7. infant algorithm;
8. infant 112 decision;
9. selected special cases;
10. practice instructions / rotation;
11. resolution vs unresponsive fork;
12. one-rescuer / two-rescuer 112 recap;
13. transition to CPR.

Some may be animations/states of one slide rather than separate pages.

Slides support the instructor; they do not replace demonstration or practice.

# 15. Instructor roles

## Instructor 1

Suggested default:

- primary narrative lead for prevention/recognition/child explanation;
- child/adolescent practice station.

## Instructor 2 / Mafalda where present

Suggested default:

- infant explanation/demo or scientific clarification as assigned;
- infant practice station.

Do not make one instructor the permanent lecturer and the other a silent assistant. Both should have a defined teaching/coaching job.

Exact role assignment can vary, but the run-of-show must state who owns each transition before public delivery.

---

# 16. Micro-checklist for practice observers

Keep it intentionally short.

Observer should watch only for approved high-value cues such as:

- recognized effective vs ineffective cough correctly;
- used the correct age-specific branch;
- positioned the child/infant appropriately;
- performed approved manoeuvre direction/location;
- reassessed / stopped appropriately;
- remembered the 112 role;
- recognized deterioration to unresponsiveness.

Do not turn peers into examiners.

The instructor remains the correction authority.

# 17. Rehearsal scorecard

During the internal full rehearsal capture:

## Timing

- each segment planned vs actual;
- time lost to equipment setup;
- time lost to participant rotation;
- Q&A overrun.

## Practice density

Per station:

- active practice seconds / learner;
- corrected repetitions / learner;
- waiting seconds / learner;
- number of instructor corrections possible;
- whether all learners reached no-prompt attempt.

## Learning friction

- point where learners confuse effective/ineffective cough;
- point where 112 logic becomes unclear;
- child vs infant sequence confusion;
- body-positioning difficulties;
- special-case confusion.

## Experience

- energy before split;
- station energy;
- embarrassment/psychological-safety problems;
- equipment frustration;
- transition smoothness.

# 18. Pass / revise criteria for ACT 3 V1

ACT 3 is ready for first rebuilt commercial delivery only when:

1. Mafalda signs off the clinical sequence/wording;
2. actual timing remains inside the course budget without rushing;
3. infant station gives every participant meaningful corrected practice;
4. child station delivery standard is explicitly resolved — either suitable child-specific equipment or a scientifically/operationally approved alternative;
5. 112 logic is understood in rehearsal without repeated instructor rescue;
6. transitions do not create dead time;
7. practice remains psychologically safe;
8. no critical teaching job relies on a slide that participants cannot interpret quickly.
