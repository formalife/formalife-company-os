# Funnel 01 — Full Delivery System V2 Research Proposal

Status: RESEARCH-BASED PROPOSAL — replaces V1 as the current design proposal, but is NOT yet a founder-approved delivery decision and all clinical content still requires Dott.ssa Mafalda Camposarcone review before customer use
Date: 2026-09-22

Purpose: redesign the Formalife Full live course from zero using current competitor evidence, current resuscitation-education guidance, general learning science and Layer 1 operating principles.

This file does not change the founder-approved G2 positioning, G3 offer or G4 proof architecture. It changes the proposed way the live experience should be constructed and delivered.

Governing commercial direction remains:

**focused choking/weaning trigger -> broader parent-first preparedness system -> BEFORE -> LIVE -> AFTER / HOME REFERENCE + REFRESH.**

Core V2 design conclusion:

**the Full should not be built as a sequence of topic lectures. It should be built as an attention-managed, scenario-led sequence of repeated learning loops: situation -> prediction -> concise explanation/demonstration -> action/practice -> corrective feedback -> retrieval/debrief -> transfer.**

---

# 1. Why V1 needed revision

V1 correctly protected practice time but still organised the 240 meaningful minutes primarily as topic blocks. That creates four risks:

1. **coverage bias** — teaching many topics because they belong in the syllabus rather than because the live room is the best medium;
2. **attention decay** — long thematic blocks can become mini-lectures even when the overall course is labelled practical;
3. **cognitive overload** — novice parents receive multiple independent procedures and red flags without enough repeated decision structure;
4. **weak transfer** — participants may remember chapters rather than being able to decide what kind of situation they are in and what first action is required.

V2 therefore organises the experience by **decision state and learning cycle**, not by disease catalogue.

# 2. Competitor benchmark — what the strongest players actually do

Research checked 2026-09-22.

Observable current structure:

- public Baby & Child First Aid: approximately 4 hours;
- hands-on CPR/choking practice on manikins with instructor feedback;
- live Q&A;
- broad emergency coverage;
- 81-page handbook;
- 15 post-course resources;
- private 4-hour format explicitly includes a roughly 10-minute break midway.

Design logic inferred from the observable product:

- broad consumer promise can fit a four-hour flagship only because durable reference resources carry part of the information load;
- physical skills receive in-person practice/feedback;
- the course experience is not expected to be the only memory store after the event.

Source: https://tinyhearts.com/products/public-course and https://tinyhearts.com/products/private-courses

## CPR Kids — Australia

Observable current structure:

- approximately 3h15 public class;
- taught by paediatric nurses;
- hands-on CPR/choking;
- broad topics including drowning, burns, fever, head injury, seizures, poisoning, allergy/anaphylaxis and red flags;
- explicit Q&A time;
- lower-priority topics such as limb injuries and some bite/sting content are delivered online after/outside the class.

Design logic inferred:

- preserve scarce live time for the highest-value human/practical work;
- move some long-tail knowledge to asynchronous learning instead of extending classroom time;
- continue learning after the event.

Source: https://cprkids.com.au/our-classes/public-classes/

## Safe Beginnings — Canada

Observable current structure:

- approximately 2–2h15 consumer workshop concentrated on CPR, choking and injury prevention;
- broader 5-hour certification format adds bleeding, burns, allergies, fevers, seizures, head injury, heart attack/stroke/AED and other content;
- detailed digital resources are supplied after the workshop.

Design logic inferred:

- there is a real **scope-depth-time tradeoff**;
- an essentials workshop can stay short only by narrowing scope;
- expanding breadth and certification requires materially more time;
- reference content complements the live event.

Sources: https://safebeginnings.ca/infant-child-cpr-2-hours/ and https://safebeginnings.ca/register/

## Camillo Rescue Baby 2.0 — Italy

Observable current structure:

- repeated exercises;
- hybrid alternative with roughly two hours of theory online before in-person practice;
- free repeat within 12 months;
- additional video/manual/community resources.

Design logic inferred:

- separate cognitive explanation from motor rehearsal;
- use repetition and feedback to reduce passive learning;
- allow theory to move online when convenience or practice density matters;
- use retraining to compensate for skill decay.

Source: https://www.camillo.online/rescue-baby-21

- explicitly separate cognitive learning from psychomotor coaching when useful;
- face-to-face time is especially valuable for practice and feedback;
- interactive checks are used instead of passive content consumption alone.

- shorter Community Lifesaver product concentrates CPR/AED, choking, severe bleeding and seizures into ~3 hours.

Design logic inferred:

- live time is reserved for skills, interaction and high-value emergency decisions;
- knowledge-heavy long-tail topics can move out of the room;
- shorter products narrow the emergency set rather than pretending all topics receive equal depth.

# 3. Benchmark conclusion

The market does **not** reveal one universal best syllabus order.

It does reveal a consistent design pattern among stronger programs:

1. CPR/choking/emergency recognition carry disproportionate live importance;
2. hands-on practice and instructor feedback are central where motor skill matters;
3. broad secondary first-aid topics are often compressed, moved online or supported by reference material;
4. 4 hours is a validated consumer flagship duration, while 2–3 hours normally implies narrower depth/scope;
5. blended/flipped learning is common when the provider wants to protect hands-on time;
6. post-course references and repeat/refresh mechanisms reduce dependence on one massed learning event.

Formalife should therefore avoid competing by listing more topics. It should compete through **better instructional architecture around the parent decision job**.

# 4. Attention — correct interpretation

## There is no validated universal 10–15 minute attention cliff

The often-repeated claim that adults can only pay attention for 10–15 minutes is not supported as a hard biological threshold by the literature.

Reviews of the primary evidence conclude that the data do not justify a universal 10–15 minute lecture limit.

However:

- attention fluctuates during lectures;
- short lapses become common;
- changing pedagogy through questions, demonstrations and active participation reduces self-reported attention decline;
- instructor quality and engagement materially affect attention.

Therefore Formalife should **not** build a timer around a mythical fixed attention span.

It should build an **attention choreography**.

Sources:

- Bradbury, 2016/2017 review: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28145268/
- Bunce, Flens & Neiles, 2010: https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/ed100409p

## V2 attention design rules

### Rule A — no passive monologue longer than approximately 10–12 minutes

This is a design constraint, not a claim that attention dies at minute 13.

After a short explanation, change what the learner does:

- predict;
- answer;
- observe a demonstration;
- move/practice;
- teach back;
- decide a case;
- compare options;
- retrieve from memory.

### Rule B — change cognitive mode approximately every 8–15 minutes

The objective is to prevent long passive runs and repeatedly re-engage the learner.

### Rule C — complete a learning loop approximately every 25–35 minutes

A learning loop should produce an observable learner action, not merely more information.

### Rule D — use one real break and one brief reset

For a roughly 4h10 event:

- one ~10-minute break around the middle portion of the event;
- one ~3–5 minute stand/reset later if energy visibly drops.

Breaks are primarily fatigue/recovery management, not a magical attention reset.

### Rule E — movement is part of attention management

Practice stations, standing demonstrations and scenario work naturally interrupt sitting/passive load. Do not treat a 60-minute practical lab as equivalent to a 60-minute lecture.

# 5. Learning-science rules for the live product

## 5.1 Active learning over passive coverage

Large-scale evidence shows active learning outperforms traditional lecturing on learning outcomes in comparable educational settings.

Formalife implication:

**coverage is not the objective; learner action is.**

Every major concept must eventually produce a prediction, decision, demonstration, practice or retrieval event.

Reference: Freeman et al., 2014, PNAS.

## 5.2 Cognitive load — segment and scaffold

Novices cannot efficiently process many unrelated procedures at once.

Formalife implication:

- teach one common emergency decision model first;
- then apply it repeatedly;
- show a correct worked example/demonstration before asking for complex independent performance;
- progressively increase complexity;
- keep slides visually simple and remove nonessential detail from the room.

Reference: Sweller, van Merriënboer & Paas cognitive-load work and reviews.

## 5.3 Retrieval practice

Recalling information is itself a learning event, especially when corrective feedback follows.

Formalife implication:

- ask learners to retrieve the first action before re-explaining it;
- begin later blocks with a short recall from earlier blocks;
- finish with unaided retrieval rather than a final summary lecture only.

References: Rowland 2014 meta-analysis; Agarwal et al. 2021 classroom review.

## 5.4 Demonstration -> deliberate practice -> immediate feedback -> repeat

Current resuscitation education guidance emphasises feedback, deliberate practice and repeated skill work.

Formalife implication for choking/CPR:

- demonstrate a small skill unit;
- learner performs;
- instructor/feedback device corrects immediately;
- learner repeats the corrected movement;
- only then add scenario complexity.

If economically feasible, feedback-capable CPR manikins are a high-priority equipment improvement because current AHA/ERC guidance supports real-time CPR feedback during training.

## 5.5 Stepwise progression

Skills should progress from simple to integrated rather than from explanation directly to a complicated scenario.

Formalife sequence:

**recognise -> see -> do slowly -> correct -> repeat -> perform inside a scenario -> debrief.**

## 5.6 Scenario + structured debrief

Simulation research supports debriefing as an important component of simulation learning; recent evidence also emphasises a psychologically safe learning environment and facilitator expertise.

Formalife implication:

- scenarios are not theatre;
- each scenario has one or two learning objectives;
- debrief is concise: what did you notice? what did you decide? what was correct/incorrect? what will you do next time?
- no humiliation or fear-based correction.

## 5.7 Spaced learning / booster training

ERC/AHA education guidance supports spaced learning and booster training for skill retention.

Formalife already has a strong strategic asset here: free practical retraining within 12 months.

V2 should add light spaced retrieval between the original course and the refresh rather than leaving a 12-month vacuum.

## 5.8 Flipped/blended principle without overbuilding digital

ERC and major providers use flipped/blended learning so cognitive material can occur outside the classroom and in-person time can focus on active work.

Formalife does **not** need a three-hour eLearning course now.

It should use a very small pre-live layer and durable post-live references, then progressively move lower-value theory out of the room if real delivery data show this improves learning and experience.

# 6. V2 event-production principles

The course is both instruction and a live customer experience.

## 6.1 Start with relevance, not administration

Administrative/logistics information belongs before the event wherever possible.

The first live minutes should answer:

**“What kind of situation am I preparing for, and what will I be able to decide/do differently by the end?”**

Do not spend the highest-attention opening on long instructor bios or housekeeping.

## 6.2 Establish psychological safety

Participants must be allowed to be wrong during practice without embarrassment.

Operating language:

- practice is for correction;
- errors in simulation are useful;
- nobody is expected to arrive competent;
- clinical questions outside scope are redirected appropriately.

## 6.3 One recurring mental model

Do not teach eight unrelated emergency mini-protocols.

Use one reviewed orientation model repeatedly across the day, currently framed commercially as:

**PREVENT -> RECOGNIZE -> ACT -> ESCALATE.**

Exact clinical implementation remains subject to Mafalda review.

## 6.4 Teach through contrasts

High-value decisions often become memorable through contrast:

- gagging vs true choking;
- responsive vs unresponsive;
- breathing normally vs not normally;
- observe/seek advice vs emergency escalation;
- useful action vs common harmful action.

Only scientifically approved contrasts may be used.

## 6.5 Protect practice density

Waiting in line is not practice.

With up to 12 participants and two instructors, equipment/layout should minimise inactive time.

Target operating principle:

**during core skill labs, no learner should spend long periods watching other participants when they could be practising.**

Exact manikin ratio depends on available equipment; equipment quantity becomes a delivery-capacity variable.

## 6.6 Q&A is controlled, not allowed to hijack the agenda

Use three routes:

- answer now — within scope and useful to the room;
- parking lot/reference — useful but not worth consuming core practice time;
- individual medical question — redirect to appropriate medical pathway.

## 6.7 Transitions are designed

Every transition should tell the learner:

- what just happened;
- what remains the same;
- what changes in the next situation.

This reduces the feeling of a random topic list.

## 6.8 End with retrieval and action, not more content

The final minutes should force the participant to retrieve:

- how to orient themselves in an emergency;
- the first action in the most important learned states;
- where the Pack/reference/refresh fit.

Then the event ends with a concrete home-preparedness action, not another lecture slide.

# 7. Proposed V2 live architecture — 4h10 calendar block

Total calendar block: **250 minutes / 4h10**.

Includes:

- ~235 minutes learning/event activity;
- 10-minute real break;
- 5-minute active reset/transition.

The key change from V1 is that these are **acts and learning loops**, not chapter lectures.

## ACT 0 — OPEN / CALIBRATE

**00:00–00:10 — 10 min**

Job:

- immediate relevance;
- psychological contract;
- explain how the course works;
- baseline retrieval/prediction.

Suggested choreography:

1. 2–3 min: short realistic parent scenario/question;
2. 2 min: participants choose what they would do first;
3. 3 min: explain course job and boundaries;
4. 2–3 min: introduce the recurring decision architecture.

No long biographies. Authority can be demonstrated concisely and reinforced through the experience/materials.

---

## ACT 1 — THE EMERGENCY DECISION ENGINE

**00:10–00:30 — 20 min**

Job:

Build one common orientation layer before procedures.

This block should be scenario-led and cover, subject to scientific review:

- immediate safety/orientation;
- responsive vs unresponsive;
- normal vs concerning breathing at an appropriate lay level;
- when/how to activate professional help;
- role allocation when two caregivers are present;
- the principle of avoiding dangerous delay.

Attention choreography:

- micro-scenario;
- short explanation;
- pair decision;
- corrective debrief;
- second micro-scenario.

Outcome:

Participants acquire a reusable skeleton that will be retrieved repeatedly later.

---

## ACT 2 — CHOKING MASTERY LOOP

**00:30–01:35 — 65 min**

This is the primary signature practical act.

Proposed internal rhythm:

1. **5 min — trigger scenario / prediction**
2. **8 min — recognition contrasts**
3. **7 min — instructor demonstration**
4. **15 min — guided practice round 1**
5. **3 min — common-error feedback reset**
6. **15 min — guided practice round 2 / age-context variations**
7. **7 min — scenario application**
8. **5 min — unaided retrieval / teach-back**

Total: 65 min.

Principles:

- explanation never dominates the act;
- learner hands-on time is protected;
- correction occurs while the skill is still being performed;
- scenario comes after basic movement has been rehearsed;
- the learner must retrieve the decision sequence before leaving the act.

Exact maneuvers, age differences, recognition criteria and prohibited actions require Mafalda review.

---

## ACT 2B — RETRIEVAL BRIDGE

**01:35–01:45 — 10 min**

Job:

Connect choking to the next emergency state without beginning another long lecture.

Use:

- one scenario where the condition changes;
- participant prediction of what changes;
- concise debrief;
- explicit bridge to unresponsive/resuscitation work.

This converts the syllabus from “next chapter” into a causal progression.

---

## BREAK

**01:45–01:55 — 10 min**

Real break.

Do not fill it with compulsory video content.

The break occurs after a complete high-intensity learning arc and before the second major psychomotor act.

---

## ACT 3 — UNRESPONSIVE CHILD / CPR / AED MASTERY LOOP

**01:55–02:50 — 55 min**

Proposed internal rhythm:

1. **5 min — scenario / first-action prediction**
2. **8 min — recognition + sequence explanation**
3. **7 min — instructor demonstration**
4. **15 min — coached practice round 1**
5. **3 min — corrective feedback reset**
6. **12 min — repeat practice + AED/variation integration where appropriate**
7. **5 min — retrieval + debrief**

Total: 55 min.

Equipment principle:

If affordable/available, prioritise CPR manikins with objective feedback capability. Current AHA/ERC education guidance supports real-time CPR feedback for skill acquisition.

Boundary:

This is a meaningful parent-level practical introduction, not the future deep BLSD Formalife product and not a professional credential.

---

## ACTIVE RESET

**02:50–02:55 — 5 min**

Stand, water, equipment reset, one rapid retrieval question.

This is not a second formal break; it prevents the event from becoming a long sedentary second half.

---

## ACT 4 — FIRST-ACTION DECISION LAB

**02:55–03:40 — 45 min**

This is the largest structural departure from V1.

Do **not** teach secondary emergencies as seven mini-lectures.

Teach them as **decision cases** in four clusters.

Candidate clusters, subject to Mafalda review:

### Case family A — child is breathing but breathing is becoming dangerous

- respiratory distress;
- severe allergic reaction/anaphylaxis.

### Case family B — altered neurological state/event

- seizure/febrile convulsion;
- relevant head-injury red flags.

### Case family C — serious injury

- major bleeding;
- burns/scalds;
- fall/head injury first-action/escalation decisions.

### Case family D — exposure/environment

- poisoning/dangerous ingestion;
- drowning/water incident context.

Proposed rhythm:

- ~5 min explain lab rules;
- four ~8-minute case cycles = ~32 min;
- ~8 min synthesis/debrief.

Each case cycle:

1. scenario shown;
2. individual/pair chooses first action + escalation;
3. facilitator reveals/corrects;
4. one dangerous misconception is addressed;
5. move on.

The job is **first action + what not to do + escalation**, not complete medical management.

If a topic cannot be taught safely at this depth, move it to Pack/reference/customer education instead of stealing more live minutes.

---

## ACT 5 — INTEGRATED SCENARIO CAROUSEL

**03:40–04:00 — 20 min**

Job:

Force transfer across the course rather than ending with isolated chapter memory.

Use 2–3 short scenarios with different starting states.

Each scenario should require participants to:

- orient;
- classify the situation;
- choose first action;
- communicate/activate help;
- execute a practical action where appropriate;
- explain why.

Use concise structured debrief after each.

Do not introduce new content here.

---

## ACT 6 — RETRIEVAL CLOSE / HOUSEHOLD TRANSFER

**04:00–04:10 — 10 min**

No final lecture.

Suggested close:

1. **3 min — unaided retrieval:** participants write/say the core decision structure and first actions they most need to remember;
2. **2 min — correct the highest-value remaining errors;**
3. **2 min — household transfer:** decide who else needs to know where Pack/contacts/resources are;
4. **3 min — explain Guide, Pack, customer stream and free refresh as continuation of learning.**

The event ends by moving learning into the household and into spaced reinforcement.

---

# 8. Live-time allocation by educational job

Approximate V2 allocation excluding the 10-minute break and 5-minute reset:

- orientation/common decision framework: ~30 min;
- choking mastery + bridge: ~75 min;
- unresponsive/CPR/AED mastery: ~55 min;
- secondary emergency decision lab: ~45 min;
- integrated scenarios + retrieval close: ~30 min.

Thus roughly **160 minutes of the 235 learning minutes (~68%)** are concentrated on common decision logic + choking + unresponsive/CPR + integrated application rather than a long list of secondary conditions.

This is intentionally more concentrated than V1.

---

# 9. BEFORE — revised pre-live layer

V2 should use a small flipped layer, but not create a second course.

Recommended required consumption target:

**12–18 minutes.**

Three micro-components:

1. **orientation / expectations — ~4–5 min**
2. **foundational mental model / vocabulary needed to save live time — ~5–8 min**
3. **3–5 low-stakes prediction/knowledge questions — ~3–5 min**

Purpose:

- remove housekeeping;
- create initial familiarity;
- expose misconceptions before live work;
- prepare the participant to use the room for action.

The live course must still briefly reactivate the foundation because completion does not equal retention.

Do not place essential clinical safety solely in pre-work with no live reinforcement.

---

# 10. AFTER — spaced-learning layer

The current free practical refresh within 12 months becomes more valuable if supported by lightweight retrieval between events.

Recommended V2 learning reinforcement sequence — HYPOTHESIS:

- **24 hours:** 3-question recall + Pack/reference activation;
- **7 days:** one short choking/decision scenario;
- **30 days:** one mixed emergency decision scenario;
- **90 days:** one CPR/choking recall prompt + refresh entitlement reminder;
- **6 months:** short self-check + invite to choose a future refresh window;
- **within 12 months:** practical refresh session.

These are not sales emails disguised as learning.

They can coexist with the broader customer editorial stream, but the learning boosters have a distinct educational job.

This architecture follows current resuscitation-education emphasis on spaced learning/booster training.

# 11. Room and instructor choreography

## Room

Prefer a layout that can switch quickly between:

- attention to facilitator/demo;
- pairs/small groups;
- floor/manikin practice;
- scenario circle/debrief.

Avoid fixed classroom rows if they materially slow transition into practice.

## Two instructors

Recommended roles:

### Lead facilitator

- controls event rhythm;
- explains/scaffolds;
- protects scope and time;
- leads debrief.

### Skills coach / second facilitator

- observes learner performance;
- gives corrective feedback;
- manages second practice group/station;
- tracks recurring errors;
- supports scenario logistics.

Roles may swap by act.

## Practice ratio

With maximum 12 participants, split into two groups of up to 6 for major skill work.

Equipment ratio should be chosen to minimise waiting. If learners spend a substantial share of a skill lab waiting for a manikin, the course is violating the practice-density design even if the agenda says “hands-on”.

---

# 12. Slide/material rules

Slides are support, not the event.

Default rules:

- one decision/contrast per slide where possible;
- visual demonstration beats text description for movement;
- long reference lists belong in Pack/hub;
- no copying textbook paragraphs to screen;
- show algorithms only if they remain usable by a lay learner and survive scientific review;
- every major slide sequence should lead to learner action.

Use physical cards/manikins/scenario prompts whenever they improve learning without adding meaningless theatre.

---

# 13. Formative assessment without turning the Full into certification

The Full is not a professional credential, but “attendance only” does not mean instructors should have no idea whether people followed.

Use low-stakes formative checks:

- prediction questions;
- show-of-hands/choice cards;
- brief teach-back;
- instructor skill feedback;
- scenario decisions;
- end retrieval.

Purpose:

- expose misconceptions;
- guide correction;
- improve the product.

Do not market these as proof of real-world competence.

---

# 14. Psychological and emotional design

The topic naturally contains fear. Formalife should not need theatrical fear to keep attention.

Design principles:

- realistic stakes, not graphic shock;
- normalize uncertainty at the beginning;
- praise correct reasoning, not bravado;
- correct errors immediately but without embarrassment;
- distinguish “practice error” from moral failure;
- after emotionally intense scenarios, debrief and re-orient before adding new information.

The target emotional trajectory is:

**concern -> orientation -> active capability-building -> corrected practice -> clearer decisions -> preparedness**, not panic -> information overload -> temporary reassurance.

---

# 15. What should move out of the live room first if timing fails

If pilot editions consistently overrun, do **not** first cut choking/CPR practice.

Move or compress in this order:

1. background explanation that can be read/watched;
2. detailed prevention lists;
3. long-tail injury/illness detail;
4. optional Q&A;
5. secondary scenarios.

Protect:

- emergency decision framework;
- choking recognition/practice;
- unresponsive/CPR practice;
- corrective feedback;
- integrated retrieval/debrief.

---

# 16. V2 validation plan

This architecture is not “proven Formalife” until delivered.

For the first 3–5 rebuilt editions, record:

## Timing

- actual start/end of every act;
- overrun points;
- transition time;
- break length.

## Engagement / attention proxies

- visible passive waiting time;
- participation rate in prediction/retrieval;
- questions generated;
- where energy visibly drops;
- where instructions need repeating.

Do not pretend these are neurological attention measurements; they are operational signals.

## Practice density

- approximate minutes each participant actually practises choking;
- approximate minutes each participant actually practises CPR;
- equipment waiting bottlenecks;
- number/type of corrections required.

## Learning signals

- recurring misconceptions at opening;
- recurring errors during practice;
- end-of-course retrieval failures;
- scenario decision errors.

## Customer evidence

Ask specifically:

- where did the course feel too fast/slow?;
- which section felt like information overload?;
- which practical section needed more/less time?;
- did secondary emergency cases feel useful or superficial?;
- was the break placed at the right moment?;
- did the 4h10 block feel justified by the experience?;
- what did they remember unaided at close / follow-up?

## Economics / capacity

- instructor strain;
- room reset burden;
- equipment required;
- feasibility at 5, 8, 10 and 12 participants;
- whether 2-Caregiver mix changes station quality.

# 17. Founder/scientific decisions required before V2 becomes current

1. approve/reject the decision-state architecture vs topic-chapter architecture;
2. Mafalda validates whether the 45-minute First-Action Decision Lab can safely cover the proposed case families at the stated depth;
3. approve exact role of AED inside Full;
4. confirm available manikin/equipment inventory and feasible learner:manikin ratio;
5. decide whether 12–18 minute pre-work is mandatory or strongly recommended;
6. approve one 10-minute break at ~1h45 + 5-minute active reset later;
7. approve/modify spaced-learning booster sequence;
8. decide whether feedback-capable CPR manikins are current asset, near-term purchase or later upgrade.

---

# 18. Recommendation

Adopt V2 as the **pilot architecture** after Mafalda's clinical review.

The strategic reason is not that every competitor follows this exact order. They do not.

The reason is that V2 combines the strongest recurring design principles visible across the market and education evidence:

- 4-hour-class consumer viability;
- concentrated practical depth;
- active learning;
- feedback and repetition;
- cognitive load management;
- scenario-based transfer;
- durable reference;
- blended/pre-post support;
- spaced reinforcement;
- attention managed through changing learner activity rather than arbitrary lecture timers.

V2 should replace V1 only after founder + scientific approval; until then V1 remains historical/provisional and V2 is the current research-backed proposal.
