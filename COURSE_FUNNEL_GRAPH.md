# Formalife Course Funnel Graph

Status: PROVISIONAL STRATEGIC COURSE GRAPH — founder review pending
Date: 2026-09-21

Purpose: define the smallest coherent set of substantive course-topic nodes that can cover the Formalife pediatric-safety territory, based on observed parent problems plus international product precedent, and show how customers can enter through different needs without creating dozens of disconnected SKUs.

This is a strategic topic graph, not a build backlog and not final commercial naming. A node can later be delivered online, live, privately, through a specialist partner or in several formats without becoming a different topic merely because delivery changes.

`FORMALIFE_BUILD_SEQUENCE.md` continues to govern implementation order. `FORMALIFE_COMMERCIAL_ARCHITECTURE.md` governs the wider acquisition/retention system. Research support is in `PARENT_DEMAND_COURSE_RESEARCH.md`.

## 1. What changed from the previous 13-node hypothesis

The previous graph mixed three different things:

1. substantive customer problems large enough to support a course;
2. contexts such as travel;
3. system mechanics such as caregiver expansion and refresh.

Research against spontaneous parent questions and international operators supports a cleaner separation.

### Retained substantive nodes

- Starting Solids & Feeding Safety;
- Choking & Disobstruction;
- Pediatric Emergency Preparedness;
- BLSD Formalife — non-certified;
- BLSD Certified;
- Sickness & Red Flags;
- Home Safety & Babyproofing;
- Water Safety & Drowning Prevention.

### Retained but redefined

- `New Baby Safety` becomes **Newborn Safety & Safe Sleep** so the node is anchored to a clear early-life safety job rather than a loose checklist.
- `Car Seat & Road Safety` becomes **Car Seat Safety / Child Passenger Safety**. Generic road/pedestrian/bicycle safety is not part of this course job.

### Removed as autonomous course nodes

- `Travel, Outdoor & Seasonal Safety` -> contextual modules/resources routed mainly into Water, Car Seat and Home Safety;
- `Family & Caregiver Safety` -> cross-cutting caregiver-expansion mechanism;
- `Skills Refresh & Rehearsal` -> lifecycle/retraining layer attached to practical skill courses.

### New specialist nodes discovered

- **Food Allergy & Anaphylaxis Preparedness**;
- **Child + Pet Safety**.

The resulting proposal contains **12 substantive course-topic nodes**. That number is a research result, not a target quota.

## 2. Governing architecture

Formalife should not force every customer through one linear funnel.

The mature system is a graph with:

- a small **emergency capability spine**;
- multiple **problem/life-stage entry nodes**;
- a few **specialist depth nodes**;
- a separate **credential lane**;
- cross-cutting **caregiver, refresh and free-utility layers**.

The central emergency spine is:

**Choking or direct entry -> Pediatric Emergency Preparedness -> BLSD Formalife when deeper resuscitation competence is desired -> practical refresh over time.**

This is not a mandatory staircase. High-intent customers can enter directly at the relevant node, and specialist problems do not need to route artificially through the flagship.

No course should be deliberately incomplete to force the next purchase. A next step must solve a distinct additional job, add meaningful depth or become relevant because the child's life stage/risk environment changed.

## 3. Proposed course universe — 12 substantive nodes

### C01 — Newborn Safety & Safe Sleep

**Primary parent question**

"The baby is arriving / has just arrived: how do I make sleep and the first home environment genuinely safe, and how do I distinguish current safety guidance from random advice?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

Safe-sleep uncertainty is a repeated high-anxiety newborn problem with dedicated public education and standalone training internationally. It also gives Formalife a legitimate entry point before weaning without becoming a generic antenatal or sleep-coaching brand.

The node is deliberately broader than one safe-sleep checklist but narrower than a general newborn-care course.

**Scope**

- safe sleep / sleep environment and suffocation-risk reduction;
- products and sleep-space hazards;
- temperature/clothing and other safety-relevant sleep decisions within validated guidance;
- basic newborn home/environment readiness;
- medication/product-storage basics;
- caregiver consistency around newborn safety;
- orientation to other risks that deserve separate depth later, without duplicating those full courses.

Excluded: generic sleep training, breastfeeding, soothing, routines, developmental sleep coaching and broad newborn care.

**Useful courses before**

None.

**Natural next steps**

- C07 Sickness & Red Flags as illness questions emerge;
- C02 Starting Solids & Feeding Safety and C03 Choking when feeding transition begins;
- C08 Home Safety & Babyproofing when mobility approaches;
- C10 Car Seat Safety when seat selection/fit/transition requires depth;
- C04 Pediatric Emergency Preparedness for broader response competence.

**Entry-point strength**

High around pregnancy, discharge home and the first months. Strong life-stage timing but likely substantial free-information competition, so paid value must come from trusted synthesis, localization, decision support and practical implementation rather than merely repeating public guidance.

**International precedent**

NICHD/AAP maintain dedicated safe-sleep education; consumer safe-sleep courses exist; broader infant-safety courses commonly make safe sleep a major pillar.

**Funnel role**

Acquisition / early lifecycle.

**Build vs partner**

Formalife-owned only with explicit pediatric/scientific governance.

### C02 — Starting Solids & Feeding Safety

**Primary parent question**

"My baby is starting solids: how do I introduce food safely without being paralysed by choking, textures and allergens?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

Starting solids is a highly visible life-stage transition with a large body of specialist knowledge and established paid products. The customer job extends beyond emergency choking response into readiness, textures, self-feeding, food preparation, feeding environment and allergen introduction.

**Scope**

- readiness and transition into complementary feeding;
- textures/cuts and safe food presentation;
- feeding environment and safe self-feeding practices;
- gagging vs choking education where appropriate;
- safe introduction framework for common allergens within specialist competence;
- practical progression and reference resources.

**Useful courses before**

None mandatory.

**Natural next steps**

- C03 Choking & Disobstruction for focused response competence;
- C11 Food Allergy & Anaphylaxis Preparedness only when diagnosis/risk makes it relevant;
- C08 Home Safety as mobility increases.

**Entry-point strength**

Very high because the trigger is predictable, time-bounded and already strongly associated with choking fear.

**International precedent**

Solid Starts sells a standalone starting-solids course and a broader product ecosystem around the same problem.

**Funnel role**

Acquisition / lifecycle / partner-led horizontal expansion.

**Build vs partner**

Preferred **specialist partnership**. Svezzy remains the natural current candidate rather than Formalife recreating feeding competence internally.

---

### C03 — Choking & Disobstruction

**Primary parent question**

"I am afraid my child could choke and I would not know what to do."

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

The pain is immediate, specific, highly salient and already evidenced in Formalife's customer base. It also supports meaningful supervised motor-skill practice and applies beyond the starting-solids curriculum to objects, older children and additional caregivers.

**Scope**

- choking prevention relevant to the immediate context;
- recognition of effective cough / airway obstruction and gagging distinctions where scientifically appropriate;
- what not to do;
- disobstruction sequence;
- practical supervised rehearsal in live formats;
- concise home reference / refresh layer.

**Useful courses before**

None. C02 is complementary, not a prerequisite.

**Natural next steps**

- C04 Pediatric Emergency Preparedness for broader capability;
- C05 BLSD Formalife for deeper resuscitation practice;
- practical refresh/rehearsal over time;
- direct caregiver participation in C03/C04 where another caregiver lacks the skill.

**Entry-point strength**

Very high. This remains Formalife's strongest current acquisition wedge.

**International precedent**

Choking is consistently central in Tiny Hearts, AHA, Red Cross, Daisy First Aid, Mini First Aid and Safe Beginnings parent training.

**Funnel role**

Primary acquisition / front-end / bridge into core.

**Build vs partner**

Formalife-owned core competence.

### C04 — Pediatric Emergency Preparedness

**Primary parent question**

"If something serious happened to my child, would I recognise it quickly enough and know what to do before professional help takes over?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

This is the central broad job that defines Formalife's consumer promise. International category leaders prove that parents buy comprehensive first-aid/emergency education rather than only micro-topics.

**Scope**

Current flagship V1 remains governed by `FLAGSHIP_SCOPE_V1.md`, including subject to scientific validation:

- emergency recognition, red flags and escalation;
- high-depth choking and practical disobstruction;
- meaningful practical introduction to pediatric resuscitation and AED;
- respiratory distress / severe allergic emergencies;
- seizures / febrile convulsions;
- selected high-consequence injuries/exposures such as serious bleeding, burns, head injury, poisoning and drowning context.

The Full Course remains curated rather than encyclopedic.

**Useful courses before**

None mandatory. Direct high-intent purchase must remain possible.

**Natural next steps**

- C05 BLSD Formalife for deeper CPR/AED/resuscitation competence;
- practical refresh/rehearsal;
- C07 Sickness & Red Flags when the customer wants deeper health-navigation knowledge;
- specific prevention/specialist nodes only when a real life-stage or exposure makes them relevant.

**Entry-point strength**

High for people whose pain is broad preparedness rather than one narrow trigger.

**International precedent**

Tiny Hearts' four-hour public/private courses, Daisy First Aid and other family first-aid operators validate a broad parent emergency course as a central paid product.

**Funnel role**

Core flagship.

**Build vs partner**

Formalife-owned core competence with explicit scientific governance.

### C05 — BLSD Formalife — non-certified proprietary course

**Primary parent question**

"I do not just want to have heard CPR once; I want enough practice to feel genuinely capable of resuscitating a child and using a DAE."

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

Deep resuscitation competence is a distinct learning job from broad first aid. It requires more deliberate practice, repetition, scenarios and feedback than the flagship can allocate without becoming distorted.

**Scope**

- high-repetition pediatric CPR practice;
- AED use;
- scenarios and decision sequences;
- individual correction and deliberate practice;
- digital preparation/reference;
- family/caregiver practice-sharing where useful;
- planned refresh.

Non-certification remains part of the rationale: the product is optimized for learning, not credential compliance.

**Useful courses before**

C04 recommended but not mandatory for a sufficiently motivated direct entrant. C03 can also be a natural predecessor.

**Natural next steps**

- scheduled refresh/reassessment;
- additional household caregivers learning the same substantive skill;
- C06 only when a formal credential later becomes necessary.

**Entry-point strength**

Medium as a broad acquisition product; high as depth/ascension for prepared customers.

**International precedent**

AHA CPR Anytime demonstrates non-credential home CPR/choking learning, practice, refresh and sharing.

**Funnel role**

Specialist depth / high-value core ascension.

**Build vs partner**

Formalife-owned proprietary product, subject to scientific/technical governance.

### C06 — BLSD Certified

**Primary parent/customer question**

"I need a valid BLSD certification for work, volunteering, regulation or another formal requirement."

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

The customer's job is credential acquisition. It is economically real but conceptually distinct from Formalife's proprietary consumer education.

**Scope**

Exactly the applicable certified curriculum, prerequisites and assessment requirements of the competent accreditation/certification system.

**Useful courses before**

Only any formal prerequisites required by the certification system.

**Natural next steps**

- renewal as required;
- C05 if the customer separately wants deeper non-credential practice;
- C04 only when broader parent/caregiver preparedness is a distinct need.

**Entry-point strength**

High for credential-intent search; low relevance to parents who do not need certification.

**International precedent**

The distinction between consumer training and professional/certified training is common among strong safety operators and remains strategically important for Formalife.

**Funnel role**

Credential lane.

**Build vs partner**

Governed by applicable accreditation/provider arrangements.

### C07 — Sickness & Red Flags

**Primary parent question**

"My child is ill: what can I safely observe at home, when should I call the pediatrician and what warning signs mean I should escalate urgently?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

This is a repeated parent problem with a different cognitive job from practical first aid. The value lies in recognising patterns, red flags and care pathways without pretending to diagnose remotely.

**Scope**

Subject to pediatric governance:

- fever and when to worry;
- respiratory symptoms/distress recognition;
- dehydration / gastro warning signs;
- serious rash / sepsis-type red flags where appropriate;
- febrile convulsions in context;
- general critically-unwell-child recognition;
- escalation framework: home observation vs pediatric contact vs urgent/emergency care;
- explicit boundaries against diagnosis/prescribing.

**Useful courses before**

None.

**Natural next steps**

- C04 when the customer wants broader emergency-action skills;
- C11 if food allergy/anaphylaxis becomes a specific diagnosed issue;
- lifecycle reactivation into other safety nodes as the child grows.

**Entry-point strength**

High and recurrent, especially during early childhood and daycare/nursery years.

**International precedent**

Tiny Hearts sells Surviving Sickness separately and in a Health & Safety bundle with first aid.

**Funnel role**

Acquisition / horizontal core / digital-reference product.

**Build vs partner**

Formalife-owned only with visible pediatric authority and controlled scope.

---

### C08 — Home Safety & Babyproofing

**Primary parent question**

"My baby is starting to crawl/walk: what hazards am I missing and what should I change in the house before mobility gets ahead of me?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

Mobility is a strong, predictable trigger. The problem is broad enough to support systematic instruction but coherent enough to remain one safety domain rather than many micro-courses.

**Scope**

- falls, furniture tip-over, stairs, windows and balcony risks;
- burns/scalds/fire/electricity;
- poisons, medicines, chemicals;
- button batteries, magnets and small objects;
- bath/water hazards in the home;
- blind cords and strangulation hazards;
- room-by-room risk assessment;
- product/gear hazards and recall awareness;
- visiting/holiday-home adaptation as a module rather than a separate course.

**Useful courses before**

None.

**Natural next steps**

- C09 when water/pool/beach exposure becomes material;
- C10 when child-passenger questions arise;
- C04 if the family wants broader response skills;
- C12 when pets create an additional home-risk interaction.

**Entry-point strength**

High around crawling, pulling to stand, walking or moving into a new home.

**International precedent**

Safe Beginnings sells a substantial standalone home-safety course; Mini First Aid sells a separate Baby Proofing class.

**Funnel role**

Lifecycle acquisition / prevention vertical / horizontal cross-sell.

**Build vs partner**

Can become Formalife-owned after appropriate injury-prevention expertise is secured; specialist collaboration may be preferable initially.

### C09 — Water Safety & Drowning Prevention

**Primary parent question**

"My child is now around baths, pools, villas, beaches or open water: what layers of prevention actually matter and what should we do if something goes wrong?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

The problem has a clear high-consequence exposure trigger and a dedicated international education category. It combines environmental prevention, supervision systems and emergency preparedness in a way that generic travel content cannot replace.

**Scope**

- supervision systems and explicit caregiver handoff;
- barriers/environmental controls;
- bath, pool, beach and open-water risk;
- age-appropriate flotation/swim-safety concepts within specialist guidance;
- recognising a water emergency;
- immediate response and bridge into resuscitation competence;
- travel/villa water-risk planning.

**Useful courses before**

None mandatory. C04/C05 improve emergency depth but should not gate prevention education.

**Natural next steps**

- C05 BLSD Formalife for deeper resuscitation competence;
- refresh before high-exposure seasons where useful.

**Entry-point strength**

High around summer, holidays, new pool access and swim-stage transitions.

**International precedent**

American Red Cross has a dedicated Water Safety for Parents & Caregivers course and broader family water-safety system.

**Funnel role**

Seasonal/lifecycle acquisition / prevention specialist.

**Build vs partner**

Prefer build with qualified aquatic-safety/resuscitation expertise or specialist partnership; do not extrapolate from generic first-aid competence alone.

---

### C10 — Car Seat Safety / Child Passenger Safety

**Primary parent question**

"Is the seat I chose appropriate, installed and fitted correctly, and when should I change configuration or move to the next seat?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

The questions are technical, recurring at purchase/transition moments and supported by a dedicated consultation/certification ecosystem. This is a much clearer commercial job than generic 'road safety'.

**Scope**

- selecting the appropriate restraint within applicable local standards;
- correct installation and fit;
- rear-facing/forward-facing and later transition principles under local rules;
- common misuse/errors;
- reading limits/manuals correctly;
- safe everyday loading/use;
- when specialist 1:1 checking is needed.

**Useful courses before**

None.

**Natural next steps**

No forced back-end. Lifecycle reactivation occurs at seat/size transitions. C08 or C04 are relevant only when the family independently has those broader needs.

**Entry-point strength**

Very high around pregnancy/newborn discharge, first-seat purchase and transitions.

**International precedent**

Safe Beginnings sells a dedicated non-certification consumer car-seat course, offers consultations and separates it from professional CPST certification.

**Funnel role**

Acquisition / lifecycle / specialist prevention.

**Build vs partner**

**Specialist-led.** Formalife should not own this product until Italian/EU standards, competence and any relevant professional qualification pathway are explicitly covered.

---

### C11 — Food Allergy & Anaphylaxis Preparedness

**Primary parent question**

"My child has a food allergy: how do we prevent exposures, make other caregivers safe and know exactly when and how to use emergency medication?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

After diagnosis, the family's job expands well beyond the brief anaphylaxis section of a generic first-aid course. It includes daily risk management, cross-contamination, action plans, epinephrine competence and transferring the system to daycare, school, relatives and babysitters.

**Scope**

With allergy-specialist governance:

- understanding the child's emergency action plan rather than creating individualized medical advice;
- recognition of allergic reaction/anaphylaxis;
- correct use of prescribed auto-injectors;
- cross-contamination and exposure prevention principles;
- eating outside the home / school/daycare/caregiver handover;
- emergency communication and post-epinephrine escalation;
- refresh and caregiver practice.

**Useful courses before**

None. A diagnosed family needs direct access.

**Natural next steps**

- caregiver participation/household sharing;
- C04 for broader emergency competence if desired;
- C02 only when the family is concurrently navigating starting solids and specialist guidance makes it relevant.

**Entry-point strength**

High inside the diagnosed/at-risk subgroup; not a mass-market acquisition offer for all parents.

**International precedent**

Food Allergy Canada/AllergyAware and AAFA operate dedicated parent/caregiver allergy-anaphylaxis education.

**Funnel role**

Specialist depth / targeted acquisition / caregiver expansion.

**Build vs partner**

Preferred **specialist partnership/co-creation** with pediatric allergy expertise; Formalife should not generalize from emergency-first-aid knowledge.

---

### C12 — Child + Pet Safety

**Primary parent question**

"We have a dog and a baby is arriving / starting to crawl: how do we prevent unsafe interactions and know when the animal is uncomfortable before a bite happens?"

**Why this merits an autonomous course**

The audience is narrower, but the trigger is precise, stakes are high and a durable specialist paid market already exists around the baby-arrival and mobility transitions.

**Scope**

For an appropriately qualified specialist:

- preparing the household dog for baby arrival;
- environmental management and separation;
- supervision systems;
- recognising stress/discomfort signals;
- changes when the baby begins crawling/walking;
- safe interaction rules for adults/children;
- when to seek individualized veterinary/behavioural support.

The first version should focus on dogs because that is where the strongest specialist precedent exists; do not pretend one generic pet curriculum covers all species.

**Useful courses before**

None.

**Natural next steps**

- C08 Home Safety as mobility changes the broader home environment;
- individualized specialist consultation when there are concerning animal behaviours;
- no forced route into the emergency spine.

**Entry-point strength**

High within dog-owning expectant/new-parent households, low outside that subgroup.

**International precedent**

Family Paws sells `Dogs & Storks` and `Dogs & Toddlers` as live and self-paced programs and operates a specialist educator network.

**Funnel role**

Specialist-partnership acquisition / lifecycle niche.

**Build vs partner**

Preferred **specialist partnership**, not internal Formalife authorship without genuine behavioural/veterinary competence.

## 4. Cross-cutting layers that are deliberately not course nodes

### 4.1 Caregiver expansion

One trained parent does not remove the safety gap created by grandparents, partners, babysitters and other caregivers.

Formalife should therefore engineer caregiver expansion into substantive products through mechanisms such as:

- couple/family/private formats;
- household digital access where economics allow;
- caregiver passes/credits;
- shareable reference material;
- direct invitations for another caregiver to take C03/C04/C05 or another relevant node;
- emergency/allergy/safety handover templates.

This replaces the old `Family & Caregiver Safety` meta-course unless future demand proves a genuinely independent curriculum.

### 4.2 Skills refresh / rehearsal

Refresh remains strategically important for practical skills but is treated as lifecycle infrastructure rather than a topic.

Possible forms include:

- scheduled practice labs;
- short reassessment sessions;
- digital refresh prompts/reference;
- seasonal refresh tied to water exposure;
- customer-owned practice products;
- repeat live practice after elapsed time.

This replaces the old C13 topical node while preserving its economic and educational function.

### 4.3 Travel / outdoor / seasonal overlays

Travel is a context in which stronger safety problems become salient, not necessarily a coherent course by itself.

Routing examples:

- villa/pool/beach -> C09 Water Safety;
- car journey/seat issue -> C10 Car Seat Safety;
- holiday home/hotel hazards -> C08 Home Safety principles;
- heat/sun/cold/seasonal hazards -> maintained reference content / seasonal campaign;
- trip emergency preparation -> C04 resources / first-aid-kit utility.

### 4.4 Free utility / reference opportunities

Several real parent jobs are more naturally solved by frequently used tools than by another course:

- product recall / unsafe-product monitoring;
- home-safety audit;
- first-aid-kit builder / checklist;
- family emergency-contact and handover template;
- medication-storage / poison-prevention reference;
- car-seat selection/fit decision aid once specialist competence exists;
- stage-based safety checklists;
- carefully governed red-flag reference tools.

These fit the Solid Starts-style principle: **useful recurring utility -> repeated trust -> relevant paid education**, not generic lead-magnet production.

## 5. Major funnels through the graph

### Funnel A — Choking acquisition -> emergency mastery

**specific choking fear / starting-solids trigger -> free useful choking resource or Guide/Light -> C03 Choking -> C04 Pediatric Emergency Preparedness -> C05 BLSD Formalife if deeper mastery is desired -> refresh over time**

This remains the strongest immediate Formalife-owned funnel because current assets and customer evidence already exist.

### Funnel B — Newborn lifecycle

**pregnancy/newborn safety concern -> C01 Newborn Safety & Safe Sleep -> later reactivation by actual life-stage trigger**

Possible branches:

- starting solids -> C02 and/or C03;
- recurrent illness uncertainty -> C07;
- crawling/walking -> C08;
- car-seat purchase/transition -> C10.

This is a branching lifecycle, not a mandatory sequence.

### Funnel C — Illness / health-navigation entry

**fever/illness/red-flag question -> useful governed reference -> C07 Sickness & Red Flags -> C04 only if the parent wants broader emergency-response competence**

Allergy diagnosis can instead route directly into C11.

### Funnel D — Environmental prevention entry

**new exposure -> relevant specialist node**

Examples:

- mobile baby / new home -> C08 Home Safety;
- pool/beach/villa -> C09 Water Safety;
- car-seat purchase/transition -> C10 Car Seat Safety;
- dog + baby/crawling transition -> C12 Child + Pet Safety.

Each node may create a customer relationship without being forced through a generic flagship upsell.

### Funnel E — Specialist-partner entry

**specialist audience / Formalife audience -> co-created specialist product -> common customer record -> only genuinely relevant Formalife next step**

Current likely families:

- Svezzy / feeding -> C02;
- allergy specialist -> C11;
- pet-behaviour specialist -> C12;
- child-passenger specialist -> C10.

The purpose is to broaden customer value inside the safety territory without pretending Formalife internally owns every discipline.

### Funnel F — Credential intent

**formal credential need -> C06 BLSD Certified -> renewal as required**

Only when a distinct consumer need exists should that customer be shown C04 or C05. Certification remains a separate door, not the consumer brand's center.

## 6. Second-purchase logic

The graph should optimize the second relevant purchase, but no universal next product exists.

Three types of second purchase are valid:

1. **depth** — C03/C04 -> C05;
2. **life-stage change** — C01 -> C02/C03/C08/C10 when the family context changes;
3. **new specialist problem** — diagnosis/exposure produces C07, C09, C11 or C12.

Caregiver expansion and refresh can also create additional revenue without pretending the family has a new topical problem.

## 7. What the graph does not authorize

This map does **not** authorize building 12 courses now.

`FORMALIFE_BUILD_SEQUENCE.md` remains controlling:

1. prove the current Guide/Light/Full commercial engine;
2. reproduce trusted distribution;
3. build one strong scalable digital flagship;
4. add owned demand/lifecycle infrastructure;
5. expand adjacently only after the core has earned the right to carry more complexity.

The mature graph is useful now because it prevents local product decisions from creating dead ends and allows the first customer relationship/data model to anticipate later needs.

## 8. Current open questions before founder approval

### OPEN QUESTION — Safe Sleep packaging

The research supports safe sleep strongly. The proposed answer is one `Newborn Safety & Safe Sleep` node rather than a generic New Baby course plus a second Safe Sleep course. This remains testable.

### OPEN QUESTION — Italian paid-demand ranking

International precedent does not establish which later vertical has the highest Italian willingness-to-pay. No build priority among C07-C12 should be inferred from this map without local validation.

### OPEN QUESTION — specialist partner availability

C02, C10, C11 and C12 are substantially better if built with credible specialists. Partner quality, economics, IP/data terms and willingness remain unknown.

### OPEN QUESTION — water-safety ownership

C09 is a strong topic node, but the appropriate Italian specialist/scientific delivery model still needs to be determined.

### OPEN QUESTION — paid value where public information is free

Safe sleep, water safety, allergy and poison prevention all have strong free authoritative resources. A Formalife paid product must add materially better synthesis, localization, implementation, tools, feedback, practice or maintained reference value.
