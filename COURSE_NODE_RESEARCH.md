# Formalife Course Node Research

Status: CURRENT RESEARCH — revised course-node candidate; founder decision pending
Date: 2026-09-21

Purpose: test the provisional 13-node course graph from first principles. The method deliberately starts from recurring parent questions and only afterward clusters those questions into possible course nodes. It then cross-checks each cluster against established international parent-education products so Formalife does not invent a course merely because a topic sounds useful.

This document supersedes `COURSE_FUNNEL_GRAPH.md` as the **leading research candidate**, but it does not convert the revised graph into a founder decision or build commitment.

## 1. Method

Two evidence streams were kept separate before synthesis.

### A. Parent-question evidence

Signals were gathered from spontaneous parent discussions and question-led public resources, including recent Italian and English-language communities. These signals are qualitative and do not measure market size. Their job is to answer a narrower question:

**Does this problem appear naturally in the parent's own language, without first inventing a product around it?**

### B. International product evidence

The question clusters were then checked against established parent/caregiver education from operators including Tiny Hearts, Safe Beginnings, Tinyhood, American Red Cross, American Heart Association, Red Nose Australia, Family Paws, hospital grandparent/caregiver classes, Nurtured Nest and child-safeguarding organizations.

A strong standalone node should normally satisfy most of these conditions:

1. a specific recurring parent question exists;
2. the problem is substantial enough for at least ~30 minutes of useful education;
3. a credible international operator already treats it as a standalone course/program or clearly differentiated product;
4. it is not mostly a duplicate of another Formalife node;
5. it creates a natural entry point, depth step, cross-sell or retention step;
6. it fits Formalife's current safety/prevention/emergency meaning, or can be clearly identified as a specialist-partner adjacency instead of silently broadening the brand.

## 2. What the parent-question evidence actually says

### Q1 — Safe sleep / newborn sleep environment

Recurring parent question:

**"Come faccio a sapere che il mio neonato sta dormendo davvero in sicurezza?"**

Italian parent discussions show explicit fear around SIDS, sleep position, cot setup, blankets, reflux/regurgitation and whether improvised changes to the sleep environment are safe. Similar questions recur internationally.

International validation is unusually strong:

- Red Nose Australia sells a dedicated 1-hour Safe Sleep webinar and maintains a dedicated safe-sleep education system;
- Tinyhood has dedicated safe-sleep/newborn-sleep education and separate safe-nursery content;
- Nurtured Nest places safe sleep as a major component of its Infant CPR + Safety product.

**SYNTHESIS:** safe sleep is a genuine standalone question. The old generic `New Baby Safety` node is too broad as a knowledge category. `New Baby Safety` is better treated as a lifecycle bundle/funnel; the underlying course node should be **Safe Sleep & Newborn Environment**.

### Q2 — Starting solids / feeding safely

Recurring parent question:

**"Come inizio i solidi senza sbagliare e senza mettere mio figlio a rischio?"**

Spontaneous discussions repeatedly show confusion about pieces, textures, gagging/choking, baby-led weaning, safe cuts and fear after prior choking-like events.

International validation:

- Tiny Hearts sells a dedicated Introducing Solids course with readiness, feeding methods, safe eating, choking prevention and allergen introduction;
- Tiny Hearts explicitly excludes choking treatment from that course and routes it to First Aid;
- Solid Starts built an entire consumer business around the recurring starting-solids problem.

**SYNTHESIS:** this is a real standalone node and should remain distinct from emergency choking response. For Formalife the preferred ownership model remains specialist collaboration rather than recreating Svezzy's competence from zero.

### Q3 — Choking response

Recurring parent question:

**"Se mio figlio soffoca, saprei riconoscerlo e intervenire?"**

This is already supported by Formalife historical evidence and current Italian category demand. Italian discussions also show parents who are comfortable with feeding only until they imagine or experience a choking event.

International validation:

- dedicated choking/CPR modules or workshops appear across Tiny Hearts, Safe Beginnings, AHA and other first-aid providers;
- Tiny Hearts deliberately separates choking treatment from Introducing Solids.

**SYNTHESIS:** keep as a focused node. It answers a different job from Starting Solids and remains a high-salience Formalife acquisition wedge.

### Q4 — Broad pediatric emergency preparedness

Recurring parent question:

**"Se succedesse qualcosa di serio a mio figlio, saprei capire che è un'emergenza e cosa fare per prima cosa?"**

This question is broader than choking or illness and maps to parents wanting a general emergency capability.

International validation:

- Tiny Hearts' 4-hour Baby & Child First Aid course covers red flags, critically unwell child, CPR, defibrillation, choking, anaphylaxis, respiratory distress, fever/convulsions, injuries, burns and poisoning;
- Tinyhood sells an Ultimate Safety Class covering illnesses, injuries, emergencies and CPR;
- St John and other parent-first first-aid operators use broad baby/toddler first-aid formats.

**SYNTHESIS:** keep as Formalife's live flagship node. Its breadth must remain curated and hierarchical rather than encyclopedic.

### Q5 — Deep CPR / resuscitation competence without credential need

Recurring parent question:

**"Non voglio solo aver visto la rianimazione: saprei davvero farla e usare un DAE?"**

International validation:

- AHA Family & Friends CPR explicitly serves people who want CPR skills but do not need a course-completion credential and names parents, grandparents and caregivers as an ideal audience;
- AHA CPR Anytime productizes at-home CPR/choking learning and practice;
- Safe Beginnings' most popular CPR/choking workshop is explicitly non-certification for parents/caregivers.

**SYNTHESIS:** the proprietary non-certified BLSD/CPR node is strongly validated and should remain distinct from both broad First Aid and certified BLSD.

### Q6 — Sick child / when to seek help

Recurring parent question:

**"Quando mio figlio sta male, come distinguo ciò che posso osservare da ciò per cui devo chiamare il pediatra o cercare assistenza urgente?"**

Italian search/content demand and parent discussions repeatedly center on fever, breathing, dehydration and when to call/escalate.

International validation:

- Tiny Hearts sells Surviving Sickness separately from First Aid and bundles the two;
- Tiny Hearts frames the distinction as everyday illnesses plus knowing the difference between home management and seeking medical help;
- Tinyhood includes illness education but also demonstrates demand for dedicated illness/safety material.

**SYNTHESIS:** keep as a standalone digital/reference-heavy node. It answers a different question from emergency procedures and should be pediatrician-led with explicit non-diagnostic boundaries.

### Q7 — Babyproofing / home hazards

Recurring parent question:

**"Ora che gattona/cammina, cosa devo davvero mettere in sicurezza in casa e quali pericoli non sto vedendo?"**

Recent Italian discussions explicitly ask what protections are actually necessary when a child starts crawling, including stairs, cupboards, sockets, furniture and the general problem of letting the child explore safely.

International validation:

- Safe Beginnings sells a 5.5-hour, 55+ lesson Home Safety course with lifetime access covering fire, water, poisoning, button batteries, choking hazards, toddler bedrooms, travel/visiting and seasonal safety;
- Tinyhood sells dedicated Babyproofing and Safe Nursery education.

**SYNTHESIS:** keep as a major standalone node. Absorb poisoning prevention, button batteries, magnets, falls, burns/fire/electricity, furniture tip-over and most travel/holiday home-environment issues here rather than fragmenting them into separate courses.

### Q8 — Child passenger / car-seat safety

Recurring parent question:

**"Il seggiolino che sto usando è quello giusto, montato e regolato correttamente per mio figlio?"**

Italian parent discussions show uncertainty around choosing seats, infant positioning, transitions from the infant carrier and whether setups are genuinely safe.

International validation:

- Safe Beginnings sells a dedicated six-module Car Seat Safety course with lifetime access and a separate free car-seat selection tool;
- Tinyhood offers dedicated car-seat installation/buckling education.

**SYNTHESIS:** keep as a standalone node, but rename the old `Car Seat & Road Safety` to **Car Seat / Child Passenger Safety**. General pedestrian/bicycle/road-safety education is a separate problem and does not currently have equally strong parent-course validation inside the 0-6 Formalife model.

### Q9 — Water / drowning prevention

Recurring parent question:

**"Come tengo mio figlio al sicuro vicino all'acqua, e cosa devo sapere se qualcosa va storto?"**

Parent discussions show acute anxiety around pools, holidays, beaches and toddlers near water.

International validation:

- American Red Cross has a standalone Water Safety for Parents and Caregivers online course focused on drowning risk, prevention, water competency and common environments;
- Safe Beginnings covers water hazards deeply inside Home Safety, showing that the topic can also be bundled.

**SYNTHESIS:** keep as a standalone node because it has a strong seasonal/life-event entry trigger and an established standalone international course. Its deepest next link is CPR/BLSD.

### Q10 — Other caregivers / grandparents / babysitters

Recurring parent question:

**"Come faccio a essere sicuro che nonni, babysitter e altri caregiver siano aggiornati sulle regole di sicurezza e sappiano cosa fare?"**

Spontaneous discussions include parents explicitly looking for training they can share with grandparents/aunts rather than personally lecturing them, and conflict where older caregivers rely on outdated experience.

International validation:

- hospitals run dedicated Grandparents/Caregivers classes covering updated baby care, safe sleep, car seats, CPR/first aid and changed recommendations;
- Nurtured Nest sells Grandparenting as a focused course;
- Safe Sitter and similar programs use the same role-based pattern.

**SYNTHESIS:** keep, but understand its architecture correctly. This is a **role-based composite course**, not a unique knowledge vertical. Formalife should reuse approved modules from Safe Sleep, Choking/CPR, emergency readiness and household handover instead of maintaining a second contradictory curriculum.

### Q11 — Child + dog/pet safety

Recurring parent question:

**"Come preparo il cane all'arrivo del neonato e come evito problemi quando il bambino inizia a gattonare/camminare?"**

This appears naturally in both Italian and international parent discussions, especially around introducing a newborn to an established dog and the behavioral change when the child becomes mobile.

International validation is strong and highly specific:

- Family Paws has operated dedicated `Dogs & Storks` and `Dogs & Toddlers` programs, live and self-paced, plus an 8+ hour baby bundle;
- Nurtured Nest independently sells a Dogs + Kids safety course;
- health systems have hosted Family Paws programs.

Italian market evidence also exists through dog/child safety webinars and veterinary/pediatric education, so this is not an uncontested white space.

**SYNTHESIS:** this is a real node that the original 13 missed. It fits injury-prevention/life-stage safety but requires specialist animal-behavior authority. Preferred Formalife model: **partner-led or co-created course**, not internal invention.

### Q12 — Practical skill decay / refresh

Recurring parent question:

**"Ho fatto il corso mesi fa: mi ricordo ancora davvero cosa fare?"**

The need follows naturally from practical-skill decay and from parent behavior of revisiting videos/materials after training.

International products commonly provide repeat access, refresh and home-practice mechanisms even when they do not sell a separately named refresh course.

**SYNTHESIS:** retain **Skills Refresh & Rehearsal** as a continuity node. It is not a new thematic category; it is a monetizable/retention layer connected principally to choking, emergency first aid and BLSD.

### Q13 — Credential need

Recurring customer job:

**"Mi serve una certificazione BLSD valida."**

This is a real buying job but not a parent-safety content category.

**SYNTHESIS:** retain certified BLSD as a **side credential lane**, not as a thematic consumer node and not as a definition of the Formalife brand.

## 3. Topics that are real but should NOT become standalone Formalife nodes now

### Travel / outdoor / seasonal safety

Parents do have real questions around holidays, hotels, heat, beaches, playgrounds and unfamiliar environments. However the international course architecture does not strongly validate this as a separate core parent course. Strong operators tend to absorb it into Home Safety, Water Safety, Car Seat Safety or seasonal resources.

**Decision recommendation:** remove `Travel / Outdoor / Seasonal Safety` as a standalone course node. Use it as lifecycle campaign/bundle packaging and as modules/resources routed to the relevant specialist node.

### Poisoning / medication / button batteries / magnets

The questions are real and high consequence, but strong operators generally treat them as components of Home Safety and/or First Aid rather than separate parent courses.

**Decision recommendation:** merge prevention into Home Safety and emergency response into Pediatric First Aid/Emergencies.

### Allergies / anaphylaxis

There is a real condition-specific market for parents of diagnosed food-allergic children, and specialist paid courses exist. For the general parent market, however, leading operators integrate:

- allergen introduction into Starting Solids;
- acute allergic/anaphylaxis response into First Aid/Emergencies.

**Decision recommendation:** do not create a generic standalone Formalife allergy course now. If a diagnosed-allergy vertical is later justified, build it with a specialist rather than opening an unlimited disease-by-disease catalog.

### Road / pedestrian / bicycle / playground safety

These are real child-safety topics, especially as age increases, but current evidence is stronger for child/school education than for a distinct parent-course product in the initial 0-6 model.

**Decision recommendation:** use resources/modules initially; revisit when Formalife expands into older-child safety.

### First-aid kit / family emergency plan

A real parent need, but not enough evidence for a separate course node relative to the overlap it creates.

**Decision recommendation:** make it a high-value module, tool or lead magnet inside Newborn/Safe Sleep, First Aid/Emergencies and Caregiver Update.

## 4. Validated categories that sit at the current brand boundary

These are not invented ideas. They have real parent questions and established training markets. The issue is **brand scope**, not demand.

### Body Safety / abuse prevention / consent

Real parent question:

**"Come insegno a mio figlio parti intime, confini, consenso e cosa fare se qualcosa non va, senza spaventarlo?"**

Strong evidence exists for 60-90+ minute parent/caregiver workshops and structured programs. Examples include Children's Trust Massachusetts, Center for Child Protection, Body Safety Australia and published parent-focused prevention programs/reviews.

**Strategic status:** VALIDATED ADJACENCY, not admitted to the current core graph yet. It changes Formalife from pediatric/injury/health safety toward broader safeguarding. If the future category becomes `child safety` rather than `pediatric safety`, this is one of the first serious nodes to reconsider, ideally specialist-led.

### Digital safety for young children

Real parent question:

**"Come imposto dispositivi, contenuti e regole digitali in modo sicuro per un bambino piccolo?"**

Government and nonprofit systems already provide dedicated under-5/young-child online-safety parent education, and paid/structured parent workshops exist.

**Strategic status:** VALIDATED ADJACENCY, but currently outside the strongest Formalife meaning and based on a different specialist ecosystem. Keep in the outer ring for later partnership or a broader/separate child-safety architecture.

## 5. Revised node architecture — leading candidate

The research no longer supports the original 13 nodes unchanged.

### Core thematic nodes

1. **Safe Sleep & Newborn Environment**
   - Parent question: "Come faccio a sapere che il mio neonato dorme e riposa in un ambiente davvero sicuro?"
   - Replaces broad `New Baby Safety` as the underlying content node.

2. **Starting Solids & Feeding Safety** — specialist/partner-led
   - Parent question: "Come inizio i solidi in modo sicuro e gestibile?"

3. **Choking & Disobstruction**
   - Parent question: "Se mio figlio soffoca, saprei riconoscerlo e intervenire?"

4. **Pediatric First Aid & Emergencies** — flagship
   - Parent question: "Se succede qualcosa di serio, saprei riconoscerlo e fare la prima cosa giusta?"

5. **BLSD Formalife / Pediatric CPR & AED Deep Practice**
   - Parent question: "Saprei davvero rianimare mio figlio, o ho soltanto visto le manovre?"

6. **Sickness & Red Flags**
   - Parent question: "Quando mio figlio è malato, quando posso osservare e quando devo cercare aiuto?"

7. **Home Safety & Babyproofing**
   - Parent question: "Ora che si muove, cosa devo davvero mettere in sicurezza in casa?"

8. **Car Seat / Child Passenger Safety**
   - Parent question: "Il seggiolino è scelto, installato e usato correttamente per mio figlio?"

9. **Water Safety & Drowning Prevention**
   - Parent question: "Come tengo mio figlio al sicuro vicino all'acqua?"

10. **Child & Dog Safety** — specialist/partner-led
    - Parent question: "Come mantengo sicura la convivenza tra il mio bambino e il cane, soprattutto quando il bambino cambia fase?"

### Role-based / continuity / credential nodes

11. **Grandparents & Caregivers Safety Update** — composite course
    - Parent question: "Come faccio a mettere nonni/babysitter sulla stessa linea di sicurezza e preparazione?"

12. **Emergency Skills Refresh & Rehearsal** — continuity
    - Parent question: "Ricordo ancora davvero come fare dopo mesi dal corso?"

13. **BLSD Certified** — credential side lane
    - Customer job: "Mi serve una certificazione BLSD valida."

### Lifecycle bundles/funnels, not additional course nodes

`New Baby Safety`, `Starting Solids Safety`, `Summer/Water`, `Travel`, `New Caregiver`, `First Daycare` and similar stage labels should usually be treated as **bundles, campaigns or routing paths** that select the relevant underlying courses rather than as automatically new curricula.

Example:

**New Baby Safety bundle/path** may combine or route among Safe Sleep, Car Seat Safety, Pediatric First Aid/Emergencies and selected Home Safety resources without forcing Formalife to maintain another overlapping master course.

- `Starting Solids & Feeding Safety` -> **KEEP**.

- `Pediatric Emergency Preparedness` -> **KEEP**, renamed/clarified as Pediatric First Aid & Emergencies.
- `BLSD Formalife` -> **KEEP**.
- `BLSD Certified` -> **KEEP AS SIDE LANE**, not thematic core.
- `Sickness & Red Flags` -> **KEEP**.
- `Home Safety & Babyproofing` -> **KEEP AND EXPAND AS ABSORBER** for poisoning prevention, button batteries/magnets, many seasonal/home-away hazards.

- `Car Seat & Road Safety` -> **NARROW** to Car Seat / Child Passenger Safety.
- `Travel / Outdoor / Seasonal Safety` -> **REMOVE AS STANDALONE NODE**; use as lifecycle packaging/resources and route to Home/Water/Car Seat/Emergencies.
- `Family & Caregiver Safety` -> **KEEP BUT REFRAME** as Grandparents & Caregivers Safety Update, a role-based composite course built from shared modules.
- `Skills Refresh` -> **KEEP AS CONTINUITY**, not a thematic vertical.
- **ADD Child & Dog Safety** as a validated specialist/partner-led node.
- **IDENTIFY Body Safety and Digital Safety** as real validated adjacent categories deliberately held outside the current core pending a future brand-boundary decision.

## 8. Layer 1 interpretation

This revised architecture follows current Layer 1 principles:

- start from a real customer problem rather than inventing products and finding reasons afterward;
- keep a focused brand meaning while allowing multiple coherent products;
- do not add a line merely because the same customer could buy it;
- design second purchases deliberately without deliberately crippling the earlier product;
- reduce complexity by reusing modules in role/lifecycle bundles instead of maintaining duplicate curricula;
- use specialist partnerships when the customer problem is real but the necessary authority does not belong in Formalife's internal core.

## 9. Open decision

The next founder decision is not whether each course should be built now. It is whether this revised **13-node graph + two outer-ring validated adjacencies** is the correct long-term commercial curriculum map.

If accepted, the next strategic step is to rebuild the funnel links around these revised nodes and define the lifecycle bundles that route customers through them without creating duplicate products.
